Lesson 5 · May 1, 1937

The Prophecy of Daniel 7

PDFShare

Daily Lesson Study: Place a check mark in the proper space.

Memory verse

Study Helps: "Daniel and the Revelation," pp. 123-141; "Beacon Lights of Prophecy" (Spicer), pp. 53-67.

The Vision of Daniel

  1. 1.When was the vision of Daniel 7 given? Who was Belshazzar? Dan. 7:1.

Notes

The noted Assyriologist, Dr. Daugerty, in his book, "Nabonidus and Belshazzar," says: "There are facts which make it extremely probable that Nabonidus married a daughter of Nebuchadnezzar, and that therefore Belshazzar was a grandson of the great king."

It is not known with exactness when Belshazzar began to reign conjointly with his father Nabondaius, or Nabonidus; but it was most likely about 540 or 541 B. C. This vision was given Daniel in the first year of Belshazzar's reign.

  1. 2.What was Daniel first shown in vision? Verses 2, 3.
  2. 3.What do "winds" represent? "waters"? Jer. 25:31-33; Rev. 17:15.

Notes

From out of great wars among peoples and nations of Asia and Europe, there arose four great kingdoms. The surging, turbulent waters of the Great Sea are a fit symbol of those mighty conflicts during which nations fell and new kingdoms formed.

The Interpretation of the Dream

  1. 4.What are the four beasts said to represent? Dan. 7:17, 23.

Notes

As seen by verse 23, the word "king" here denotes a kingdom; so these four beasts are symbols of four great kingdoms.

  1. 5.Describe the first beast. In what way is it a fitting symbol of Babylon? Verse 4; Hab. 1:6-8.

Notes

Interpreting the dream of Daniel 2, it was explained to Nebuchadnezzar that his was the first kingdom in that prophecy, and was represented by the head of gold. As gold is the most valuable of metals, so the lion is the king of beasts, and represents Babylon. This mighty kingdom became independent of Assyria at the fall of Nineveh, reached its greatness under Nebuchadnezzar, declined, and fell in 538 B. C. The wings denoted speed of conquest; the plucking of the wings signified the loss of that spirit of conquest; and the man's heart given it represented the decline of its warlike character.

  1. 6.How are the Medes and Persians symbolized? Dan. 7:5.

Notes

The three ribs in the mouth of the bear represent the provinces especially oppressed—Babylon, Lydia, Egypt. The Persians were overthrown by Grecia in 331 B. C.

  1. 7.By what is Grecia symbolized? What do the four heads represent? Verse 6.

Notes

The fleet-footed leopard is a fit symbol of the Grecian, or Macedonian, Empire under Alexander the Great. The added symbol of the four wings, indicates the speedy conquest of Persia. Within two years (333-331 B. C.) the kingdom of the Persians was overthrown. But upon the death of Alexander (323), the empire soon was divided among his generals,—Cassander, Lysimachus, Seleucus, Ptolemy,—indicated by the four heads of the leopard.

  1. 8.What nation succeeded Grecia? Describe the symbol? Verse 7.

Notes

"This is allowed, on all hands, to be the Roman Empire. It was dreadful, terrible, and exceeding strong; . . . and became in effect, what the Roman writers delight to call it, the empire of the whole world."—Dr. Adam Clarke.

The fate of the Greeks was decided at the Battle of Pydna, 168 B. C. Henceforth the iron monarchy of Rome continued until its dissolution in 476 A. D.

"Here is the same hard, crushing empire that was represented by the iron of Nebuchadnezzar's image in the second chapter. Rome marched straight onward in the way foretold. . . . Step by step, as these things were taking place, those who knew the Scriptures saw prophecy fulfilling. Hippolytus, who lived in Rome in the late second and early third century, wrote: 'Rejoice, blessed Daniel! thou hast not been in error: all these things have come to pass. . . . Already the iron rules; already it subdues and breaks all in pieces.''Ante-Nicene Fathers,' Vol. V, p. 210."—"Beacon Lights of Prophecy," p. 58.

A Divided Kingdom

  1. 9.What indicated that this fourth kingdom would be divided? Dan. 7:7, 24.
  2. 10.What did the ten horns represent? During what period were these divisions formed? Verse 24.

Notes

Before the fall of Rome in 476 A. D., barbarian tribes from the north and east invaded Europe and North Africa, and eventually established themselves in the territory of Western Rome. These nations were: the Vandals, Visigoths, Suevi, Burgundians, Franks, Ostrogoths, Heruli, Alemanni, Angles, and Saxons, and the Lombards—ten in all, represented by the ten horns.

  1. 11.What took place among the horns? Verse 8.

Notes

Three were rooted up, and in their place arose a little horn. In the next lesson will be studied the rise, character, and fall of the little horn.

God's Kingdom Supplants All Others

  1. 12.To whom will the kingdom ultimately be given? Verse 27.

Notes

These earthly kingdoms are temporary, their glory transitory, but to the saints will be given the kingdom which will endure forever.

Teaching Outline

  1. I.A Vision Given to the Prophet 1. Four beasts appear. Dan. 7:2, 3. 2. Winds represent war, strife; waters represent people. Jer. 25:31-33; Rev. 17:15.
  2. II.Dream and Interpretation 1. The beasts represent kings or kingdoms. Dan. 7:17, 23. 2. The lion represented Babylon. Verse 4. 3. The bear, Medo-Persia. Verse 5. 4. The leopard beast, Grecia. Verse 6. 5. The nondescript beast symbolized Rome. Verse 7.
  3. III.A Divided Kingdom 1. The last kingdom would have ten divisions. Verses 7, 24. 2. The ten horns symbolized the ten kingdoms into which Rome was divided. Verse 24. 3. A little horn rooted up three others. Verse 8.
  4. IV.God's Kingdom Will Supplant All Others 1. The dominion will be given to the saints. Verse 27.
