Lesson 2 · January 13, 1923

The Frailty of Man; the Root of Temptation

PDFShare

Lesson Scripture: James 1:9-18.

Daily Study Outline

  1. 1.Read the lesson scripture.
  2. 2.Class distinction; frailty of man, questions 1-3.
  3. 3.True riches, questions 4, 5.
  4. 4.Enduring temptation, questions 6-10.
  5. 5.Wages of sin; danger of not knowing the Scriptures, questions 11-13.
  6. 6.Source of every good and perfect gift, questions 14, 15.
  7. 7.Review the lesson.

Questions

  1. 1.In what may one of low degree rejoice? James 1:9.
  2. 2.In what are the rich to rejoice? Why should this be a cause for rejoicing? Verse 10. Note 1.
  3. 3.How is the frailty of the rich man illustrated? Verse 11.
  4. 4.For what good purpose may wealth be used? Luke 16:9-12.
  5. 5.What is characteristic of those who love riches? Eccl. 5:10.
  6. 6.Upon whom is a blessing pronounced? What is promised to those who endure temptation? James 1:12. Note 2.
  7. 7.What are the tempted not to say? Why should they not say this? Verse 13.
  8. 8.Where is the root of all temptation? Verse 14. Note 3.
  9. 9.What is the offspring of lust? Verse 15, first part.
  10. 10.When finished, what does sin bring forth? Verse 15, last part.
  11. 11.What is the wage that sin pays? Rom. 6:23; Rev. 20:14. Note 4.
  12. 12.What caution is given in reference to this matter? James 1:16.
  13. 13.What did the Saviour say as to why the Pharisees had erred? Matt. 22:29. Note 5.
  14. 14.From whom does every good and perfect gift come? How is the fact of God's unchangeableness expressed? James 1:17. Note 6.
  15. 15.What has God done for us? By what are we begotten? For what purpose? Verse 18. Compare 1 Peter 1:23. Note 7.

Notes

1. Among men, there are class distinctions; but all are one in Christ Jesus. Christianity brings all up on the same level. The true value of a man consists in moral worth. Character is what the Lord considers of value. Christ did not die for the talented and wealthy only. He died for all. All have equal opportunity to be saved. The poor of this world, the ignorant, the downcast, the downtrodden, the one of lowly rank or humble circumstances, though of "low degree" in man's sight, may be of infinite value in God's sight. The rich of this world, without noble aspirations, covetous and selfish, unless they repent, may be only as a withered branch. Well indeed may such a one who comes to himself, and seeks the Lord in lowliness of spirit, rejoice in that his heart has been broken and humbled.

2. Before we receive the crown, we must be tested. It is in trial that the chaff is separated from the wheat. There must be battle fields in every life. Fire and hammer and file are necessary to give form to metal. So character is shaped and brightened in trial. Those who faithfully endure the grinding, testing process will receive the crown.

3. The word "lust" here means desire. The source of all temptation is in man himself. There may be inducement to sin; but there must be some inclination, some desire for it, to give it power. If there were no desire, or appetite, for food, there would be no disposition to eat even if food were placed before us. The unrestrained man will be drawn along by his own natural propensities to sin. Power over sin in his own fleshly heart is the need of every human being.

4. The wages, or reward, of sin is not eternal life in misery, and unspeakable torture in the lake of fire; but it is death, the second death, in the "fire prepared for the devil and his angels." The righteous receive the gift of God, eternal life; the wicked, the opposite, eternal death.

5. Error is darkness. Truth is light. Ignorance of the Scriptures and of the power of God is responsible for all the error and darkness that cover the earth, whether scientific or religious.

6. God is always the same, at all seasons and in all ages. "I am the Lord, I change not." There is no alteration in His character, His purposes, His plans. What He was in the eternity of the past, He will be countless ages yet to come. Human affairs change, nations pass away, but God is the same. We have to-day the same blessed Christ that healed the sick and comforted the sorrowful when on earth. Heb. 1:10-12; 13:8.

7. The word from which the phrase "begat He us" is translated is the same that in verse 15 is rendered "bringeth forth." Sin "bringeth forth" death, but the word of God "bringeth forth" life. It is the voice of God, His word, that "bringeth forth" the dead out of their dusty beds at the resurrection.
