Lesson 8 · August 21, 1915

Feeding the Multitude

Lesson Scripture: Matt. 14:15-23.

Questions

  1. 1.Though Jesus, after hearing of the death of John, went with His disciples into a retired place to rest, how did He spend the day? Luke 9:11.
  2. 2.Toward evening, what did His disciples say to Him? Matt. 14:15. Note 1.
  3. 3.How did Jesus respond to them? Verse 16.
  4. 4.What answer did the disciples make? Mark 6:37.
  5. 5.What question did Jesus ask them? Verse 38.
  6. 6.How much food did they find? Matt. 14:17.
  7. 7.What direction did Jesus then give? Verse 18.
  8. 8.What did He command the multitude to do? Verse 19, first part.
  9. 9.In what form did they sit down? Mark 6:39, 40. Note 2.
  10. 10.What did Jesus then do? Matt. 14:19, middle part.
  11. 11.What part did the disciples act? Verse 19, last part.
  12. 12.What did the multitude do? Verse 20, first part.
  13. 13.What was done with the fragments? Verse 20, last part. How much was there?
  14. 14.How many of the people had eaten? Verse 21.
  15. 15.What did Jesus immediately do? Verse 22.
  16. 16.Where did He then go? For what purpose? Verse 23, first part.
  17. 17.Where was He when evening came? Verse 23, last part.

Notes

1. Mark says, "that they may go into the country round about, and into the villages, and buy themselves bread: for they have nothing to eat." Luke adds the idea of finding a lodging place. Luke 9:12. Evidently the multitude had brought no food with them, not expecting, perhaps, to stay so long, but held by the words and works of Jesus, they had lingered all day; or if they had brought anything, it was eaten at midday, and they were now hungry and faint.

2. Jesus would have the feeding of the multitude proceed decently and in order. They were to sit down in a clean place — upon "green grass"; and "by companies"—that is, in the customary form observed at a feast, as the Greek word symposia, "drinkings together," indicates. And they sat down "in ranks"—that is, in squares or double rows, with fifty in each single row, or a hundred in each double row, the latter probably facing one another. This would make it easy for the disciples to distribute the food, and for the multitude to be counted, fifty of the double rows making five thousand.
