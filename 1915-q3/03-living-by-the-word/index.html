Lesson 3 · July 17, 1915

Living by the Word

Lesson Scripture: Matt. 13:1-9, 18-23.

Questions

  1. 1.What did Jesus do after the visit of His mother and brethren? Matt. 13:1.
  2. 2.When the multitudes came, where did He and they take position? Verse 2.
  3. 3.In what effective way did Jesus teach them? How did He begin? Verse 3.
  4. 4.What feature of the sowing is first mentioned? What was the outcome? Verse 4.
  5. 5.Where did some of the other seed fall? With what immediate result? Verse 5.
  6. 6.What happened to the plants later? Verse 6.
  7. 7.Where else did some of the seed fall? With what result? Verse 7.
  8. 8.Where did still other seed fall? Verse 8. With what result?
  9. 9.What call to attention shows the importance of this parable? Verse 9. Note 1.
  10. 10.How does Jesus introduce the interpretation of this parable? Verse 18.
  11. 11.What does the seed sown by the wayside represent? Verse 19, first part. Note 2.
  12. 12.What happens to this word of the kingdom? Verse 19, last part.
  13. 13.Who is represented by him that received the seed into stony places? Verse 20. Note 3.
  14. 14.What is the outcome in this case? Verse 21. Note 4.
  15. 15.Who is represented by him that received the seed among thorns? Verse 22, first part.
  16. 16.What is the result in this instance? Verse 22, last part.
  17. 17.Who is represented by him that received the seed into good ground? Verse 23, first part.
  18. 18.What is the result of hearing and understanding the word? Verse 23, last part.
  19. 19.What is suggested by the various degrees of fruit bearing? Compare Matt. 25:14, 15.

Notes

1. "That with which the parable of the sower chiefly deals is the effect produced on the growth of the seed by the soil into which it is cast. By this parable Christ was virtually saying to His hearers, It is not safe for you to stand as critics of My work, or to indulge disappointment because it does not meet your ideas. The question of greatest importance to you is, How do you treat My message? Upon your reception or rejection of it your eternal destiny depends."—"Christ's Object Lessons," pages 43, 44.

2. Bear in mind through this interpretation that the seed represents the "word of the kingdom," and the soil the heart of the hearer. The various conditions of the soil indicate conditions of the heart. The fruitage itself represents elements of character, and deeds of love in the life, not only of the individual who receives the word, but also of every other to whom he may minister that word. In the missionary sense, the hundredfold, the sixtyfold, and the thirtyfold may represent the souls brought to a saving knowledge of the gospel by various workers.

3. The words "forthwith" (verse 5), "anon" (verse 20), and "by and by" (verse 21), are all the same word in the original, and mean immediately. It is sometimes translated elsewhere straightway.

4. The word "offended" here, as usually elsewhere in the Gospels, means caused to stumble, or, more literally still, entrapped, ensnared.
