Lesson 9 · November 27, 1886

The Rich Man and Lazarus

PDFShare
  1. 1.How does our Saviour introduce the parable of the rich man and Lazarus? Luke 16:19.
  2. 2.How does he bring in the second character of the parable? Verse 20.
  3. 3.What did Lazarus desire in his destitute condition? Verse 21.
  4. 4.How did even the animals have compassion on him? Same verse.
  5. 5.What change came to both these men? Verse 22.
  6. 6.What is the meaning of the word "hell," as found in verse 23?—It means the grave.
  7. 7.Repeat a passage of scripture in which the same original word is translated "grave." 1 Cor. 15:55.
  8. 8.Could it be true that a dead man literally cried out in the grave?
  9. 9.What must we conclude, then, with reference to the language of this passage?—That it is a figure of personification, or prosopopœia.
  10. 10.What is Dr. Webster's definition of the last-named figure?—"A figure, by which things are represented as persons, or by which things inanimate are spoken of as animated beings, or by which an absent person is introduced as speaking, or a deceased person is represented as alive and present. It includes personification, but is more extensive in its signification."
  11. 11.What is the rich man represented by this figure as seeing? Verse 23.
  12. 12.How is he represented as addressing Abraham? Verse 24.
  13. 13.What favor did he ask? Same verse.
  14. 14.What did Abraham ask him to remember? Verse 25.
  15. 15.What great change had taken place? Last part of same verse.
  16. 16.How did he show that it was impossible to grant the favor which the rich man desired? Verse 26.
  17. 17.On what errand did the rich man want to have Lazarus sent? Verse 27.
  18. 18.Why did he make this petition? Verse 28.
  19. 19.How is Abraham represented as answering him? Verse 29.
  20. 20.How did the rich man continue to urge his request? Verse 30.
  21. 21.What did Abraham then say to him? Verse 31.
  22. 22.What important truths are illustrated by this parable?
  23. 23.How may we be sure that those who are represented as conversing in this parable are not disembodied spirits, but personified dead men?—Because Lazarus could not return to warn the rich man's brethren without being first raised from the dead. See verses 30, 31.
  24. 24.How does God sometimes speak of things? Rom. 4:17, last part of the verse.
  25. 25.How can it be said, as in Luke 20:38, of Abraham and other dead saints, that they all live unto God?—For the reason that in his purpose they are all to live again.

Notes

What the parable teaches.—"By this parable our Lord illustrates several great truths.—1. The folly and vanity of riches. 2. The worth of true piety, though attended by the deepest poverty. 3. The importance of that great lesson inculcated in the previous parable,—to make friends of the mammon of unrighteousness. Luke 16:9; 1 Tim. 6:17-19. The rich man had neglected this, wasting all on himself, although wretched, suffering Lazarus lay at his gate. The folly of this criminal neglect is shown in that part of the parable in which the rich man, in his distress, as if remembering the past, is represented as asking of Lazarus the water that could be brought on the tip of his finger, and even this is denied. 4. The certainty of future recompense, and the great contrast that it will make with the present state of things. 5. The sufficiency of the Scriptures to instruct and warn mankind."—J. N. Andrews.
