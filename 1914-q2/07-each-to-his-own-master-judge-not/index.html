Lesson 7 · May 16, 1914

Each to His Own Master; Judge Not

Lesson Scripture: Rom. 14:1-12.

Questions

  1. 1.How should we treat those weak in the faith? Verse 1. Note 1.
  2. 2.What may one class in the church believe? Verse 2, first part.
  3. 3.What might the weak do? Verse 2, last part.
  4. 4.What caution is given against judging? Verse 3. Note 2.
  5. 5.What rebuke is given to the one who assumes to judge others? Verse 4, first part.
  6. 6.What admonition does Jesus give concerning this matter? Matt. 7:1.
  7. 7.To whom must each one stand or fall? Rom. 14:4, second part.
  8. 8.What can God do for every true child, despite human judgment? Verse 4, last part. Note 3.
  9. 9.To what other differences among Christians did the apostle refer? Verse 5, first part. Note 4.
  10. 10.What was every man to be? Verse 5, last part. Note 5.
  11. 11.To whom and for whom do all serve who are children of faith? Verse 6. Note 6.
  12. 12.Why can we not say it is the business of no one but ourselves what we do? Verse 7.
  13. 13.To whom do we live or die? To whom do we wholly belong? Verse 8.
  14. 14.What price has been paid for us? Verse 9; 1 Cor. 6:19, 20.
  15. 15.In view of the price paid for us, what pertinent questions are asked both classes? Rom. 14:10, first part.
  16. 16.Before what tribunal shall we all stand? Verse 10, last part.
  17. 17.What scripture is quoted in proof of this? Verse 11. Compare Isa. 45:23.
  18. 18.Of whom and to whom shall each one give account? Rom. 14:12. Note 7.

Notes

1. "Weak in the faith."—Alford suggests that this "weak in the faith" was probably the overscrupulous Jewish convert afraid of meats offered to idols, or of being brought into contact with undiscoverable uncleanness. In foreign lands he abstained from prepared food, and ate only that which he could trace from nature to his own use. Various ceremonial days had strong hold upon a conscience not wholly enlightened and a faith not strong. These converts were to be received as Christians, and their doubtful thoughts not judged or condemned.

2. "Not judge."—The very scrupulous should not "judge"—condemn — his brother who eats, for God has received him. The strong in faith should not "despise"—consider as of no account — the brother who was wavering, doubtful, perhaps overscrupulous.

3. "Shall be holden up."—Despite man's judgment, God is able to make His children stand. One of the great, dominant thoughts of this chapter is, Judge not.

4. "Every day."—By "every day" we must not conclude that the Sabbath is included. This is guarded in the great fundamental law, the Decalogue, over which there can be no question. The expression means the "every day" of the days apart from the Sabbath, such as the Passover, day of Atonement, etc. See Ex. 16:4, where the term "every day" is used, including only "the six working-days." Compare Ex. 16:4, 27, 28; Eze. 46:1. The controversy and the questionings pertained to the national feast-days, as Passover, Pentecost, and others, which had been celebrated as a matter of lifelong habit by the Jews. As feasts of Levitical obligation they were no more of force after the cross, but as national festivals they had a strong hold upon many among the Jewish converts.

5. "In his own mind."—God holds each one responsible. Each person must, or should, decide for himself. And this is true in moral as well as ceremonial observances. The power of choice is for each individual to exercise.

6. "And he that regardeth not the day, to the Lord he doth not regard it" is not found in the Revised Version, not being in the best Greek manuscripts.

7. "Each one."—The great Judgment-day is an individual affair. We can not hide in the crowd, nor shield ourselves by some one else. We must then stand alone, face to face, so to speak, with God. He is the Lawgiver, He is the Judge. Bearing this in mind, we shall not care to judge or despise or condemn our brother. The great thought of the lesson will be ours, "Judge not, that ye be not judged."

Supplementary Questions for Home Study

  1. 1.Is the emphasis in this lesson upon diet, or upon judging?
  2. 2.What great rule should govern our eating and drinking? 1 Cor. 10:31.
  3. 3.What is the great rule regarding Sabbath observance? Ex. 20:8-11; Isa. 58:13.
