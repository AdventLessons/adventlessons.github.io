Lesson 12 · December 19, 1914

Covetousness

PDFShare

Questions

  1. 1.Repeat the tenth commandment. Ex. 20:17. Note 1.
  2. 2.What warning did the Saviour utter against covetousness? Luke 12:15. Note 2.
  3. 3.By what parable did He emphasize this solemn warning? Verses 16-21.
  4. 4.How is covetousness defined by the apostle Paul? Col. 3:5. Note 3.
  5. 5.What will be the awful end of all idolaters? Eph. 5:5; Rev. 22:15.
  6. 6.How does the Lord regard a covetous person? Ps. 10:3.
  7. 7.What was the sin of Achan? Joshua 7:20, 21. Note 4.
  8. 8.What is the root of all evil? How have some erred in this matter? What has been the sad result? 1 Tim. 6:10.
  9. 9.What warning does the apostle give? Verse 11.
  10. 10.What kind of persons were to be chosen anciently as leaders among God's people? Ex. 18:21.
  11. 11.To whom do all things in the earth belong? To whom do we belong? Ps. 24:1; 50:9-12; Haggai 2:8.
  12. 12.What is man's relationship to all these things? Luke 19:13.
  13. 13.How much of all we have is holy to the Lord? Lev. 27:30-33.
  14. 14.What charge does the Lord bring against His people? Mal. 3:8. Note 5.
  15. 15.What is the sad result of robbing God? Verse 9.
  16. 16.What blessing is promised to those who repent? Verses 10, 11.
  17. 17.What promise is made to those who hate covetousness? Prov. 28:16.

Notes

1. "The greatest sin which now exists in the church is covetousness."—"Testimonies for the Church," volume 1, page 194.

2. "I saw that anciently the covetousness of some led them to withhold a suitable proportion; they made their offering stinted. This was recorded in heaven, and they were cursed in their harvest and their flocks just as they withheld. Some were visited with affliction in their families. God would not accept a lame offering."—Id., page 221.

3. "The word of God defines covetousness as idolatry. It is impossible for men and women to keep the law of God and love money. The heart's affections should be placed upon heavenly things. Our treasure should be laid up in heaven; for where our treasure is, there will our heart be also."—Id., volume 3, page 130.

4. "A concealed golden wedge and a Babylonish garment troubled the entire camp of Israel. The frown of God was brought upon the people because of the sin of one man. Thousands were slain upon the field of battle because God would not bless and prosper a people among whom there was even one sinner, one who had transgressed His word."—Id., page 239.

5. "I saw that many who profess to be keeping the commandments of God are appropriating to their own use the means which the Lord has intrusted to them, and which should come into His treasury. They rob God in tithes and in offerings. They dissemble, and withhold from Him to their own hurt. They bring leanness and poverty upon themselves and darkness upon the church, because of their covetousness, their dissembling, and their robbing God in tithes and offerings."—Id., page 269.
