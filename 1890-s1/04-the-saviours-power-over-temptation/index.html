The Saviour's Power over Temptation

This lesson PDF Whole supplement PDF · 7 lessonsShare on WhatsApp
  1. 1.What occasioned the need of a Saviour?
  2. 2.Who introduced sin into the world?
  3. 3.If Christ is to be a Saviour, what power must be manifest? Ans. Power over Satan and his temptations.
  4. 4.Immediately after his baptism where was Jesus led, and for what purpose? .
  5. 5.How long did this time of temptation last? .
  6. 6.Name the first of the recorded temptations? .
  7. 7.State the especial point in this? See note 1.
  8. 8.Give the Saviour's reply. .
  9. 9.What was the second great temptation? .
  10. 10.What was the especial feature of this? See note 2.
  11. 11.How did Jesus meet this? .
  12. 12.State the third temptation? .
  13. 13.To what did Satan appeal in this? See note 3.
  14. 14.How could he make such an offer? .
  15. 15.Who delivered it into his hands? See note 4.
  16. 16.How was this temptation met? .
  17. 17.Show the similarity between these temptations and those under which Adam fell.
  18. 18.In what other way did Christ manifest his power over Satan? .
  19. 19.What will be the final result? .

Notes

1. In the first temptation Satan appealed to the appetite of Christ under the most trying circumstances. Jesus would not work a miracle to relieve himself, but endured it as a man who could not work a miracle, but simply trust God.

2. In the second temptation Satan says: Now in the first temptation you trusted God; you were right in so doing. Now I want you to do the same here, and trust him still further. Thus he seeks to lead Jesus into presumption, just where many men fall, for the land of presumption borders on the pathway of trust.

3. In the third temptation Satan appeals to man's love for glory and power, and seeks to add strength to this by reminding Jesus that he has come here to die to obtain all this, but to obtain which he offers an easier way. Worship me and you shall have it.

4. According to , Adam, by obeying Satan, became his servant, and his possessions, with himself, passed into the hands of his new master.
