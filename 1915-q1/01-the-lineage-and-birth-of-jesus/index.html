The Lineage and Birth of Jesus

Lesson Scripture: Matthew 1

Questions

  1. 1.What title does Matthew give to his book? Matt. 1:1. Note 1.
  2. 2.Why is Jesus called the Son of David? Isa. 11:1-5; Jer. 23:5, 6.
  3. 3.Why is Jesus called the Son of Abraham? Gen. 22:18; Gal. 3:16. Note 2.
  4. 4.Look through the genealogy of Christ in Matt. 1:2-16, and note the names that are familiar to you.
  5. 5.How many generations were there from Abraham to David? From David to the captivity? From the captivity to Christ? Verse 17.
  6. 6.By whom and to whom was the birth of Christ foretold? Luke 1:26-31.
  7. 7.What did the angel instruct Joseph to call this Child of miraculous birth? Matt. 1:21.
  8. 8.Why was this name to be given? What is the meaning of the name Jesus? Matt. 1:21, last part. Note 3.
  9. 9.What is the meaning of the name Christ? John 1:41. Note 4.
  10. 10.What prophecy was fulfilled in the birth and naming of Jesus? Isa. 7:14; Matt. 1:22, 23.
  11. 11.Who was this Jesus, born into the world by the Holy Spirit? 1 Tim. 3:16.
  12. 12.How does John declare His eternal existence and His manifestation in the flesh? John 1:1-3, 14.
  13. 13.Being thus Emmanuel, "God with Us," creative power manifested in human flesh, what was He to do for those who put their trust in Him? Verses 12, 13.

Notes

1. Whether this title is meant for the entire book or not, it is very suitable for the first part. As the book of Genesis includes much more than the genesis of the heavens and the earth, so may the book of Matthew contain much more than the mere generation or lineage of Jesus.

2. Abraham and David were two great lights in the history of the Jews. All Jews prided themselves on being sons of Abraham and subjects of the royal house of David, especially as the Messiah was promised as the seed of both. Jesus being of the lineage of both according to the flesh, it was fitting that He should be called Son of Abraham and Son of David. Abraham is the spiritual father of all that believe (Rom. 4:11), and David was the prototype of Christ as the King of the true Israel. Christ was frequently addressed as "Thou Son of David," but preferred to style Himself more broadly "the Son of man," emphasizing His relationship to the human family as elder Brother.

3. Jesus is the Greek form of Joshua. See Acts 7:45 and Heb. 4:8, where reference is plainly made to Joshua. The meaning of Joshua is Jehovah-salvation, or Jehovah-saviour. Thus Matthew interprets the name Jesus, when he says, "He shall save His people from their sins."

4. Christ is the Greek equivalent of Messiah, meaning anointed. This is why the expression "the Christ" is sometimes used, the term Christ not being originally a proper name. Thus Jesus Christ means Jesus the anointed.
