Lesson 10 · March 6, 1915

Treatment of Enemies

Lesson Scripture: Matt. 5:38-48

Questions

  1. 1.What is the ancient law of compensation for wrong-doing? Matt. 5:38; Lev. 24:17-22.
  2. 2.Does this law justify a man in avenging himself on a wrong-doer? Prov. 20:22; 24:29. Note 1.
  3. 3.What does Jesus say about our attitude toward the evil-doer? Matt. 5:39-41. Note 2.
  4. 4.How ought we to respond to those who ask for help? Verse 42.
  5. 5.What is to be our guide in giving to the needy? Deut. 15:7, 8.
  6. 6.In what spirit should we render aid to the needy? Luke 6:35.
  7. 7.What compensation is assured to those who give to the poor? Deut. 15:10.
  8. 8.What other saying did Jesus mention? Matt. 5:43.
  9. 9.What course toward enemies does Jesus urge upon us? Verse 44.
  10. 10.Whose children do we thus become? Verse 45, first part.
  11. 11.How does our Father illustrate this lesson in nature? Verse 45, last part.
  12. 12.If we do no more than love those who love us, to whom may we be compared? Verse 46; Luke 6:32.
  13. 13.What is intimated concerning those who greet their brethren only? Matt. 5:47.
  14. 14.To whom would our Father have us be kind? Luke 6:35, last part.
  15. 15.What standard is set before us? Matt. 5:48.

Notes

1. The law in Leviticus requiring the offender to restore in kind, was a civil law, to be administered by proper authority. Its supreme justice is evident. It does not, however, justify a man's seeking to requite the wrong himself, for in so doing he might commit a greater wrong. The exacting of the penalty must be left to disinterested persons.

2. The Revised Version reads, "Resist not him that is evil," or, more literally, "Resist not the evil one." This instruction was especially fitting to the Jews under the Roman yoke, and was evidently meant to teach them not to resist the authority of their rulers. How wonderfully Jesus exemplified the principle during His trial and crucifixion, not only toward authorities, but toward any who did Him evil! See Isa. 50:6. Such a course will often cause him "that is of the contrary part" to be ashamed.
