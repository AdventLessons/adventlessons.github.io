Lesson 10 · June 8, 1901

The Offerings (Continued)

The Burnt-Offering

  1. 1.What must the burnt-offering be? How must it be offered? .
  2. 2.How does the worshiper identify himself with this offering? What promise is connected with it? .
  3. 3.What is then done with the offering? How much of it is accepted? .
  4. 4.Were the daily offerings burnt-offerings or sin-offerings? . What did they symbolize? Ans.—"The daily consecration of the nation to Jehovah, and their constant dependence upon the atoning blood of Christ."—.
  5. 5.How was the acceptance of this offering shown? ; ; , margin. Ans.—"Fire flashed from heaven and consumed the sacrifice."—. Note 1.
  6. 6.The fire having once been kindled by the Lord, what command was given concerning it? . Note 2.
  7. 7.How are we to present ourselves to the Lord? .
  8. 8.How do we find acceptance? . Note 3.

The Meat-Offering

  1. 9.Of what materials was the meat-offering composed? . Note 4.
  2. 10.What was represented by the flour? .
  3. 11.What was poured upon the flour? . What was poured upon Christ? . What, then, is represented by the oil? ; . Note 5.
  4. 12.With what was the offering seasoned? . What are we exhorted to have? ; . By thus using salt in their sacrifices the people were bound to Jehovah in most solemn covenant. Read ; . Note 6.
  5. 13.Everything in Christ's outer and inner life was a sweet odor to God; hence how much of the frankincense was to be burnt on God's altar? . Repeat . How much of the oil and flour was burnt? . What was done with the rest of the offering? What kind of offering was it? .
  6. 14.In what different ways could it be prepared? . What must be excluded from it? ; ; ; . What is represented by leaven? Honey would not stand the fire, hence was excluded.

Notes

  1. 1.When we see sin losing its hold upon us, being reduced as it were to ashes, by the fire of the Holy Spirit, we may know that God is accepting our consecration, and that we are serving the true God. .
  2. 2.This fire kindled by God, represented one phase of the work of the Holy Spirit. ; ; . This fire must never be allowed to go out. . So we are commanded not to quench the Holy Spirit, but should keep it burning, with the fat of our sin-offerings and our whole burnt-offerings.
  3. 3.In these different sacrifices we get so many different aspects of Christ's one sacrifice. All the sacrifices stood for the one who offered them. Christ gave Himself for us. Christ is our burnt-offering: we bring Him to God, and He accepts Him as a whole burnt-offering for us, and counts us just as worthy as the offering we bring. .
  4. 4.There was no life taken nor any blood shed in this offering, hence it did not represent Christ in His death, but Christ in His life. . In the meat or food-offering we have a shadow of the perfect Man.
  5. 5.This offering was either baked, boiled, or fried in oil, showing how fully the Holy Spirit entered into the life and work of Christ (), and must not be separated from ours.
  6. 6.The salt represented the saving qualities of the righteousness of Christ. By using salt in their sacrifices the people were bound to Jehovah in a most solemn covenant. When the Arabs make a covenant together they put salt on the blade of a sword, and every one puts a little in his mouth. This constitutes them blood relations, and they remain faithful to each other, even when life is in danger.
