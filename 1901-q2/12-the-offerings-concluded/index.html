Lesson 12 · June 22, 1901

The Offerings (Concluded)

PDFShare

The Purification-Offering

  1. 1.What command did the Lord give to Moses and Aaron? Num. 19:1, 2.
  2. 2.Who is represented by this red heifer? Ans.—Christ. Why must the heifer be red? Isa. 63:1-3; Rev. 19:13.
  3. 3.What is represented by a yoke? Lam. 1:14; Nahum 1:11, 13; Gal. 5:1. Note 1.
  4. 4.Did Christ ever come under this yoke? 2 Cor. 5:21; 1 Peter 2:21, 22.
  5. 5.Who were to bring the red heifer to the priest? Who was to slay it? Where was it to suffer? Num. 19:2, 3.
  6. 6.Who brought Christ to the priest? Who slew Him? Matt. 26:47, 57; 27:2, 26. Where did He suffer? Heb. 13:12.
  7. 7.What was then done with the offering? What was burnt with it? Num. 19:4-6.
  8. 8.What was done with the ashes? For what purpose were they kept? Verse 9.
  9. 9.For whose benefit were they used? Verse 10.
  10. 10.Who were to be benefited by the death of Christ? Heb. 2:9; Acts 2:39.
  11. 11.For what was this offering made? What was the effect upon the tabernacle if the person was not cleansed? Num. 19:11, 13, 16.
  12. 12.What was the effect upon everything the unclean person touched? Verse 22; Hag. 2:13, 14.
  13. 13.What would befall the one who refused to purify himself? Num. 19:20.
  14. 14.Who only will be cut off? Ps. 37:9.
  15. 15.What, then, is represented by touching a dead body?
  16. 16.How did Paul feel when he came in contact with it? Rom. 7:23, 24, margin.
  17. 17.Describe the manner of purification. Num. 19:17-19.
  18. 18.What does the water represent? Eph. 5:26. Note 2.
  19. 19.How did David refer to this mode of cleansing? Ps. 51:7.
  20. 20.If the touching of the dead body represented sin and death, what did the cleansing represent? Eph. 2:1, 4, 5. Repeat Heb. 9:13, 14.

Notes

1. The yoke represents the yoke of bondage and sin. Christ never came under this yoke. 1 Peter 2:22. He who was above the law, being the Author of the law, voluntarily became sin for us, suffering the penalty of the broken law in our stead. Yet, never having committed sin, He never came under the yoke of bondage. His submission was wholly voluntary.

2. This was called the water of separation, because when sprinkled upon a defiled person it separated him from his uncleanness, and admitted him to all the privileges which he for a time had lost. Num. 5:2. The ashes point to the suffering and death of Christ; the Holy Spirit uses the word (symbolized by the running water) to remind us of that suffering. Ps. 19:7, margin.
