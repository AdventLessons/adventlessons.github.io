Lesson 4 · May 15, 1886

Breadth of the Law

PDFShare

[Note to the Student.—Do not consider the lesson learned until you can give at least the substance of every text, with the correct reference for each. The references in black letters indicate those texts that should be committed to memory. A little diligent application each day will enable you to learn them, although this need not be considered a test of scholarship.]

  1. 1.What can you say of the perfection of the ten commandments?
  2. 2.Of whose character are they a likeness? Give proof.
  3. 3.How much of our duty is comprised in the ten commandments?
  4. 4.If we do the commandments, what sort of characters will we have? Deut. 6:25.
  5. 5.Of whose righteousness will we be partakers? 1 John 3:7; compare Isa. 51:6, 7.
  6. 6.If to do the commandments is righteousness, what must it be to fail to do them?
  7. 7.And what is all unrighteousness? 1 John 5:17.
  8. 8.Then what is sin? 1 John 3:4.
  9. 9.How may we know that any action is sinful? Rom. 7:7.
  10. 10.To what law does Paul here refer as pointing out sin?—To the law which contains the commandment "Thou shalt not covet."
  11. 11.Then of what law is it that John speaks when he says that "Sin is the transgression of the law"?—Of the law of ten commandments.
  12. 12.Can there be any sin committed that is not covered by the law? Rom. 4:15; 5:13.
  13. 13.What did David say of the law? Ps. 119:96.
  14. 14.How broad is it? Heb. 4:12.
  15. 15.How did Jesus illustrate this in the case of the sixth commandment? Matt. 5:21, 22.
  16. 16.Do the commandments forbid evil thoughts and desires as well as open sins? Matt. 5:27, 28.
  17. 17.What is the whole duty of man? Eccl. 12:13.
  18. 18.What reason is given why we should do our duty by keeping the commandments? Verse 14.
  19. 19.What does this statement indicate? (See note.)

Notes

"Fear God, and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man. For God will bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good, or whether it be evil." Eccl. 12:13, 14. Keeping the commandments is declared to be the whole duty of man. The reason given why we should thus do our whole duty, is that "God will bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing." This statement, coming in the connection that it does, can indicate nothing else than that the commandments of God cover every work, with every secret thing. Whosoever therefore keeps the commandments will be perfect, even in his secret thoughts.
