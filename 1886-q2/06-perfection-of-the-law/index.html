Lesson 3 · May 8, 1886

Perfection of the Law

PDFShare

[Note to the Student.—Do not consider the lesson learned until you can give at least the substance of every text, with the correct reference for each. The references in black letters indicate those texts that should be committed to memory. A little diligent application each day will enable you to learn them, although this need not be considered a test of scholarship.]

  1. 1.Under what circumstances were the ten commandments spoken?
  2. 2.What steps were taken for their preservation?
  3. 3.Where do we find them recorded?
  4. 4.In what two precepts are these ten commandments summed up? Matt. 22:35-40.
  5. 5.In making this statement, did Christ utter a new truth? Lev. 19:18; Deut. 6:5. He quoted from the law. See Matt. 22:36.
  6. 6.Can there be any duty outside of what we owe to God and our fellow-creatures?
  7. 7.Then what may be said of the ten commandments? Eccl. 12:13.
  8. 8.What does David say of the commandments? Ps. 119:172.
  9. 9.What does the Lord, through one of his prophets, say of his own righteousness? Isa. 51:6.
  10. 10.Who are they who know (or have) this righteousness? Isa. 51:7.
  11. 11.Then what is the law of God?—The righteousness of God.
  12. 12.Since the law of God is his righteousness, what may truly be said of it? Ps. 19:7.
  13. 13.Quote other statements from the Psalms concerning the nature of the law. Ps. 111:7; 119:86, 138, 142, 151.
  14. 14.What did Paul say of it? Rom. 7:12.
  15. 15.Since the commandments are an expression of the righteousness of God, is it possible to speak of them in too exalted terms?

Notes

Since the law of God is "the righteousness of God," or the expression of God's character, it is evident that every epithet which is applied to the character of God, may with propriety also be applied to the law.
