Lesson 10 · June 26, 1886

Christ the End of the Law

PDFShare

(Continued.)

[Note to the Student.—Do not consider the lesson learned until you can give at least the substance of every text, with the correct reference for each. The references in black letters indicate those texts that should be committed to memory. A little diligent application each day will enable you to learn them, although this need not be considered a test of scholarship.]

  1. 1.To whom is Christ the end of the law? Rom. 10:4.
  2. 2.For what purpose is he the end of the law to believers? Rom. 10:4.
  3. 3.What is righteousness?—Obedience to the law. See Deut. 6:25.
  4. 4.What will those have who keep the law? Rom. 10:5; Matt. 19:17.
  5. 5.Then since it was designed that men should keep the law, to what may the law be said to have been ordained or designed? Rom. 7:16.
  6. 6.But since all men have violated the law, what does it now bring them? Rom. 7:10; 6:23.
  7. 7.By what means has life again been brought to view? 2 Tim. 1:10.
  8. 8.Who has life to bestow? John 5:25, 26.
  9. 9.For what purpose did he come to earth? John 10:10.
  10. 10.To whom will he give this life? John 3:16.
  11. 11.Since the law, which was ordained to life, cannot, because of sin, give us life, who is now our life? Col. 3:4.
  12. 12.Then how, as a whole, is Christ the end of the law?—He secures to believers justification before God (Rom. 3:24, 25; 8:1); he enables us to keep the law (Rom. 8:3, 4), a thing that without him we could not do (Gal. 5:17; John 15:5); and as a consequence of our continued faith and obedience, he secures to us eternal life (John 3:16; Matt. 19:17; Rev. 22:14); thus enabling the law to fulfill its design, which was to give life (Rom. 7:10).
  13. 13.Then what may Christ truly be said to be to those who believe in him? 1 Cor. 1:30.
  14. 14.In whom alone can we be complete? Col. 2:8-10.
