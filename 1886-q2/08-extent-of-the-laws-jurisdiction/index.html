Lesson 5 · May 22, 1886

Extent of the Law's Jurisdiction

PDFShare

[Note to the Student.—Do not consider the lesson learned until you can give at least the substance of every text, with the correct reference for each. The references in black letters indicate those texts that should be committed to memory. A little diligent application each day will enable you to learn them, although this need not be considered a test of scholarship.]

  1. 1.What is sin? 1 John 3:4.
  2. 2.Is everything that is wrong a violation of the law? 1 John 5:17.
  3. 3.Is sin ever imputed where there is no law? Rom. 4:15.
  4. 4.Then when we find sin imputed to any people, of what may we be sure?
  5. 5.What does the Bible say of Cain? Gen. 4:7, 8; 1 John 3:12.
  6. 6.What is said of the men of Sodom? Gen. 13:13.
  7. 7.Then, according to Rom. 4:15, what must we conclude concerning Cain and the men of Sodom?
  8. 8.Why would not God allow Abraham and his family to occupy the land of Canaan as soon as the promise was made? Gen. 15:13, 16.
  9. 9.What was the particular sin of the Amorites? 1 Kings 21:26.
  10. 10.What law forbids idolatry?
  11. 11.What other positive proof have we that the people in the time of Abraham had the law? Gen. 26:5.
  12. 12.By whom did sin enter into the world? Rom. 5:12.
  13. 13.Then what did Adam violate?
  14. 14.What particular period does the apostle specify, during which death reigned? Rom. 5:14.
  15. 15.What alone causes death? Rom. 5:12; James 1:15.
  16. 16.And what is necessary in order that sin may be imputed? Rom. 5:13.
  17. 17.Then what besides sin and death must also have existed from Adam to Moses?
  18. 18.What does Paul say of the condition of both Jews and Gentiles? Rom. 3:9.
  19. 19.If one is charged with sin, of what is he certainly in possession? Rom. 4:15; 5:13.
  20. 20.To whom alone can a law speak? Rom. 3:19.
  21. 21.As a consequence of what the law says, how do all the world stand? Ib.
  22. 22.Then who are under obligation to keep the law?—The whole world.
  23. 23.If the law existed from the beginning, why was it necessary to give the law from Sinai? Rom. 5:20.

Notes

Since "sin is the transgression of the law," "and sin is not imputed when there is no law," it follows that the fact that sin is imputed to any people is evidence that they had the law and were amenable to it. Therefore since sin is imputed to the Amorites, the Sodomites, Cain, Adam, etc., all those people knew, and were answerable to, the law of God.

"The law entered that the offense might abound." Rom. 5:20. An offense is a violation of law, a transgression, a sin. Therefore the text means, the law entered that the sin might abound. Then there was sin before the time spoken of when the law "entered." But as there can be no sin without law, it follows that the law existed before the time when "the law entered," which was at Sinai. The "entering" of the law was the formal giving of it from Sinai. Before that time, people knew the law only as portions of it remained written in their hearts (Rom. 2:15), or as they were taught by the servants of God, like Enoch, Noah, and Abraham, who had direct communication with Heaven. But after the giving of the law from Sinai, each person could at all times carefully examine the whole law for himself, and consequently sins which before might have seemed trivial, or might have passed unnoticed, were made to stand out in their true character. Thus the offense was made to "abound." As Paul elsewhere says, sin, by the commandment, became "exceeding sinful." Rom. 7:13.
