Lesson 14 · April 10, 1886

The Sanctuary and Its Cleansing

PDFShare

(Review.)

[Note to the Student.—Do not consider the lesson learned until you can give at least the substance of every text, with the correct reference for each. The references in black letters indicate those texts that should be committed to memory. A little diligent application each day will enable you to learn them, although this need not be considered a test of scholarship.]

  1. 1.After ascending from the earth, what position did our Saviour take? Heb. 4:14; 8:1.
  2. 2.What service does he perform there? Heb. 8:2.
  3. 3.How had this heavenly sanctuary been typified? Read Heb. 8:3-5; 9:1-5.
  4. 4.By whose command was this worldly sanctuary made? Heb. 8:5; Ex. 25:1, 2, 8.
  5. 5.How many sacred apartments had this sanctuary? Heb. 9:1-3.
  6. 6.What did each of these apartments contain? Verses 2-5.
  7. 7.What similar things have been shown in the heavenly sanctuary? Rev. 4:1-5; 8:3; 11:19.
  8. 8.What purpose was served by the worldly sanctuary? Heb. 9:9.
  9. 9.What significance had the service of the priests in the worldly sanctuary? Heb. 8, last part of verse 4 and first part of verse 5.
  10. 10.How was the worldly sanctuary figuratively polluted? Lev. 4:3-7, 13-18; 10:17, 18.
  11. 11.By what ceremonies was it cleansed? Lev. 16.
  12. 12.Why was so much importance attached to the blood?—Because it represented the life of the victim. Lev. 17:11.
  13. 13.What was signified by the sprinkling of the blood upon the mercy-seat?—That the law beneath the mercy-seat demanded the life of the one who had transgressed it.
  14. 14.What were all these ceremonies meant to impress upon the minds of men?—That by disobeying God, they had forfeited all right to eternal life; and their only hope of salvation was in the promised Redeemer, who would make an atonement for them. Eze. 18:4; 1 John 3:5; 2 Cor. 5:21.
  15. 15.Who bears the sins of penitent believers? 1 Pet. 2:24.
  16. 16.How long will he continue to bear them?—Till they are blotted from the books of record, and laid upon the head of Satan.
  17. 17.In what two ways, then, do the sins of penitent believers find their way into the heavenly sanctuary?—Christ bears them there, and they are recorded in the books.
  18. 18.How was it in the worldly sanctuary?—The priest bore them in figure till the cleansing of the sanctuary, and it would seem that the blood which stained the altar recorded them.
  19. 19.By what evidence are men to be judged? Dan. 7:9, 10; Rev. 20:12.
  20. 20.What must constitute a part of the cleansing of the sanctuary?—The blotting out of the sins recorded there.
  21. 21.What must take place before they can be blotted out?—They must first serve their purpose in the Judgment.
  22. 22.What follows from this?—That the cleansing of the sanctuary must include an examination of the books, to see who of those that have once entered the service of God are entitled to have their sins blotted out.
  23. 23.Whose names will be retained in the book of life? Rev. 3:5.
  24. 24.How will their sins be canceled? Read Heb. 9:14, 15, 24-26; 1 John 2:2.
  25. 25.What will constitute the complete cleansing of the sanctuary?—The blotting out of sins, and the laying of them upon the head of Satan, the antitypical scapegoat.
  26. 26.What will be done with the sin records that are not blotted out?—They will be reserved to be examined during the Judgment of the thousand years, that sentence may be rendered accordingly, and will confront the sinner when his day of perdition and execution comes. 2 Pet. 3:7; Rev. 20:12-15.
