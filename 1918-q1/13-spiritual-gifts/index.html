Lesson 13 · March 30, 1918

Spiritual Gifts

Questions

  1. 1.… to the subject of spiritual gifts? 1 Cor. 12:1.
  2. 2.What gift is promised every believer, in conversion? Acts 2:38, 39.
  3. 3.As this gift of the Holy Spirit is received, what gifts in turn does He bestow upon the church of Christ as needed for service? 1 Cor. 12:8-10.
  4. 4.How essential to the work of witnessing for Christ are the gifts of the Spirit? Verse 3. Note 1.
  5. 5.As every member of Christ is to be a witness, what then is given to every one? Verse 7.
  6. 6.Who makes distribution of the various gifts for service? Verse 11.
  7. 7.What illustration is used to show the importance of every gift in the work God has given His church to do? Verses 14-18.
  8. 8.What exhortation is given concerning the exercise of these gifts? Rom. 12:3-8.
  9. 9.How does the Corinthian letter list the administrative, teaching, and other gifts in the church? 1 Cor. 12:28. Note 2.
  10. 10.What part do these gifts have in building up and guiding the church in service? Eph. 4:11, 12.
  11. 11.What did the prophet John note as two characteristics of the remnant church which would draw down upon them the attacks of the enemy? Rev. 12:17.
  12. 12.In these visions of the prophet John, what is the testimony of Jesus said to be? Rev. 19:10, last part.
  13. 13.In the experience of the remnant church, what two characteristics have most often aroused opposition? Ans.—Their loyalty to the commandments of God, and their claim to the possession of the gift of the spirit of prophecy. Note 3.
  14. 14.Yet what has the history and experience of this people abundantly demonstrated? 2 Chron. 20:20.
  15. 15.What part have spiritual gifts in maintaining unity and guarding the church from error? Eph. 4:11-15.
  16. 16.No matter what gift for service one may have, what is the essential thing in one's personal experience? 1 Cor. 13:1-3.

Notes

1. Without the Spirit, no truly effective witness can be borne. We are to remember that only the Lord, by His Spirit, can make our service effective. Christ is our example in the continuous confession, "I can of mine own self do nothing." On the other hand, with the Spirit's witness, the feeblest endeavor or testimony may be used of God for the accomplishment of great results.

2. "Helps." This plain gift is nevertheless a precious one. While no one, perhaps, has all the gifts, here is one that all may well aspire to. What a blessing in the church are the "helpers," as one version puts it—always holding up the hands of those leading out in any work, sharing the burdens! The marginal reference, Num. 11:17, is worth looking up.

"Governments." Weymouth's translation renders it "powers of organization." The word is derived from the verb "to steer," as steering or guiding a boat. It is an invaluable gift in church or conference work, as in all organized or associate service.

3. As the days of 1844 brought the beginning of the Judgment hour, and the time for the rise of the remnant church, keeping the commandments of God (Rev. 14:6, 7, 12), the Lord set in that church the gift of the spirit of prophecy. All should be familiar with the work of Sister Ellen G. White, in the exercise of that gift in connection with the cause of God in our time. This gift has been a blessed and guiding agency, under God, in the history of the advent movement. All along, critics have assailed this gift, even as the loyalty of the church to the commandments of God has been assailed; but the gift of the spirit of prophecy has borne the divine credentials in the church. By its fruits it is known. The more perilous the times become, the greater need of giving heed to the instruction that has come through this gift in the remnant church.
