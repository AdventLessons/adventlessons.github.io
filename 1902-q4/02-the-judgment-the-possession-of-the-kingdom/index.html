Lesson 2 · October 11, 1902

The Judgment.—The Possession of the Kingdom

This lesson PDF Whole quarter PDF · 13 lessonsShare on WhatsApp

Questions on Daniel 7:1-18

  1. 1.What did Daniel next behold in his vision?
  2. 2.Who took His position as judge?
  3. 3.What description is given of the Judge?
  4. 4.What description is given of His throne?
  5. 5.What remarkable thing was next observed?
  6. 6.How many were in attendance upon the Judge?
  7. 7.What definite statements interpret the meaning of the scene presented before the prophet?
  8. 8.What next attracted attention in the vision?
  9. 9.How is the final experience of the ten-horned beast described?
  10. 10.What was taken away from the other beasts? What was granted to them?
  11. 11.Who now appears as one of the actors in the vision?
  12. 12.How and where does He come?
  13. 13.What was given unto Him? For what purpose?
  14. 14.What is declared concerning His dominion and kingdom?
  15. 15.How did the vision affect the prophet Daniel?
  16. 16.How did he seek to learn the meaning of the vision? With what result?
  17. 17.What were the four beasts said to be?
  18. 18.Who would afterward take the kingdom? How long would they retain it?

Notes

The judgment scene here described is evidently the same as the one mentioned in , where it is stated that "the dead were judged." It is the great assize of the world, when all that have lived upon the earth have a case in court. The time of the sitting of this final court of award is not given here, but will appear from other prophecies.

The little horn is so closely identified with the fourth beast that when the attention of the prophet is called to it by reason of the great words which it spoke, he noted the slaying of the beast and the burning of his body.

A remarkable person is here introduced in the vision. He is none other than the Son of man. The kingdom given to Him, "which shall not be destroyed," is evidently the same as the fifth kingdom of the second chapter, "which shall never be destroyed." Compare , R. V.

The kingdom which is said in to be given to the Son of man is said in to be taken by the saints of the Most High. Thus it appears that when the kingdom is given to the Son of man it is given to the saints in Him. Compare .

Babylon took the kingdom, but could not retain it. Medo-Persia then took the kingdom, but could not retain it. Grecia followed, and then Rome, but neither of them could retain the kingdom. What these great kingdoms could not do with all their power, the saints of the Most High are to do by the power of faith in Christ Jesus. .
