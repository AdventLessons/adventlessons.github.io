Lesson 3 · July 17, 1920

Education and Redemption

PDFShare

Daily Study Outline

SubjectAssignment
1.Result of mingling good and evilQues. 1,2
2.Results of confusing good with evilQues. 3-7
3.Adam's hope after sinningQues. 8,9
4.The earthy and the heavenly imageQues. 10-12
5.Means of changing the imageQues. 13, 14
6.Common purpose of education and redemptionQues. 15, 16
7.Review the lesson.

Questions

  1. 1.What did God tell our first parents would be the result if they partook of the forbidden tree? Gen. 2:17.
  2. 2.What would eating of this fruit add to the knowledge of the transgressors? Ans. The knowledge of evil.
  3. 3.How did the tempter subtly confuse good and evil in his statement to Eve? Gen. 3:5.
  4. 4.How did Eve confuse good and evil in her view of the matter? Verse 6.
  5. 5.What evils resulted immediately from this confusion and the consequent transgression? Verses 7-13.
  6. 6.What results of sin did God predict for the future? Verses 16-19.
  7. 7.Why was it necessary to drive Adam and Eve from the garden? Verses 22, 23.
  8. 8.Though now under sentence of death through sin, what hope was held out to them in Christ when the curse was pronounced on Satan? Verse 15.
  9. 9.What promise is made to every sinner? 1 Cor. 15:22.
  10. 10.In whose image was Adam created? Gen. 1:27.
  11. 11.What image do all sinners bear? 1 Cor. 15:49, first part.
  12. 12.When made alive in Christ, what image shall we bear? Verse 49, last part.
  13. 13.Through what means is this change in image brought about? Gal. 2:20; 3:13; 2 Cor. 3:18.
  14. 14.What, then, is the object of redemption? Ans. To restore in man the image of God that he lost by sin.
  15. 15.What is the true object of Christian education? Ans. "The true object of education is to restore the image of God in the soul."—"Patriarchs and Prophets," page 595.
  16. 16.What, then, is the relation of Christian education to redemption?

Lessons We Can Learn

  1. 1.God's original purpose for man was that he should have knowledge of only the good. As his knowledge of good, of God, should increase, he would grow more Godlike in character.
  2. 2.By partaking of the forbidden tree—forbidden so as to prevent the learning of evil—man added the knowledge of evil to his knowledge of good.
  3. 3.Henceforth it was easy to confuse good and evil. In fact, Eve was enticed to a knowledge of evil by what she mistook for good, and Adam joined her in transgression because of a mistaken view of the highest good—he would rather die in transgression with Eve than live in righteousness without her.
  4. 4.The fruits of evil followed immediately upon the knowledge of evil: sense of guilt and shame, laying blame for wrongdoing upon others, insinuation that even God was to blame, loss of access to the tree of life, exile from their happy home.
  5. 5.Though it was necessary to pronounce judgment on the sinner; God mingled that judgment with mercy in the promise of a Redeemer to restore the lost estate.
  6. 6.The object of redemption is to renew in man the image of God in which he was created. The object of Christian education is identical with that of redemption: "To restore the image of God in the soul."
  7. 7.CONCLUSION: "In the highest sense, the work of education and the work of redemption are one; for in education, as in redemption, 'other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ.' "—"Education," page 30.
