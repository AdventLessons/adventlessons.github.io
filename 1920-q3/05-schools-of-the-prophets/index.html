Lesson 5 · July 31, 1920

Schools of the Prophets

PDFShare

Questions

  1. 1.What is a prophet? 1 Sam. 9:9. A "seer" is a see-er, one who, by spiritual endowment, sees deeply into things past or things present or things future. A "prophet," a later name for "seer," is a for-speaker, a mouthpiece of God. See Amos 7:12.
  2. 2.Who was called in his childhood to become a prophet? 1 Sam. 3:2-4, 20.
  3. 3.What part did his mother have in his consecration to the Lord? 1 Sam. 1:20, 27, 28.
  4. 4.What was the spiritual situation in Israel at that time? 1 Sam. 3:1; 2:12, 17; 3:12, 13; 4:21, 22.
  5. 5.What was one of the chief causes of this spiritual decline? 1 Sam. 3:12, 13. Ans. Unfaithfulness of parents in the instruction and discipline of their children.
  6. 6.What special command had been given to parents just before Israel entered Canaan? Deut. 11:18-21.
  7. 7.What was the result of disregarding this command? Ans. "Through unfaithfulness in the home, and idolatrous influences without, many of the Hebrew youth received an education differing widely from that which God had planned for them. They learned the ways of the heathen."—"Education," pages 45, 46.
  8. 8.Where did Samuel live? 1 Sam. 7:17.
  9. 9.Where was the ark of the Lord? Verses 1, 2.
  10. 10.What did Samuel teach the people? With what result? Verses 3, 4.
  11. 11.What further provision was made for instruction? Ans. "Further provision was made for the instruction of the young, by the establishment of the schools of the prophets."—"Patriarchs and Prophets," page 593.
  12. 12.Where were these schools located? Ans. "In Samuel's day there were two of these schools,—one at Ramah, the home of the prophet, and the other at Kirjath-jearim, where the ark then was. Others were established in later times."—Ib.
  13. 13.Who attended these schools? Ans. "If a youth desired to search deeper into the truths of the Word of God, and to seek wisdom from above, that he might become a teacher in Israel, these schools were open to him."—Ib.
  14. 14.What were these students called? Ans. "Samuel gathered companies of young men who were pious, intelligent, and studious. These were called the sons of the prophets."—Ib. See also 2 Kings 2:3, 5, 7, 15.
  15. 15.Who were the instructors in these schools? Ans. "The instructors were men not only well versed in divine truth, but those who had themselves enjoyed communion with God, and had received the special endowment of His Spirit."—Ib.
  16. 16.What were the chief subjects of study? Ans. "The chief subjects of study in these schools were the law of God, with the instructions given to Moses, sacred history, sacred music, and poetry."—Ib.
  17. 17.What was the grand object of all study? Ans. "In those schools of the olden time it was the grand object of all study to learn the will of God, and man's duty toward Him."—Ib.
  18. 18.How did the students and many of the teachers support themselves? Ans. "The pupils of these schools sustained themselves by their own labor in tilling the soil, or in some mechanical employment. . . . Many also of the teachers supported themselves by manual labor."—"Education," page 47.
  19. 19.What was the spiritual tone of these schools? Ans. "A spirit of devotion was cherished. Not only were students taught the duty of prayer, but they were taught how to pray, how to approach their Creator, how to exercise faith in Him, and how to understand and obey the teachings of His Spirit. Sanctified intellects brought forth from the treasure house of God, things new and old, and the Spirit of God was manifested in prophecy and sacred song."—"Patriarchs and Prophets," page 594.
  20. 20.What is the true object of education? Ans. "The true object of education is to restore the image of God in the soul."—Id., page 595.
  21. 21.What were the results of the work done in the schools of the prophets? Ans. "These schools 'proved to be one of the means most effective in promoting that righteousness which 'exalteth a nation.' In no small degree they aided in laying the foundation of that marvelous prosperity which distinguished the reigns of David and Solomon."—"Education," pages 47, 48.

Lessons We Can Learn

  1. 1.A seer is one who sees deeply into the things of God, past, present, and future.
  2. 2.A prophet is one who speaks for God, in reference to things past, present, or future.
  3. 3.In the fuller sense, a prophet is divinely appointed to his work. In a modified sense, a prophet is divinely called to do the work of teaching. See "Education," page 46.
  4. 4.What any one can do for God depends much upon the kind of education he receives in childhood and youth.
  5. 5.Parents have the usefulness and destiny of their children largely in their own hands.
  6. 6.The great danger of youth is that they will "learn the ways of the heathen" instead of the ways of God.
  7. 7.In addition to a Christian home, the greatest safeguard for our children is to send them to a school whose avowed object is to "restore the image of God in the soul."
