Lesson 9 · February 27, 1909

Knowing Christ

This lesson PDF Whole quarter PDF · 13 lessonsShare on WhatsApp

.

Suggestion.—Read the two chapters studied, and fix in mind the points developed in the previous lessons. Be able to state clearly the relation existing between Paul and the Philippian brethren, and the circumstances under which the epistle was written.

  2. 2.How important does this admonition seem to be? ; .
  3. 3.Who only can rejoice in the Lord? .
  4. 4.Against what did Paul warn the Philippians? . Note 1.
  5. 5.What is meant by the "concision"? Ans. The circumcision, i. e., the Jews. See . Note 2.
  6. 6.Who are the truly circumcised? ; .
  7. 7.What did circumcision indicate? .
  8. 8.Were circumcised people counted as true children of Abraham if they had not righteousness? ; Matt. 3:⟦9?⟧
  9. 9.If an outwardly circumcised person had not righteousness, how was his circumcision counted? .
  10. 10.Who alone is man's righteousness? ; .
  11. 11.Then in whom must all the truly circumcised have trusted? .
  12. 12.If outward observances could avail for any one, how did Paul stand? .
  13. 13.What were some of the things in which he once trusted? .
  14. 14.How could he trust in his zeal in persecuting the church? .
  15. 15.Why was he not perfect, since he was blameless "touching the righteousness which is in the law"? . Note 3.
  16. 16.How did he count all these things in which he might have trusted? .

Notes

1. The word "dog" is used in the Bible as a term of reproach, to signify a shameless, corrupt person. See .

2. From the matter-of-course way in which the apostle goes on to explain what the concision is, as contrasted with that which is only outward, we conclude that by the word "concision" he means "circumcision." This was a term used synonymously with Jews, but is here used with special reference to those Jews who trusted in circumcision as the means of justification. These were classed with dogs and evil workers, as equally dangerous, for they would turn all who listened to them away from the simplicity of the gospel, a proceeding classed with the beguiling of Eve by the serpent. .

3. "Concerning zeal, persecuting the church; touching the righteousness which is in the law, blameless." Besides his birth and his orthodox training, the two points above mentioned were perhaps the things upon which Saul, the Pharisee, depended most for salvation. Believing that the traditions of the fathers were the commands of God, he was "more exceedingly zealous" for them than any other man in the nation. Therefore the more he persecuted those who opposed traditions, the more favor he thought he was finding with God. Then, too, he was a Pharisee of the strictest sect. That is, he was most punctilious in the outward observance of the law. But when he saw the righteousness of the law as it is in Christ, he acknowledged that all the self-righteousness upon which he had been building his hopes of salvation was so far short of perfection that it was sufficient to condemn him; and he gladly counted it worse than useless, that he might receive the free gift of Christ's perfect righteousness.
