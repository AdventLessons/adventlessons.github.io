Lesson 11 · March 13, 1909

The Heavenly Citizenship

PDFShare

Phil. 3:15-21.

Questions

  1. 1.What was the one thing that Paul wanted to know and have? Phil. 3:8-10.
  2. 2.Did he count himself as having reached perfection? Verse 12.
  3. 3.What did he do? Verses 13, 14.
  4. 4.What exhortation does he give to others? Verses 15, 16.
  5. 5.What is the "same rule" by which we should walk? Gal. 6:14-16.
  6. 6.What confidence did Paul, by the Spirit of the Lord, have in the course which he was pursuing? Phil. 3:17.
  7. 7.Would he have us follow him regardless of Christ? 1 Cor. 11:1; Eph. 5:1; 1 Thess. 1:6.
  8. 8.What necessity is there for taking note of those who walk according to the rule set forth by Paul? Phil. 3:18, 19.
  9. 9.Instead of minding earthly things, what should we mind? Col. 3:1, 2 (margin).
  10. 10.Why should we do this? Phil. 3:20. Note.
  11. 11.If our citizenship is in heaven, how shall we hold ourselves as relates to this world? 1 Peter 2:11; Heb. 11:13-16.
  12. 12.How did David, in the height of his power, regard himself? Ps. 39:12.
  13. 13.Since our citizenship is in heaven, for what purpose do we look for the Saviour's return? John 14:1-3.
  14. 14.Before taking us to the eternal mansions that He has prepared for us, what will He do? Phil. 3:21; 1 Cor. 15:51-54.
  15. 15.What is the power by which this wonderful transformation will be effected? Phil. 3:20, 21; 2 Peter 1:3.

Notes

"For our citizenship is in heaven." A.R.V. This is the proper reading of the first part of Phil. 3:20. The word "citizenship" expresses the exact meaning of the Greek word. The idea is that this present world is not our home. Our citizenship is in heaven, where is our King; there is the city which contains our permanent residence; and we look for our Saviour to take us there according to His promise. Therefore, as stated in Phil. 1:27, "we should live as citizens, worthily."
