Lesson 5 · May 5, 1917

Paul's Defense Before Felix

Lesson Scripture: Acts 24:1-27.

Memory verse

The Narrative

  1. 1.Who appeared against Paul after five days? Acts 24:1. Note 1.
  2. 2.How did Tertullus, the lawyer, open his case? Verses 2-4. Note 2.
  3. 3.What were the charges against Paul? Verses 5, 6. Note 3.
  4. 4.What interference with the plans of the Jews did Tertullus resent? How was his plea supported? Verses 6-9.
  5. 5.How did Paul open his defense? Verses 10, 11.
  6. 6.What was his answer to the charges made against him? Verses 12, 13. Note 4.
  7. 7.What confession of faith did Paul make? Verses 14-16.
  8. 8.What was Paul's story of his arrest at Jerusalem? Verses 17-21.
  9. 9.What gave Felix an advantage in understanding the situation? What did he do? Verses 22, 23.
  10. 10.What opportunity came to Paul to bear witness for Christ? Verse 24.
  11. 11.What is said of his sermon and its effect? Verse 25. Note 5.
  12. 12.What corrupt motive influenced Felix? What did he do in his hope of gain? Verse 26.
  13. 13.What is the last word concerning Felix? Verse 27. Note 6.

Lessons for Us

  1. 1.On what is the Christian faith based? Verse 14; 2 Tim. 3:15-17.
  2. 2.What message is due to the world in this generation? Acts 24:25; Rev. 14:7.
  3. 3.Earnest as Paul was, what methods did he avoid? Acts 24:12; Isa. 42:1-3.
  4. 4.The danger of delay. The more convenient season never came. Acts 24:25. "Now." 2 Cor. 6:2.

Notes

1. Tertullus. "The name is Roman, and there is little doubt that he was an Italian, and spoke on this occasion in Latin."—Conybeare and Howson.

2. The usual recourse was had to flowery and untruthful flattery. Yet, while the character of Felix was base,—as the Roman writer, Tacitus, says, "he exercised the power of a king with the temper of a slave,"—he had put down the bandits and robbers with an iron hand, and had pursued and scattered the band of revolutionists led by the fanatical Egyptian, for whom the Roman captain had at first mistaken Paul. Acts 21:38. Paul's own manner of address, a few moments later (verse 10), was respectful and complimentary, but without false flattery.

3. The climax of the accusation was the charge of profanation of the temple. The Romans allowed the Jews to put any one to death who was found polluting their temple. With this, it was charged that Paul was a stirrer up of sedition, the Jews knowing well that the Roman authorities were likely to lay hands on any one raising sedition, with which they had had so much trouble.

4. Paul showed that he had not been long enough in Jerusalem to be the leader of a seditious party. He challenged proof of the things charged. The Jews themselves were seditious, and plotters against the superior civil authority, which later they tried to overturn altogether. As a Christian, the apostle Paul was subject to the powers of government in all things civil.

5. Temperance means self-control, chastity. Felix was living a vicious life, and the terrors of the coming Judgment made him tremble.

6. The inspired record leaves Felix in his baseness, doing injustice to Paul to please the Jews. There had been an outbreak between the Jews and the gentiles in Cæsarea; and on accusation of the Jews, Felix had been called to account by Nero, at Rome. He was pardoned, however, but apparently never achieved distinction again, and he had turned from life eternal. It is evident, from history, that Drusilla and her son by Felix perished in the great eruption of Vesuvius, in A. D. 79. One of those shapeless human forms dug out of the ashes and lava beds of Pompeii in recent years may represent the wicked but beautiful princess who listened to Paul's sermon that day in Cæsarea, calling in vain to repentance before the Judgment to come.
