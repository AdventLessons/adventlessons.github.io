Lesson 12 · September 18, 1909

Parables: The Tares; the Mustard Seed; the Leaven; the Hidden Treasure; the Pearl; the Fish; the Householder

Related Scripture: .

Lesson Helps: ; 95-134.

Questions

The Tares.

  1. 1.What was the second parable Jesus put forth? .
  2. 2.Relate the conversation that occurred between the householder and his servants. .
  3. 3.When did the householder say the wheat and tares would be separated? .
  4. 4.When Jesus had sent the multitude away, what question did the disciples ask? .
  5. 5.Whom did the sower represent? .
  6. 6.What was represented by the field? By the good seed? By the tares? By the enemy who sowed them? By the harvest? By the reapers? .
  7. 7.What separation and disposition will be made of the two classes? .

Notes

The Mustard Seed.

  1. 8.To what other thing did Jesus liken the kingdom of God? .

Notes

The Leaven.

  1. 9.To what was the kingdom again likened? .
  2. 10.What scripture was Jesus fulfilling in teaching in parables? .

Notes

The Hidden Treasure and Pearl.

  1. 11.By what parables did Jesus show the exceeding value of God's grace and salvation and the importance of seeking for it? .

Notes

The Fish.

  1. 12.By what parable did He emphatically show that the world would never be converted? .
  2. 13.What question did Jesus then ask His disciples? What did they reply? .

Notes

The Householder.

  1. 14.How did He emphasize the importance of studying the word and of having a living experience? .

Notes

We present no extended notes to this lesson. In will be found splendid developments of the passages of scripture. These lessons are worthy of double emphasis: 1. Jesus taught that evil and good would continue to the end; 2. that it was not His servants' duty to execute judgment upon sinners; this He had reserved to Himself at the end of the world; 3. that the business of the Christian is to sow the seed of truth; to spread the leaven and life of the kingdom; to lay all upon the altar that we may win the great prize; to teach the truth of God applicable to the times.
