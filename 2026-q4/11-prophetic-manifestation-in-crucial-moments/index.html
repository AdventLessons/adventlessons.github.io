Lesson 11 · December 5, 2026

Prophetic Manifestation in Crucial Moments

PDFShare

Sabbath Afternoon

Read for This Week’s Study: 2 Pet. 2:5; Judg. 4:4–16; 1 Kings 18:21, 38, 39; Isa. 59:1–4; Neh. 9:30; Rev. 1:1–3.

Memory verse

Sunday, December 6: Disaster Warning

  1. Read Hebrews 11:7, 2 Peter 2:5, and Genesis 6:9. In what ways was Noah a “preacher of righteousness”?
  2. Next time you see a rainbow, think about the promise found there. Why should it give you comfort, knowing that God keeps His promises?

Also cited: Gen. 4:26; Gen. 6:5–7; Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 92; Gen. 9:8–17.

Notes

Noah's warning was accompanied by actions that showed he believed what God had told him. “While Noah was giving his warning message to the world, his works testified of his sincerity. It was thus that his faith was perfected and made evident.”—Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 95. “By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house; by the which he condemned the world, and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith.” (Hebrews 11:7)

After the Flood, God gave the rainbow as a sign of His promise to the earth. “I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth.” (Genesis 9:13) “This does not imply that He would ever forget; but He speaks to us in our own language, that we may better understand Him.”—Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 106.

The rainbow still reminds us that God keeps His promises, and Noah's example calls us to act on what He has said.

Monday, December 7: A Mother in Israel

  1. Read Judges 4:4–16 and Judges 5:1–5. What did Deborah do for Israel?
  2. How can we learn to trust God when, at the present moment, things do not look very hopeful?

Also cited: Deuteronomy 27–30; Judg. 2:16; Judg. 4:14.

Notes

When Israel faced an army it could not overcome by its own strength, Deborah called Barak to trust God's promise. “Our heavenly Father has a thousand ways to provide for us of which we know nothing.”—The Ministry of Healing, p. 481. “And Deborah said unto Barak, Up; for this is the day in which the LORD hath delivered Sisera into thine hand: is not the LORD gone out before thee?” (Judges 4:14)

The same God who went before Barak can make a way for us when our circumstances seem hopeless.

Tuesday, December 8: A Call for Repentance

  1. Read Judges 2:16–19. What pattern, or motif, is seen here?
  2. Read Elijah’s proclamation to Israel and the response in 1 Kings 18:21, 38, 39. What happened?
  3. Our carnal natures push us to rebel, even subconsciously, against God. What choices do we need to make in order not to succumb to that nature?

Also cited: 1 Samuel 8; 1 Kings 18:18; 1 Kings 18:10–12; 1 Kings 18:17; 1 Kings 18:19; Isa. 58:11.

Notes

Israel's repeated departures from God brought suffering, yet He continued calling His people back to Himself. “Today, as in the days of Elijah, the line of demarcation between God's commandment-keeping people and the worshipers of false gods is clearly drawn.”—Prophets and Kings, p. 187.

On Mount Carmel, Elijah called the people to make a choice they had long avoided. “And Elijah came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people answered him not a word.” (1 Kings 18:21)

When God answered, the people could no longer deny whom they had witnessed. “And when all the people saw it, they fell on their faces: and they said, The LORD, he is the God; the LORD, he is the God.” (1 Kings 18:39)

We too must consider whether our daily choices agree with the faith we profess.

Wednesday, December 9: Hope in Exile

  1. Read Isaiah 59:1–4 and Nehemiah 9:30. What is being said here that applies all though the ages?
  2. Think about God’s faithfulness even when Israel turned away from Him. How have you seen the reality of this aspect of God’s loving character in your own life? Where would you be if He weren’t so patient and loving?

Also cited: 2 Chron. 36:15; Isa. 1:9, 10; Isaiah 58; 2 Kings 17:1–6; 2 Kings 24, 25; Matt. 23:37; Isa. 59:20.

Notes

Israel's exile followed years of rejected warnings, but God's willingness to save His people had not changed. “Behold, the LORD’s hand is not shortened, that it cannot save; neither his ear heavy, that it cannot hear” (Isaiah 59:1)

The problem was never a lack of power or willingness on God's part. “Yet many years didst thou forbear them, and testifiedst against them by thy spirit in thy prophets: yet would they not give ear” (Nehemiah 9:30) “When men's hearts are softened and subdued by the constraining influence of the Holy Spirit, they will give heed to counsel; but when they turn from admonition until their hearts become hardened, the Lord permits them to be led by other influences.”—Prophets and Kings, p. 425.

Even in Babylon, the people had reason to look forward to restoration. “The writings of Jeremiah were within their reach, and in these was plainly set forth the length of time that should elapse before the restoration of Israel from Babylon.”—Prophets and Kings, p. 552.

When we face the consequences of wrong choices, we can still turn to God and take His promises seriously.

Thursday, December 10: A Prophet for the Time of Waiting

  1. Read Revelation 1:1–3. What was John called to do?
  2. Think about all the light we have been given. What responsibilities rest upon us simply because we have been given so much?

Also cited: Acts 1:10, 11; Rev. 1:7; Revelation 2, 3; Rev. 14:6–12; Revelation 20; Rev. 14:14–16; Revelation 21, 22; Rev. 1:9; Rev. 22:8; Rev. 22:16; John 1:6–8; Rev. 19:10.

Notes

John's exile on Patmos seemed to end his work, but God had another purpose for him there. “In exiling John, the enemies of truth had hoped to silence forever the voice of God's faithful witness; but on Patmos the disciple received a message, the influence of which was to continue to strengthen the church till the end of time.”—The Acts of the Apostles, p. 581.

The message he received was meant to be read, heard and obeyed by those awaiting Christ's return. “Blessed is he that readeth, and they that hear the words of this prophecy, and keep those things which are written therein: for the time is at hand.” (Revelation 1:3) “Says the prophet: ‘Blessed is he that readeth’—there are those who will not read; the blessing is not for them.”—The Great Controversy, p. 341.

As we wait for Christ, we are called to study Revelation and allow its message to shape how we live.

Friday, December 11: The Prophetic Voice

“True education means more than the pursual of a certain course of study. It means more than a preparation for the life that now is. It has to do with the whole being, and with the whole period of existence possible to man. It is the harmonious development of the physical, the mental, and the spiritual powers.”—Education, p. 13.

Also cited: Rev. 14:6–12.

To discover more, visit https://whiteestate.org/absg/11.

Notes

Throughout history, God has spoken to His people when they needed correction, direction and hope. “In every generation God has sent His servants to rebuke sin, both in the world and in the church.”—The Great Controversy, p. 606.

His messages have addressed different circumstances, but His purpose has remained the same. “God's work is the same in all time, although there are different degrees of development and different manifestations of His power, to meet the wants of men in the different ages.”—Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 373. “Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets.” (Amos 3:7)

Today, we have the same responsibility to listen carefully to what God has revealed and respond with faith and obedience.
