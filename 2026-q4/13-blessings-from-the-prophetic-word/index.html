Lesson 13 · December 19, 2026

Blessings From the Prophetic Word

PDFShare

Sabbath Afternoon

Read for This Week’s Study: 1 Cor. 2:2; John 1:1–5; 2 Tim. 3:16, 17; 2 Tim. 4:1–5; Isa. 40:1–5; John 17:20–23; 1 Cor. 1:10–13.

Memory verse

Also cited: 2 Pet. 1:18, 19.

Sunday, December 20: Prophecy Exalts Christ

  1. Read 1 Corinthians 2:2 and Galatians 6:14. How does Paul see the purpose of his life and ministry—the great motivation for which he lived?
  2. Read John 1:1–5. What do these verses tell us about who Jesus is?
  3. Consider how vast the cosmos is, and to think that the One who created it, Jesus, is also the One who died for us! What great hope does that offer you?

Also cited: Rev. 12:17; Revelation 19:10; The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3, p. 182.

Notes

The purpose of prophecy is to lead us to Jesus Christ, whose glory was revealed through His coming into the world. “From the days of eternity the Lord Jesus Christ was one with the Father; He was ‘the image of God,’ the image of His greatness and majesty, ‘the outshining of His glory.’ It was to manifest this glory that He came to our world.”—The Desire of Ages, p. 19.

Paul kept Christ crucified at the centre of his preaching. “For I determined not to know any thing among you, save Jesus Christ, and him crucified.” (1 Corinthians 2:2)

John shows us who this Christ is and how He came among us. “And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.” (John 1:14)

As we study prophecy today, let us keep our eyes on Christ and the salvation He has brought us.

Monday, December 21: Prophecy Affirms the Bible

  1. Read 2 Timothy 3:16, 17. How does this passage emphasize both the authority and inspiration of Scripture?
  2. Read Luke 24:27. After His resurrection, what did Jesus use to help two despondent disciples understand His unique purpose?
  3. Why did Jesus first point the two disciples on the road to Emmaus to the Scriptures before revealing to them who He was? What should that tell us about how foundational the Word must be to us?

Also cited: Second Peter 1:21; John 1:1–3; John 7:38; Signs of the Times, March 8, 1910.

Notes

Jesus taught His followers to understand His life and mission through the Scriptures. “Beginning at Moses, the very Alpha of Bible history, Christ expounded in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself. Had He first made Himself known to them, their hearts would have been satisfied.”—The Desire of Ages, p. 796.

On the road to Emmaus, He led two discouraged disciples through what had already been written about Him. “And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.” (Luke 24:27)

Jesus also directed those who questioned Him to examine the Scriptures for themselves. “Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me.” (John 5:39)

When we open the Bible today, let us read carefully and allow its testimony to deepen our knowledge of Christ.

Tuesday, December 22: Prophecy Is God’s Truth Detector

  1. Read 2 Timothy 4:1–5. What important advice does Paul give Timothy concerning God’s Word? Why was he so adamant that Timothy be spiritually prepared for what was to come?

Also cited: Gen. 2:16, 17; Gen. 3:1–5; 2 Chron. 36:14–16; Matt. 4:1–11; Matt. 4:4; 2 Thess. 2:11, 12; John 8:32.

Notes

God has given us His Word as the standard by which we can recognise truth and reject error. “But God will have a people upon the earth to maintain the Bible, and the Bible only, as the standard of all doctrines and the basis of all reforms.”—The Great Controversy, p. 595.

Paul's instruction to Timothy shows the importance of faithfully teaching that Word, even when its message is unwelcome. “Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort with all longsuffering and doctrine.” (2 Timothy 4:2)

As we encounter different teachings today, let us examine them against Scripture rather than accepting them simply because they sound convincing.

Wednesday, December 23: Prophecy Comforts God’s People

  1. Read Matthew 11:28, 29. What does Jesus tell us to do? What does He promise all who come to Him?
  2. Read Isaiah 40:1–5. What message does God give His prophet for His hurting people? How can we apply that principle to us today?
  3. In what area of your life do you need to hear a word of comfort and hope? What does God empower us to do after He comforts us? (2 Cor. 1:3, 4).

Also cited: Genesis 1; Gen. 3:9; Gen. 3:15; Gen. 3:21; Isa. 12:1; Isa. 49:13; Isa. 51:3; 2 Tim. 3:12.

Notes

God knows the burdens His children carry and does not leave them to face their troubles alone. “He is watching over you, trembling child of God. Are you tempted? He will deliver.”—The Desire of Ages, p. 329.

Jesus invites those who are weary to find rest in Him. “Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.” (Matthew 11:28)

The same care is heard in God's message to His people through Isaiah. “Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your God.” (Isaiah 40:1)

Whatever weighs upon your heart today, remember that God sees your need and invites you to receive His comfort.

Thursday, December 24: Prophecy Unites the Body in Christ

  1. Read John 17:20–23 and 1 Corinthians 1:10–13. What core appeal is at the heart of these two passages of Scripture?
  2. At a time when the Seventh-day Adventist Church is perhaps the most diverse Protestant religious body in the world, how can we exhibit the kind of unity that helps people believe in Jesus? What forces threaten that unity?

Also cited: 1 Cor. 1:10; John 17:21; Acts 6:1; Ephesians 4:11–15; Eph. 4:13; Acts 16:31.

Notes

Jesus desires a unity among His followers that grows from love rather than pressure or personal advantage. “When men are bound together, not by force or self-interest, but by love, they show the working of an influence that is above every human influence.”—The Desire of Ages, p. 678.

Such love becomes visible when Christ changes the way His people treat one another. “By the power of His grace manifested in the transformation of character the world is to be convinced that God has sent His Son as its Redeemer.”—The Ministry of Healing, p. 470.

Jesus prayed for this unity because it would help the world recognise that the Father had sent Him. “That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us: that the world may believe that thou hast sent me.” (John 17:21)

Let us consider how our words and actions today can strengthen fellowship and make Christ's love visible to others.

Friday, December 25: Try It Again

Also cited: Ps. 34:8.

To discover more, visit https://whiteestate.org/absg/13.

Notes

Understanding the Scriptures begins with a humble heart that is ready to follow the truth it receives. “They can be understood only by those who are humbly seeking for a knowledge of the truth that they may obey it.”—Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 112.

Psalm 34 invites us to discover God's goodness through personal experience, rather than relying only on what others have said. “O taste and see that the LORD is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him.” (Psalm 34:8)

Read Ellen White's writings again for yourself, prayerfully and with a willing heart, and consider what changes as you put their counsel into practice.
