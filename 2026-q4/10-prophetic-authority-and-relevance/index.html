Lesson 10 · November 28, 2026

Prophetic Authority and Relevance

PDFShare

Sabbath Afternoon

Read for This Week’s Study: Exod. 4:10–16, Luke 8:22–25, John 3:1–10, Acts 16:25–34, 1 Cor. 5:1–5, Luke 7:28.

Memory verse

Sunday, November 29: Mediating God’s Authority

  1. Read Exodus 2:11–15 and Exodus 3:1–3, 10–13. How did Moses’ time as a shepherd prepare him to be a spokesperson for God?
  2. Read Exodus 4:10–16. What can we learn from this discussion between God and Moses about how prophets are called and how they work?
  3. You may not be a prophet, but what has God called you to do for Him during your lifetime? What is the best way to prepare yourself for this calling?

Also cited: Exod. 4:16; Exod. 7:1; Exod. 2:14; Exod. 3:14–17.

Notes

Before Moses could lead Israel, God prepared him through years of quiet service in Midian. “He was, in the providence of God, to learn patience, to temper his passions. In a school of self-denial and hardships he was to receive an education which would be of the utmost importance to him.”—Testimonies for the Church, vol. 4, p. 343.

When God called him, Moses struggled to believe that he was ready for the work entrusted to him. “These excuses at first proceeded from humility and diffidence; but after the Lord had promised to remove all difficulties, and to give him final success, then any further shrinking back and complaining of his unfitness showed distrust of God.”—Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 254.

God answered his fears with a promise of His presence and guidance. “Now therefore go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt say.” (Exodus 4:12)

Our limitations need not keep us from serving God when we trust Him to guide us in what He asks us to do.

Monday, November 30: The Authority of Jesus

  1. What was the basis of Jesus’ authority? What was the evidence of this authority? See Matt. 21:23–27 and John 17:2.
  2. What do the following verses also tell us about Jesus’ authority? Matt. 7:28, 29; Mark 1:21–27; Luke 8:22–25; Luke 9:1; John 5:25–27; and John 7:38.
  3. Even with all the evidence Jesus gave, some people still rejected Him. How can we be careful not to fall into the same trap of unbelief, given all the reasons we have to believe?

Also cited: John 1:3; Rom. 3:24; Matt. 7:29; Mark 2:10, 11; Mark 3:15; John 2:24, 25; John 10:28; John 5:19; Mark 6:7; Rev. 19:10.

Notes

Jesus spoke with an authority that was unlike anything the people had heard from their religious teachers. “The teaching of the scribes and elders was cold and formal, like a lesson learned by rote. To them the word of God possessed no vital power.”—The Desire of Ages, p. 253.

His authority was evident in His teaching and in His power over nature. “For he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.” (Matthew 7:29)

The disciples witnessed that same authority when a storm threatened their boat. “What manner of man is this! for he commandeth even the winds and water, and they obey him.” (Luke 8:25) “But He who spoke peace to the billows of Galilee has spoken the word of peace for every soul.”—The Desire of Ages, p. 336.

Whatever troubles we face today, we can come to Jesus with confidence in His power and His care for us.

Tuesday, December 1: Biblical Authority

  1. What effect did the spoken Word of God have in the following situations? See Jer. 38:1–4, John 3:1–10, and Acts 16:25–34.

Also cited: Jer. 36:22–27; 2 Kings 22:10–13; Acts 5:29; Heb. 4:12.

Notes

God's Word cannot be silenced by human opposition, and its power reaches beyond the written page. “The record of the prophecies concerning Judah and Jerusalem had been reduced to ashes; but the words were still living in the heart of Jeremiah, ‘as a burning fire,’ and the prophet was permitted to reproduce that which the wrath of man would fain have destroyed.”—Prophets and Kings, p. 436.

When Jehoiakim destroyed Jeremiah's scroll, God commanded that His message be written again. “Take thee again another roll, and write in it all the former words that were in the first roll, which Jehoiakim the king of Judah hath burned.” (Jeremiah 36:28)

The same Word that withstands opposition also works within the human heart. “While the wind is itself invisible, it produces effects that are seen and felt. So the work of the Spirit upon the soul will reveal itself in every act of him who has felt its saving power.”—The Desire of Ages, p. 173.

The change in the Philippian jailer's life showed what happens when God's message is received. “Thus the jailer's heart was united to those of his brethren, and he washed their stripes, and he and all his house were baptized that night.”—Early Writings, p. 205. “For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart.” (Hebrews 4:12)

As we read Scripture today, we should allow it to examine our hearts and bring about the changes God desires.

Wednesday, December 2: Organizational Authority

  1. What are some of the specific areas in which the prophetic gift assisted the organization and leadership of the early Christian church? Acts 6:1-7, 1 Cor. 5:1–5, 1 Cor. 7:10–16, Titus 1:5.
  2. Some people seem skeptical about “organized religion.” Whatever the challenges, what are the advantages of being part of an organized church structure? What can you give, and get, from being part of something much larger than yourself?

Also cited: Hosea 12:13; Judges 4:4–8; 2 Chronicles 20:14–17; Exodus 12:1–3; Deuteronomy 1:9–16; Mark 16:15; Acts 13:1, 2.

Notes

As the early church grew, its leaders recognised the need for an orderly way to care for its members. “Summoning a meeting of the believers, the apostles were led by the Holy Spirit to outline a plan for the better organization of all the working forces of the church.”—The Acts of the Apostles, p. 89.

The appointment of the seven in Jerusalem showed how shared responsibilities could strengthen the church's work. “The organization of the church at Jerusalem was to serve as a model for the organization of churches in every other place where messengers of truth should win converts to the gospel.”—The Acts of the Apostles, p. 91.

This concern for order continued as churches were established in other places. “For this cause left I thee in Crete, that thou shouldest set in order the things that are wanting, and ordain elders in every city, as I had appointed thee” (Titus 1:5) “Let all things be done decently and in order.” (1 Corinthians 14:40)

We honour God when we support faithful leadership and carry out our responsibilities in the church with care and humility.

Thursday, December 3: Canonical and Noncanonical Prophets

  1. Read 1 Chronicles 29:29 and 2 Chronicles 9:29. What do you notice about some of the prophets mentioned in these two passages?
  2. Read 1 Kings 11:29–39 and Luke 7:28. What do these texts teach about the authority of noncanonical prophets?
  3. Ellen G. White wrote, “The Lord desires you to study your Bibles. He has not given any additional light to take the place of His Word. This light is to bring confused minds to His Word, which, if eaten and digested, is as the lifeblood of the soul.”—Selected Messages, book 3, p. 29. How well do we as a church heed these words of hers?

Also cited: Eph. 4:12, 13; Joel 2:31; 2 Sam. 12:7–14.

Notes

The Bible refers to prophets whose writings are not included in its pages, showing that God's work through His messengers extended beyond the biblical record. “Now the acts of David the king, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of Samuel the seer, and in the book of Nathan the prophet, and in the book of Gad the seer” (1 Chronicles 29:29)

John the Baptist also held a remarkable place among God's messengers because of the work entrusted to him. “For while prophets had seen from afar Christ's advent, to John it was given to behold Him, to hear the testimony from heaven to His Messiahship, and to present Him to Israel as the Sent of God.”—The Desire of Ages, p. 220. “For I say unto you, Among those that are born of women there is not a greater prophet than John the Baptist: but he that is least in the kingdom of God is greater than he.” (Luke 7:28)

Ellen White's writings likewise direct attention to the Bible rather than taking its place. “Little heed is given to the Bible, and the Lord has given a lesser light to lead men and women to the greater light.”—Selected Messages, book 3, p. 30.

As we consider any prophetic message today, we should let it deepen our understanding of Scripture and draw us closer to Christ.

Friday, December 4: Speaking With Authority

“ ‘In these letters which I write, in the testimonies I bear, I am presenting to you that which the Lord has presented to me. I do not write one article in the paper expressing merely my own ideas. They are what God has opened before me in vision—the precious rays of light shining from the throne.’ ”—Selected Messages, book 1, p. 29.

To discover more, visit https://whiteestate.org/absg/10.

Notes

Throughout history, God has made His will known through people whom He chose to speak on His behalf. “God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son” (Hebrews 1:1–2)

The authority of a true prophetic message comes from God, who gives it, rather than from the person who delivers it. “For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost.” (2 Peter 1:21) “In ancient times God spoke to men by the mouth of prophets and apostles. In these days He speaks to them by the testimonies of His Spirit.”—Testimonies for the Church, vol. 4, p. 147.

We should receive God's messages with reverence, examine our lives in their light, and respond with willing obedience.
