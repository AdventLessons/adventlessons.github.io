Lesson 12 · December 12, 2026

End-Time Prophetic Revelations

PDFShare

Sabbath Afternoon

Read for This Week’s Study: Joel 2:28–32, Acts 2:1–21, Eph. 4:11–14, Rev. 12:17, Rev. 19:10, Acts 2:36–42.

Memory verse

Sunday, December 13: The Promise of Joel

  1. Read Joel 2:28–32. What does God promise to do in the last days?
  2. Think of all the prophecies that have come to pass as predicted. Why should this help us trust God on the ones yet to come—such as the second coming of Jesus?

Also cited: Jer. 25:1–11; Daniel 7; Matthew 24; Joel 2:28–31; Joel 2:31; Matt. 24:29; Rev. 6:12, 13; Joel 2:30, 31.

Notes

Joel's promise reaches beyond the first outpouring of the Holy Spirit to the closing work of the church. “The outpouring of the Spirit in the days of the apostles was the beginning of the early, or former, rain, and glorious was the result. To the end of time the presence of the Spirit is to abide with the true church.”—The Acts of the Apostles, p. 54.

The former rain began a work that the latter rain will bring to its fullness. “But near the close of earth's harvest, a special bestowal of spiritual grace is promised to prepare the church for the coming of the Son of man.”—The Acts of the Apostles, p. 55.

God's promise also reaches every person who turns to Him for deliverance. “And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the LORD shall be delivered” (Joel 2:32)

As we look towards Christ's return, we should seek the Spirit's presence in our lives and trust God's promise of salvation.

Monday, December 14: Pentecost

  1. Read Acts 2:1–21. Why did Peter quote Joel in the context of Christ’s first coming? What role does prophecy play in this passage?
  2. Upon seeing these supernatural manifestations, the crowd initially thought it to be some kind of natural event. In this case, they thought that the men were drunk! (Drunk men suddenly speaking in foreign languages?) However irrational their response, what is it about people that causes them, even in the face of the supernatural, to try to find a natural explanation?

Also cited: Mark 15:33; Joel 2:23; Acts 2; Acts 2:37; Acts 2:38, 39; Matt. 3:2; Joel 2.

Notes

At Pentecost, the Holy Spirit gave the disciples power to proclaim Christ to people from many nations. “The appearance of fire signified the fervent zeal with which the apostles would labor and the power that would attend their work.”—The Acts of the Apostles, p. 39.

The gift of languages enabled the disciples to speak to people who would otherwise have struggled to understand them. “And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.” (Acts 2:4)

We too need the Holy Spirit to make our witness clear, faithful and effective, even when others misunderstand or dismiss the message.

Tuesday, December 15: Gifts of the Spirit

  1. Read Ephesians 4:11–14. Why does God give these gifts to His church? How does the gift of prophecy help accomplish God’s plans for the church?

Also cited: Romans 12:6–8; 1 Corinthians 12:8–10; 1 Corinthians 12:28; 1 Kings 19:16, 19–21; Isaiah 6; Daniel 2; Dan. 10:8–10, 18, 19; Acts 21:10–12; Ezekiel 4; Revelation 2, 3; Jer. 36:1–4; Isa. 8:20; Matt. 7:15–20; Matt. 24:24; 2 Pet. 2:1.

Notes

Christ gave different gifts to His church so that believers could serve together and grow in faith. “When Christ ascended to heaven, He left the work on earth in the hands of His servants, the undershepherds.”—Testimonies for the Church, vol. 5, p. 237.

These gifts were given for the good of the whole church, rather than for the honour of those who received them. “For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ” (Ephesians 4:12)

We should value the gifts Christ has given His church and use our own abilities to strengthen others in faith.

Wednesday, December 16: The End-Time Remnant

  1. Read Revelation 12:17. What is the “testimony of Jesus”? Compare Revelation 19:10 with Revelation 22:8, 9.
  2. What evidence have you seen to show that the ministry of Ellen G. White can be justly identified as an example of the working of “the spirit of prophecy” (Rev. 19:10)?

Also cited: Revelation 12; Revelation 22:9.

Notes

Revelation describes a final work of great power as God's people carry His message to the world. “The angel who unites in the proclamation of the third angel's message is to lighten the whole earth with his glory. A work of world-wide extent and unwonted power is here foretold.”—The Great Controversy, p. 611.

This work takes place amid opposition to those who remain faithful to God. “And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ.” (Revelation 12:17)

Revelation also explains the connection between the testimony of Jesus and the gift of prophecy. “And I fell at his feet to worship him. And he said unto me, See thou do it not: I am thy fellowservant, and of thy brethren that have the testimony of Jesus: worship God: for the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy.” (Revelation 19:10)

Our concern today should be to remain faithful to God's commandments, receive the testimony of Jesus and share His message with others.

Thursday, December 17: Modern Manifestations

  1. Read Acts 2:36–42. What was the core message of Peter’s sermon at Pentecost? How did Peter’s audience react?
  2. How much have you taken advantage of the wonderful gift we have been given through the ministry of Ellen G. White?

Also cited: Joel 2:28–31; The Great Controversy, p. 611.

Notes

Peter's sermon at Pentecost shows what happens when the Holy Spirit brings conviction to those who hear the gospel. “The power that accompanied the words of the speaker convinced them that Jesus was indeed the Messiah.”—The Acts of the Apostles, p. 43.

The same Spirit who worked through the apostles will empower God's servants in the closing work. “Servants of God, with their faces lighted up and shining with holy consecration, will hasten from place to place to proclaim the message from heaven.”—The Great Controversy, p. 612.

Yet the message calls for more than conviction; it calls for a personal response. “Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost.” (Acts 2:38)

As we consider the Spirit's work in the last days, we must also respond to His call in our own lives.

Friday, December 18: All About Mission

“Seventh-day Adventists should be foremost in uplifting Christ before the world. The proclamation of the third angel’s message calls for the presentation of the Sabbath truth. This truth, with others included in the message, is to be proclaimed; but the great center of attraction, Christ Jesus, must not be left out. It is at the cross of Christ that mercy and truth meet together, and righteousness and peace kiss each other. The sinner must be led to look to Calvary; with the simple faith of a little child he must trust in the merits of the Savior, accepting His righteousness, believing in His mercy.”—Gospel Workers, pp. 156, 157.

“The Lord requires that far greater personal effort shall be put forth by the members of our churches.”—Evangelism, p. 113.

“In the Lord’s work there are to be no idlers. Let different ones unite in labor as fishers of men. Let them seek to gather souls from the corruption of the world into the saving purity of Christ’s love.”—Evangelism, p. 115.

Also cited: Ephesians 4; Rev. 12:17; Isaiah 30:21.

To discover more, visit https://whiteestate.org/absg/12.

Notes

The gifts of the Spirit have a purpose beyond the growth of individual believers: they serve the mission Christ entrusted to His church. “The church is God's appointed agency for the salvation of men. It was organized for service, and its mission is to carry the gospel to the world.”—The Acts of the Apostles, p. 9.

That mission comes from Christ Himself, and His message must remain at its centre. “And he said unto them, Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature.” (Mark 16:15)

Whatever gifts God has entrusted to us, we should use them to help others know Christ and receive the salvation He offers.
