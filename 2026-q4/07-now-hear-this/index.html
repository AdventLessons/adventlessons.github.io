Lesson 7 · November 7, 2026

Now Hear This!

This lesson PDF Whole quarter PDF · 2 lessonsShare on WhatsApp

God sends His word in love, yet people often resist what they need to hear. Their stories call us to listen, believe and obey today.

Memory verses

Study notes

Read the study notes

God Speaks Because He Loves

God's warnings are expressions of His love. When He sent Jeremiah to Judah, His desire was that the people would hear, turn from their evil ways and receive forgiveness. He sent messengers repeatedly because He had compassion on His people. The same loving purpose remains today. God does not delight in anyone perishing. His word calls us away from sin because He wants to save us. Even when His messages were refused, "His mercy had continued its pleadings" (The Great Controversy, p. 19; Prophets and Kings, p. 435). [Jeremiah 36:1–3; 2 Chronicles 36:15; 2 Peter 3:9]

When Fear Becomes Rebellion

At Kadesh, Israel heard the fearful report of the spies and turned against Moses and Aaron. The people wanted another leader to take them back to Egypt. Joshua and Caleb pleaded with them not to rebel against the Lord, but they refused to listen. God saw beneath their anger: they did not believe Him. Their later tears did not produce obedience. Fear had become rebellion (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 389), and regret was not repentance: they "mourned for the judgment; their sorrow was not repentance" (Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 392). [Numbers 13:26–33; 14:1–11, 39–45; Hebrews 3:7–19]

The Word Cannot Be Silenced

King Jehoiakim listened as Jeremiah's scroll was read, then cut it with a penknife and threw it into the fire (Prophets and Kings, p. 433). Neither the king nor his servants were afraid or showed any sign of repentance. Yet burning the scroll could not cancel God's warning: "The burning of the roll was not the end of the matter" (Prophets and Kings, p. 436). The Lord commanded Jeremiah to write it again, and many like words were added. We may reject a message, but our rejection does not make it untrue or remove our responsibility to respond. [Jeremiah 36:20–32]

The Danger of Refusing

God's people did not reject His messengers only once. Again and again, they refused correction, mocked His prophets and preferred comforting words. Ahab hated Micaiah because he spoke unwelcome truth (Prophets and Kings, p. 195). Later, Jesus exposed the contradiction of honouring dead prophets while rejecting the One God had sent, and "many follow in the track of the Pharisees" (The Desire of Ages, p. 618). Even those who enjoy hearing Scripture may refuse to obey it. When we love darkness more than light, our hearts become increasingly resistant to correction. [2 Chronicles 36:12–16; 1 Kings 22:8; Isaiah 30:9–11; Matthew 23:29–37; Ezekiel 33:31–32; John 3:19; Proverbs 29:1]

Written for Our Admonition

These accounts were preserved so that we might learn from those who came before us. Paul warns those who think they stand to take heed lest they fall. "The history of ancient Israel is written for our benefit" (Testimonies for the Church, Vol. 4, p. 162). Their failures invite us to examine our own hearts rather than condemn theirs. The call is personal and urgent: "To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts." God asks us to receive His word with faith and answer it with obedience. As Jehoshaphat told Judah, "believe his prophets, so shall ye prosper" (Prophets and Kings, p. 201). [1 Corinthians 10:6–12; Romans 15:4; Hebrews 3:15; 2 Chronicles 20:20]

For discussion

  1. 1.Fear and the messenger. At Kadesh, how did the people's fear become anger against Moses and Aaron? Why did God call their response unbelief, and how might we react similarly when someone brings us a difficult truth from His word? [; ]
  2. 2.Cutting away the message. Jehoiakim cut and burned the scroll, but God's word was written again. In what ways might we reject, avoid or explain away a Bible teaching that challenges our choices? What does the king's example teach us about trying to silence God's word? []
  3. 3.Honouring yesterday, resisting today. Jesus rebuked those who honoured the graves of the prophets while rejecting God's word in their own day. How can we admire the faithfulness of people in the Bible yet resist the same call to faithfulness in our lives? []
  4. 4.Hearing without obeying. Ezekiel's listeners enjoyed hearing his words but did not put them into practice. What is the difference between appreciating a Bible message and obeying it? How can we tell whether God's word is changing the way we live? [; ]
  5. 5.The warning for today. Paul says these accounts were written for our admonition, and Hebrews urges us not to harden our hearts today. What warning from this week's study speaks most directly to you, and what can you do about it before the week ends? [; ]
