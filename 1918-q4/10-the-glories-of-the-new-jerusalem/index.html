Lesson 10 · December 7, 1918

The Glories of the New Jerusalem

PDFShare

Lesson Scripture: Rev. 21:14-27.

Questions

  1. 1.How is the wall of the holy city described? Rev. 21:14. Note 1.
  2. 2.What is said of the shape and dimensions of the city? Verses 15, 16. Note 2.
  3. 3.What was the height of the wall? Verses 16, 17. Note 3.
  4. 4.Of what material was the wall above the foundation composed? What is said of the city? Verse 18.
  5. 5.How were the foundations adorned? Verses 19, 20. Note 4.
  6. 6.Of what were the twelve gates composed? What is said of the street of the city? Verse 21.
  7. 7.What building was not seen in the city? Why is no temple needed? Verse 22.
  8. 8.Why does not the city need the light of the sun or the moon? Verse 23. Compare 1 John 1:5.
  9. 9.How is the whole world affected by the light and glory of the city of God? Rev. 21:24.
  10. 10.Why will there be no need of shutting the gates of the city? Verse 25.
  11. 11.What will be the relation of the nations of the saved to the holy city? Verse 26.
  12. 12.What will not be found there? Verse 27, first clause.
  13. 13.Who only will gain an entrance to the heavenly city? Verse 27, last clause.

Notes

1. "The names of the twelve apostles in the foundations of the city, show it to be a Christian and not a Jewish city; while the names of the twelve tribes on the gates, show that all the saved, from this dispensation as well as from the former, are reckoned as belonging to some one of the twelve tribes; for all must enter the city through some one of these twelve gates. It is this fact which explains those instances in which Christians are called Israel, and are addressed as the twelve tribes."—"Daniel and the Revelation" on Rev. 21:14.

2. "Twelve thousand furlongs" would equal one thousand five hundred miles. It has been generally supposed that this is the whole circumference of the city; but some eminent modern commentators believe that the language conveys the idea that this is the measurement of one side only. If this be correct, the area of the city would be two and one fourth million square miles, or more than two thirds as large as the United States. As one modern writer has expressed it, "The dimensions of the city, like its shape and everything belonging to it, are beyond the compass of our experience."

3. "The measure of a man, that is, of the angel," meaning, no doubt, that the measure used by the angel is the same as that used by man.

4. For a description of these precious stones, read "Daniel and the Revelation" on Revelation 21.
