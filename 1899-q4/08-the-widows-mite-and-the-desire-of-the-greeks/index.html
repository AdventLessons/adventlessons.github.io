Lesson 8 · November 25, 1899

The Widow's Mite, and the Desire of the Greeks

PDFShare

In the Temple at Jerusalem, A. D. 31.

(Mark 12:41-44; Luke 21:1-4; John 12:20-36.)

  1. 1.Having exposed the hypocrisy of the Pharisees and denounced their sins, where did the Lord next go? What did He do there? Mark 12:41. See note 1. Side texts: 2 Kings 12:9.
  2. 2.Who came with an offering? Of what did it consist? Verse 42. See note 2. Side texts: Brass money; see Matt. 10:9.
  3. 3.Whose attention did He call to the woman's act, and what did He say of it? Verse 43. Side texts: 2 Cor. 8:12.
  4. 4.Why was her act so highly commended? Verse 44.
  5. 5.Among the worshipers at this Passover, who are specially mentioned as being present? John 12:20. Side texts: Acts 17:4.
  6. 6.In their anxiety to know more of Jesus, to whom did these Greeks apply? What did they ask? Verse 21.
  7. 7.What did Philip do? Verse 22.
  8. 8.Without giving a direct answer, what did Jesus say? Verse 23. Side texts: John 13:31, 32.
  9. 9.How did Jesus then explain the necessity for His death? Verse 24. Side texts: 1 Cor. 15:36-38.
  10. 10.What did He say about the lives of others? Verse 25. Side texts: Matt. 10:39.
  11. 11.Then, what must those do who would follow Jesus? Verse 26. See note 3. Side texts: John 14:1-3; 1 Thess. 4:17.
  12. 12.As Jesus realized that the time of His death was near, by what words did He show that His flesh shrank from the ordeal? Verse 27. Side texts: Luke
  13. 13.As evidence of His spirit of submission, what expression came forth from His lips? What response was heard from heaven? Verse 28.
  14. 14.What did the people recognize in this voice of God? Verse 29.
  15. 15.For what purpose did God thus speak to Jesus? Verse 30. Side texts: John 11:42.
  16. 16.What did He then say of Satan? Verse 31. Side texts: John 14:30; Matt. 12:29.
  17. 17.What did Jesus say His death would do? Verses 32, 33. See note 4.
  18. 18.What question did this statement raise among the people? Verse 34. Side texts: Ps. 89:36.
  19. 19.In reply to this question what important truths did Jesus utter? Verses 35, 36. Side texts: John 8:12; 1 John 1:5-7.

Notes

Reading.

"Desire of Ages," chapters 67, 68; "Spirit of Prophecy," vol. 3, chapters 4 and 5.

1. The woes had been pronounced in the outer court of the temple. The Lord, having done His duty in this line, calmly retired to the court of the women, the innermost point to which they were admitted, and there sat down opposite the treasury. This consisted of thirteen large chests, with openings shaped like the bell of a trumpet, to receive the free-will offerings of the people.

2. These offerings were for the priests and poor people. It made no difference to this woman in the warmth of her loving zeal, that she was poorer than some for whom a portion of the money was intended. Besides, she did not positively know just what disposition the priests would make of her offering. She simply answered a call of duty, and left results with Him who seeth and knoweth all things.

3. Jesus was to die, so that He might, like the planted wheat, bring forth a product like Himself. If these Greeks would see Him, and be like Him, let them bear in mind His work, and follow Him in it, and then they would have assurance of eternal life; otherwise they would abide alone in their knowledge of Him. This lesson is for all to-day.

4. Although Satan was compassing the death of Christ in order to destroy the "Seed of Abraham," and so make God's Word of no effect (Rom. 9:6), yet the death of the "Seed" would prove not only its restoration, but the multiplication of it. Gal. 3:29. Like the kernel of wheat, if He die, instead of abiding alone, He would cause the "seed of Abraham" to abound everywhere, by drawing all to Himself.
