Lesson 9 · November 27, 1948

Gideon and Other Heroes of Faith

PDFShare

Lesson Scripture: Heb. 11:32-40.

Memory verse

Study Help: M. L. Andreasen, "The Book of Hebrews," pages 503-508.

The Lesson

  1. 1.Because of Israel's sin, what did God permit the Midianites and Amalekites to do? Judges 6:1-6.
  2. 2.Which name is first mentioned in Hebrews 11:32? Who came to him as he threshed wheat? What excuses did he make when the Lord told him to save Israel from the Midianites? Judges 6:11-16.
  3. 3.When the Midianites and Amalekites came against Israel, whom did Gideon call together? Relate the story of the fleece. Judges 6:33-40.

Notes

"Gideon dared not place himself at the head of the army without still further evidence that God had called him to this work, and that He would be with him. He prayed, 'If Thou wilt save Israel by mine hand, as Thou hast said, behold, I will put a fleece of wool in the floor; and if the dew be on the fleece only, and it be dry upon all the earth beside, then shall I know that Thou wilt save Israel by mine hand, as Thou hast said.' In the morning the fleece was wet, while the ground was dry. But now a doubt arose, since wool naturally absorbs moisture when there is any in the air; the test might not be decisive. Hence he asked that the sign be reversed, pleading that his extreme caution might not displease the Lord. His request was granted."—Patriarchs and Prophets, page 548.

  1. 4.What special experience did Gideon have which revealed his faith as well as God's power? Judges 7:2-8.

Notes

Those "that lapped, putting their hand to their mouth," instead of kneeling when they drank, were more alert than the others, keeping their eyes upon the enemy and in constant readiness for action. God could do more with three hundred alert men than with thirty-two, thousand fearful, timid, and unwatchful men.

  1. 5.How was the victory of Gideon brought about? Judges 7:19-23.
  2. 6.What is the name of the second person mentioned in Hebrews 11:32? What message did Deborah send to him? What answer did he receive to his request? Judges 4:4-9.

Notes

The result of Barak's campaign was complete victory: "There was not a man left" of Sisera's army. This story seems to be recorded to show Barak's complete faith in Deborah as a prophetess of God.

  1. 7.Which is the third name recorded in Hebrews 11:32 who "obtained a good report through faith"? Heb. 11:39.

Notes

If Samson's name were not here recorded, few would think of including him in this list. But how much more glorious might the life of Samson have been had he conformed to the plan of God!

"God's promise that through Samson He would 'begin to deliver Israel out of the hand of the Philistines,' was fulfilled; but how dark and terrible the record of that life which might have been a praise to God and a glory to the nation! Had Samson been true to his divine calling, the purpose of God could have been accomplished in his honor and exaltation. But he yielded to temptation, and proved untrue to his trust, and his mission was fulfilled in defeat, bondage, and death."—Patriarchs and Prophets, page 567.

  1. 8.What three names come next in the list of heroes of faith? Heb. 11:32.

Notes

Jephthah was "a mighty man of valor" who because of his birth was disinherited and thrust out. Judges 11:1, 2. When Gilead was in danger from enemies, the elders of Gilead appealed to Jephthah for help, and the very one whom they had hated and expelled became their savior. Verses 7, 23, 33. In this attitude he showed a magnanimous spirit, and he is enrolled among the heroes. David and Samuel are also in the list; but, as the writer says, time would fail to tell of all.

  1. 9.What was done through faith? Heb. 11;33.

Notes

Gideon and Barak, among many others subdued kingdoms. David obtained promises. 2 Samuel 7:12-17. In Daniel's case the mouths of lions were stopped. Daniel 6.

  1. 10.What other things were done through faith? Heb. 11:34.

Notes

Daniel records the story of the three young men who escaped the violence of fire. Daniel 3. Among many others, David escaped the sword. 1 Samuel 20. Samson put armies to flight, and so did Jonathan. Judges 15:8, 15; 1 Samuel 14:13, 14.

  1. 11.How does the writer continue the account? Heb. 11:35.

Notes

The widow of Zarephath received her son again, 1 Kings 17:17-24. So did the Shunammite. 2 Kings 4:32-37. We have no record in the Old Testament of any being tortured, or broken on the wheel, or beaten to death, as it is variously translated, though many suffered, which may be the meaning here.

  1. 12.What more is added to the suffering of God's saints? Heb. 11:37.

Notes

Naboth was cruelly stoned. 1 Kings 21:13. So was Zechariah. 2 Chronicles 24:20, 21; Matthew 23:35.

  1. 13.What is said of these heroes? Where did some of them find refuge? Heb. 11:38.
  2. 14.Though these all received a good report, what did they not obtain? Heb. 11:39.
  3. 15.What has God provided for us? Heb. 11:40.

Notes

"They without us should not be made perfect." God lets them wait for us that the work may be finished all at once and all go home to glory together. This statement confirms belief in a judgment to come and refutes the idea that men receive their reward at death. Those who are sleeping in Christ will not without us be made perfect.
