Lesson 11 · July 3, 1886

Under the Law

PDFShare

[Note to the Student.—Do not consider the lesson learned until you can give at least the substance of every text, with the correct reference for each. The references in black letters indicate those texts that should be committed to memory. A little diligent application each day will enable you to learn them, although this need not be considered a test of scholarship.]

  1. 1.What exhortation does Paul give in Rom. 6:12?
  2. 2.What is sin? 1 John 3:4.
  3. 3.Then what does the apostle really warn us not to do?
  4. 4.Where is this exhortation repeated? Rom. 6:13, first part.
  5. 5.If people heed this exhortation, what will have no dominion over them? Rom. 6:14, first part.
  6. 6.If a person sins, what does he transgress?
  7. 7.Then if sin has no dominion over him, what does he keep?
  8. 8.What does Paul say to those who thus refrain from sinning? Rom. 6:14, last part.
  9. 9.Will those sin who are not under the law but under grace? Rom. 6:15.
  10. 10.Speaking to those who are not under the law, to what does Paul say they had formerly been subject? Rom. 6:17.
  11. 11.But now, being not under the law, from what are they free? Rom. 6:18.
  12. 12.Then what must be meant by being "under the law"?
  13. 13.What do those do who are free from sin, or who, in other words, are not under the law? Rom. 6:18, last part.
  14. 14.And what is righteousness? Ps. 119:172.
  15. 15.Then if people are not under the law, what do they do?
  16. 16.Under what must they then necessarily be? Rom. 6:14, last part.
  17. 17.Why? John 15:4, 5; Heb. 4:14-16.

Notes

"Sin is the transgression of the law;" therefore when Paul warns us against letting sin reign over us, he warns us against transgressing the law. And since when a person sins, he transgresses the law, it follows that when sin has no dominion over him, he obeys the law.

In speaking to those who are not under the law, Paul says that they had formerly been the servants of sin (Rom. 6:17), but that now they are "made free from sin." Now if when they are not under the law, they are free from sin, it necessarily follows that when they were under the law, they were servants of sin. "Under the law," therefore, is equivalent to being in a state of sin. And so if people are not under the law, they keep the commandments. But the commandments can be kept only by the help of Christ; and this help is given by the grace of God (Heb. 4:16); therefore those who are not under the law are under grace.
