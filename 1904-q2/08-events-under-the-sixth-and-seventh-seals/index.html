Lesson 8 · May 21, 1904

Events under the Sixth and Seventh Seals

Questions.

  1. 1.What great event follows the falling of the stars, as witnessed by the prophet under the sixth seal? . Compare , last part.
  2. 2.How will that day come upon the unprepared? .
  3. 3.In view of the stupendous issue before this generation, what message does God send? See ; .
  4. 4.What will be the condition among the nations while this message is being carried? ; .
  5. 5.How are the nations restrained from utterly destroying one another? . Note 1.
  6. 6.For what special purpose are the winds of strife restrained? .
  7. 7.What is the sign or seal of the living God? ; . Note 2.
  8. 8.What follows the closing of the sealing message under the sixth seal? ; . Note 3.
  9. 9.What scene of triumph did John then behold? .
  10. 10.What question was raised and answered for the benefit of those who meet temptation and trial? .
  11. 11.What glorious picture is set before us who must serve a little longer here? .
  12. 12.What special incentive to service have we in present-day conditions?—While gusty winds of strife blow here and there, still God in mercy restrains the coming universal strife in order that the sealing message of Sabbath and Advent reform may be carried by us to all the world. Work not done in times of peace must be done in times of war and hardship. Now, just now, is the time to work.

Notes

Notes.

1. Winds are used as symbolic of war and strife. ; .

2. Creative power is the distinguishing feature of the living God. . See for proof that sign and seal have the same meaning.

3. The silence in heaven at the opening of the seventh seal shows that this seal covers the actual appearing of Christ in the clouds of heaven as He comes with all the angels to gather His saints. Ministry in heaven ceases, and the scene of activity among the heavenly beings is transferred to the earth for a little time, until the hosts of angels and the redeemed, led by their Captain and Saviour, return to the city of God. Half an hour, prophetic time, would be about seven days. In a description of this scene in we are told: "We all entered the cloud together, and were seven days ascending to the sea of glass."—P. 12.
