Lesson 7 · February 17, 1900

The Betrayal

PDFShare
Side lights "Desire of Ages," chapter 74; "Spirit of Prophecy," vol. 3, chapter 7.

In Gethsemane, A. D. 31.

(Matt. 26:48-56; Mark 14:44-53; Luke 22:47-54; John 18:2-12.)

  1. 1.Before entering the garden where Jesus was, what sign had Judas given his companions, by which they might recognize Christ? Mark 14:44.
  2. 2.Who were those in company with Judas? What did they carry with them? John 18:3. Side texts: Acts 1:16.
  3. 3.Knowing their object, what did Jesus do? Verse 4.
  4. 4.What then transpired? Verses 5-7.
  5. 5.What final reply did Jesus make? Verse 8. See note 1.
  6. 6.What attempt did Judas then make? Luke 22:47. Side texts: Mark 14:45.
  7. 7.With what words did Jesus meet him? Verse 48. Side texts: Matt. 26:50.
  8. 8.Seeing the situation to be critical, what question did the disciples ask? Verse 49.
  9. 9.How did Peter answer the question? John 18:10.
  10. 10.What reproof did Jesus then administer to Peter? Verse 11. Side texts: Matt. 26:52, 53.
  11. 11.How did Jesus repair the mistake Peter had made? Luke 22:51.
  12. 12.What question did Jesus then ask the throng of armed men? Verse 52.
  13. 13.What was the next step taken by the company of officers? John 18:12.
  14. 14.At this what did the disciples do? Mark 14:50.
  15. 15.To what place was Jesus taken? Verse 53. See note 2. Side texts: John 18:13.

Reading

"Desire of Ages," chapter 74; "Spirit of Prophecy," vol. 3, chapter 7.

  1. It is a remarkable thing that, in the midst of all His danger, Jesus did not for a moment forget to care for His disciples, and so the only favor asked of His captors was that His disciples might retain their liberty.
  1. Annas had been appointed high priest in A. D. 7, by the Emperor Quirinius, but because of his constant intrigue and unwearied plottings, was deprived of his office in A. D. 14, by Valerius. He, however, had the privilege of seeing his five sons successively raised to the same office. Caiaphas, being son-in-law to Annas, gave way to the influence of the latter to such an extent that the father-in-law really ruled matters in the office of high priest.
