Lesson 11 · March 17, 1900

Crowned and Crucified

PDFShare
Side lights "Desire of Ages," chapters 77, 78; "Spirit of Prophecy," vol. 3, chapter 10.

Jerusalem and Calvary, A. D. 31.

(Matt. 27:27-43; Mark 15:16-32; Luke 23:24-38; John 19:17-19.)

  1. 1.Pilate having delivered Jesus to the soldiers, to what place did they take Him? Matt. 27:27. See note 1. Side texts: Mark 15:16; Acts 23:35.
  2. 2.What was the first act of the soldiers, after escorting Jesus thither? Verse 28.
  3. 3.For what purpose was this done? Verse 29. Side texts: Isa. 53:3.
  4. 4.How did they further show contempt for Him? Verse 30. Side texts: Isa. 50:6, 7; Mark 15:19.
  5. 5.Having thus satisfied their spite, what did they do with Jesus? Verse 31. Side texts: Isa. 53:7, 8.
  6. 6.On the journey to Calvary, what incident transpired? Verse 32. See note 2. Side texts: Mark 15:21.
  7. 7.What other companions in affliction were led along with Jesus to crucifixion? Luke 23:32. Side texts: Mark 15:27.
  8. 8.Who went as sympathizers with the suffering One? Verse 27. Side texts: Luke 23:55; John 19:25.
  9. 9.What did Jesus say of their weeping? Verses 28-31. Side texts: Luke 19:41-44; Isa. 2:19; Rev. 6:14-17.
  10. 10.Upon arriving at Calvary, what was offered to Jesus? Matt. 27:34. See note 3. Side texts: Ps. 69:21.
  11. 11.Being raised upon the cross, what prayer did Jesus offer for His tormentors? Luke 23:34.
  12. 12.Upon beholding Jesus' manifest agony on the cross, what did the rulers and many people say? Verse 35. Side texts: Mark 15:29-32.
  13. 13.What mocking words of the soldiers were added? Verses 36, 37.
  14. 14.What writing was placed above the head of Jesus on the cross? Verse 38. Side texts: John 19:19.

Reading

"Desire of Ages," chapters 77, 78; "Spirit of Prophecy," vol. 3, chapter 10.

  1. The "common hall," or public prætorium, was the headquarters of the Roman military governor, and was located, in time of peace, in some one of the best buildings of the city, as decided by the choice of the governor.
  1. Cyrene was in North Africa, a province of Libya. That part is now known as Tunis. A large colony of Jews had settled there centuries before, and there were large numbers there still. Simon, though a Jew, was recognized as a foreigner on whom they could shift the burden of the exhausted One, whose slow motions were delaying the progress of the impatient guards.
  1. This was a bitter, stupefying drug of myrrh (Mark 15:23), but named gall (Matt. 27:34), because of its bitterness. Jesus, however, refused that which would in any way becloud His faculties, even though it might relieve His excessive pain.
