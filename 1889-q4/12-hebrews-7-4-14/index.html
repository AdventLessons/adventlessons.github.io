Lesson 12 · December 21, 1889

Hebrews 7:4-14

PDFShare
  1. 1.Unto whom was Melchizedek like in his priesthood?
  2. 2.Will Christ have any successor in his priestly office? See Heb. 5:6.
  3. 3.How was the greatness of Melchizedek proved? Heb. 7:4.
  4. 4.Who took tithes in the Mosaic dispensation? Verse 5.
  5. 5.From whom did the priests descend?—Ib.
  6. 6.Who confers a blessing, the less or the greater? Verse 7.
  7. 7.Who conferred the blessing according to Gen. 14:19, 20?
  8. 8.Who then was accounted the greater? See note.
  9. 9.What is the intention of the entire argument in Heb. 7:4-10? Ans. To magnify the order of priesthood after which Christ was called.
  10. 10.If perfection had been by the Levitical priesthood, would any other order have been required? Verse 11.
  11. 11.When was the change made from the Aaronic to the Melchizedek priesthood? Verse 12. See Col. 2:14; also note on chap. 6:20, on the beginning of the priesthood of Christ.
  12. 12.What else had to be changed on the change of the priesthood? Heb. 7:12.
  13. 13.Does the law of ten commandments say anything about the priesthood?
  14. 14.Would the ten commandments, or any one of them, need to be changed on the change of the priesthood?
  15. 15.Was there any law concerning the priesthood? Ex. 28:1; 40:13.
  16. 16.What would have been the consequence if any but the sons of Aaron had tried to act as priest? Num. 3:10.
  17. 17.Did anyone not a Levite try to act as priest? 2 Chron. 26:1, 16-18.
  18. 18.What was the result of his presumption? Verses 19, 20.
  19. 19.Of what tribe was king Uzziah?
  20. 20.Of what tribe was our Lord Jesus Christ? Heb. 7:14.
  21. 21.If the priesthood was so strictly given to Aaron, how could Christ be accepted as priest? Verse 12.
  22. 22.If that law (Num. 3:10, etc.) confining the priesthood to Aaron and his sons, had continued in force, could Christ have acted as priest?
  23. 23.What law, then, had to be changed in order to have a change of priesthood?
  24. 24.Was there ever a priest before Christ of the tribe of Judah? Heb. 7:13, 14.

Notes

We are led, by all the Scriptures, to believe that Melchizedek was greater than Abraham only in respect to his position or office as priest-king. Abraham was not a king; he was only a stranger and pilgrim in the land. The priesthood of Melchizedek and of Aaron were both typical of that of Christ; but as a type the former stood highest, because Aaron had no kingship connected with his priesthood. It is this fact that gives the priesthood of Melchizedek superiority over that of Aaron. Inasmuch as Aaron had no kingship, were it not for Melchizedek we should have but a limited and very imperfect type of the priesthood of Christ. In other respects, Abraham doubtless was not inferior to Melchizedek.
