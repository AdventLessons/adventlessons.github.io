Lesson 6 · March 12, 1887

They Are God's Messengers to Execute His Will

PDFShare
  1. 1.Through whom has God often made revelations to his people? Heb. 2:2.
  2. 2.Explain how the things contained in the book of Revelation were revealed from God the Father to the church. Rev. 1:1.
  3. 3.What is said on this point in chap. 22:6, 16?
  4. 4.Who explained Daniel's visions to him? Dan. 8:16; 9:21-23.
  5. 5.Through whom did Moses receive his instructions from the Lord? Acts 7:38.
  6. 6.Mention other cases showing that it is through angels that God's will is revealed to his people.
  7. 7.Through whom does God execute his judgments upon the wicked? Rev. 15:1; 16:1.
  8. 8.Relate a circumstance illustrating this fact. Gen. 19:1-12.
  9. 9.By whom and for what were these angels sent? Verse 13.
  10. 10.When God was angry with Balaam, whom did he send to punish him? Num. 22:22, 23.
  11. 11.Tell all about this interesting event. Num. 22:22-35.
  12. 12.Who once sent a letter to Hezekiah, reproaching God and his people? 2 Kings 19:4.
  13. 13.What did Hezekiah do with the letter? Verse 14.
  14. 14.What did Hezekiah ask God to do? Verse 16.
  15. 15.How did the Lord answer him? Verses 20, 32-34.
  16. 16.How was this promise fulfilled? Verse 35.
  17. 17.When David had sinned in numbering Israel, how did God punish him? 1 Chron. 21:14.
  18. 18.Whom did the Lord send to execute this punishment? Verse 15.
  19. 19.Relate what followed. Verses 16-20, 27.
  20. 20.How was the wrath of God at one time brought upon king Herod? Acts 12:21, 22.
  21. 21.How was he punished for this? Verse 23.
  22. 22.Then, through whom does God execute judgments upon the ungodly?

Notes

How revelations are given.—Rev. 1:1 furnishes a good illustration of how God gives revelations to his church. 1. God gives the revelation to Christ. 2. Christ gives it to an angel. 3. The angel gives it to the prophet. 4. The prophet gives it to the church; and the church gives it to the world. Probably all of the prophecies and much of the Bible were given in this manner.

"The revelation in the Old Testament was indeed given by Jehovah, but it was the common opinion of the Hebrews that it was by the ministry of angels. As Paul was discoursing here of the superiority of the Redeemer to the angels, it was to the point to refer to the fact that the law had been given by the ministry of angels."—Barnes, on Heb. 2:2.

The angel of the Lord smote him.—"Men of every rank are mortal. Even kings, 'those earthly gods,' as Watts calls them, however they may be flattered, must die; and when they have dared to persecute the church of God, they have been often cut off with marks of divine displeasure. Scripture affords many instances of this, and modern history is not without them. But Jesus lives forever; and while princes die and their works and decrees perish, the word of God must grow and prosper."—Cottage Bible, on Acts 12:23.
