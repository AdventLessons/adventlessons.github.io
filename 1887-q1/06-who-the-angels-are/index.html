Lesson 1 · February 5, 1887

Who the Angels Are

PDFShare
  1. 1.Besides God and men, what other intelligent beings are spoken of in the Bible? Luke 15:10.
  2. 2.Is much said about them in the Holy Scriptures?—They are mentioned in every book in the Bible, and in some of them many times.
  3. 3.Who were present when God laid the foundation of the earth? Job 38:4-7.
  4. 4.Had men been created at that time? Gen. 2:4, 5.
  5. 5.Who, then, must these "sons of God" have been?
  6. 6.What is the meaning of the word "angel"?—A messenger.
  7. 7.As soon as man had sinned, who was sent to guard the tree of life? Gen. 3:24.
  8. 8.Who are the cherubim?—A high order of angels who dwell near the throne of God. Ps. 80:1; Eze. 10:20; 11:22.
  9. 9.As these sons of God and also the cherubim are both mentioned before any man had died, could they have been the spirits of dead men?
  10. 10.Can dead men come back to converse with the living? Luke 16:27-31.
  11. 11.Do the dead know anything about matters on the earth? Eccl. 9:5, 6.
  12. 12.How does Job state the same truth? Job 14:10, 21.
  13. 13.Are men and angels of the same nature? Heb. 2:16.
  14. 14.How is man compared to the angels? Ps. 8:4, 5.
  15. 15.Does not Jesus assume that angels are different from men? Mark 13:32.
  16. 16.Then can angels be the spirits of dead men?
  17. 17.As holy angels would not deceive, who must the spirits be which claim to be our dead friends? Rev. 16:14.
  18. 18.Where do the angels dwell? Mark 12:25; Luke 2:13-15.
  19. 19.How many angels did John see around the throne in heaven? Rev. 5:11. 10,000×10,000 (=100,000,000) and thousands of thousands.
  20. 20.How does Paul express it? Heb. 12:22.
  21. 21.What were they doing? Rev. 5:12; Ps. 148:2.
  22. 22.What thought may this suggest as to our service toward God? Ps. 50:23.
  23. 23.In what reverential manner do the highest angels stand in the presence of God? Isa. 6:1-3.
  24. 24.Then in what spirit and with what deportment should sinful men come before God? Eccl. 5:1, 2.

Notes

Very frequently indeed are the angels and their work mentioned in the Bible. One can have no proper idea of the plan of salvation who does not study the work which the angels perform in connection with it. Throughout the Scriptures they appear frequently and prominently. Yet the Lord has not seen fit to gratify much of our curiosity as to the nature and history of these heavenly beings. As far as it is revealed, we have a right to inquire; but we should stop where revelation stops. Beyond this, speculation will be vain and unprofitable. Let teachers be guarded on this point.

Angel.—"A word signifying both in Hebrew and Greek a messenger, and therefore used to denote whatever God employs to execute his purposes, or to manifest his presence or power." "But this name is more eminently and distinctly applied to certain spiritual beings or heavenly intelligences, employed by God as the ministers of his will, and usually distinguished as the angels of God."—McClintock and Strong's Cyclopedia, Art. Angels.
