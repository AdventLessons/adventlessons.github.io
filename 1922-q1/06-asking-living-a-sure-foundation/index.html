Lesson 6 · February 11, 1922

Asking; Living; a Sure Foundation

PDFShare

Lesson Scripture: Matt. 7:7-29.

Lesson Help: "The Desire of Ages," pp. 298-314; "Thoughts from the Mount of Blessing," pp. 187-218, current edition.

Daily Study Outline

  1. 1.Assurance of answered prayer, questions 1, 2.
  2. 2.Our attitude toward all men, questions 3, 4.
  3. 3.Two gates; a tree known by its fruit, questions 5-7.
  4. 4.God's test of character, questions 8-10.
  5. 5.Claiming Christ's favor, questions 11, 12.
  6. 6.Hearing and doing, questions 13-15.
  7. 7.Review the lesson.

Questions

  1. 1.What assurance do we have that the Lord will respond to our sincere requests? Matt. 7:7, 8. Note 1.
  2. 2.What fact in human experience does He use to emphasize the certainty of God's promises? Verses 9, 10.
  3. 3.What comparison does Jesus make to show that the prayer of faith will be rewarded? Verse 11. Note 2.
  4. 4.What great rule of life did Jesus give? Verse 12.
  5. 5.How many roads and gateways have we before us? What difference is there between the two? Verses 13, 14. Note 3.
  6. 6.Of whom should we beware? In what manner do they come? Verse 15.
  7. 7.How is the character of a tree made known? Verses 17, 18.
  8. 8.What is done with a tree that does not bring forth good fruit? Verse 19.
  9. 9.What conclusions may we draw from this teaching regarding the work of false prophets? Verse 20.
  10. 10.What is the real test of character? Verse 21. See Luke 6:46.
  11. 11.On what grounds will many claim the favor of Christ in the Judgment? Matt. 7:22.
  12. 12.What reply will the Lord make to such claims? Verse 23. Note 4.
  13. 13.To whom does Jesus liken the obedient hearer of His sayings? Verses 24, 25.
  14. 14.To whom does He liken the disobedient hearer? Verses 26, 27.
  15. 15.How did the teachings of Jesus affect the people? Why were they astonished? Verses 28, 29. Note 5.

Notes

1. "Ask," "seek," "knock." Be in earnest. Many pause on the very threshold of blessing and deliverance. God does not wish to see His children in need, and if we will but place our needs before Him and ask for help, He will help us.

2. Instead of saying that the Lord will give good things to them that ask Him, Luke says (Luke 11:13) that He will give His Holy Spirit, which "brings all other blessings in its train." We must yield all in order to receive this blessing. We must make room in our hearts and lives for the fullness of the Spirit. Then the love of God will be shed abroad in our hearts. Rom. 5:5.

3. "Strait" is used in the sense of "narrow" in both verses. The gate does not open to the pilgrim laden with selfishness, worldliness, riches, sin. All these must be left without. As long as we cling to such things, we cannot enter the narrow gate, whatever we may profess to the contrary.

4. "I never knew you." Awful words will they be in the final judgment! Jesus Christ is not the minister of sin. He came to save from sin, not in sin. He died to "redeem us from all iniquity [lawlessness, law-breaking], and purify unto Himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works." Titus 2:14. It cannot be too strongly emphasized that faith in Christ leads to cleansing from sin, clean living, and righteous doing.

5. "Having authority." The scribes built their teachings on the traditions of men. Jesus came with a mission, a message, to save men; and the words He spoke were spirit and life. As the Father sent Him, so has He sent others. If we are His heralds, we have His message. And in that message is authority and power. He would not have His servants frightened from the positiveness of the message by the cry of "dogmatic," or the insolence of tradition, or the sneer of "learned" philosophical critics. "Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort with all longsuffering and doctrine." 2 Tim. 4:2.
