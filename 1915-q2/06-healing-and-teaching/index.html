Lesson 6 · May 8, 1915

Healing and Teaching

Lesson Scripture: Matt. 9:18-38.

Questions

  1. 1.What statement and request did a certain ruler make? Matt. 9:18.
  2. 2.How did Jesus respond? Verse 19.
  3. 3.While Jesus was on His way to the ruler's house, who approached Him from behind? Verse 20. What did the woman do?
  4. 4.What was her thought as she did so? Verse 21.
  5. 5.What was the effect of this touch? Luke 8:44.
  6. 6.Relate the conversation of Jesus with His disciples about the woman's act. Verses 45, 46.
  7. 7.What public testimony did the woman immediately bear? Verse 47.
  8. 8.In what comforting way did Jesus speak to her? Matt. 9:22. Note 1.
  9. 9.On arriving at the ruler's house, what did Jesus find? Verse 23.
  10. 10.What did He say to the people? Verse 24. How did they receive it? Note 2.
  11. 11.What was then done, and with what result? Verses 25, 26.
  12. 12.Who followed Jesus when He left the ruler's house? Verse 27. What did they cry?
  13. 13.What question did Jesus ask them? Verse 28. How did they answer?
  14. 14.What did Jesus then do and say? Verse 29. With what result? Verse 30, first part.
  15. 15.What charge did Jesus give them? Verse 30, last part. How did they regard it? Verse 31.
  16. 16.As they went out of the house, who was brought to Jesus? Verse 32.
  17. 17.What led the multitude to marvel? Verse 33. What did they say?
  18. 18.What self-contradictory thing did the Pharisees say? Verse 34.
  19. 19.What threefold work did Jesus continue to do in the cities and villages? Verse 35. Compare Matt. 4:23.
  20. 20.What moved Jesus with compassion? Matt. 9:36.
  21. 21.What did He say to His disciples? Verse 37. Note 3.
  22. 22.What did He bid the disciples and us pray for? Verse 38.

Notes

1. The tender and fatherly way in which Jesus addressed those who were in distress, is particularly noticeable. As He had called the palsied man "son," so He called this suffering woman "daughter." How closely He identified Himself with needy humanity, frequently calling Himself "Son of man"!

2. "The maid is not dead, but sleepeth." So simple and temporary a thing does death seem to the great Life-giver! The Scriptures often speak of death as a sleep — a most fitting term for the state of those who sleep in Jesus, implying, as it does, rest, temporary ceasing of activity, a final awakening to newness of life. The laugh of scorn must have been turned into shame and surprise when the maiden arose.

3. There would seem to have been no time in the history of the world when laborers were fewer in comparison with the greatness of the harvest, than is the case to-day. With a deep sense of its necessity, we can pray the prayer commended by our Lord to His disciples.
