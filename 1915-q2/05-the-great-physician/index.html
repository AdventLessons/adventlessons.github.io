Lesson 5 · May 1, 1915

The Great Physician

Lesson Scripture: Matt. 9:1-17.

Questions

  1. 1.Where did Jesus now return? Matt. 9:1.
  2. 2.Who was brought to Him to be healed? Verse 2, first part.
  3. 3.What did Jesus say to the sick man? Verse 2, last part. What ground for good cheer was given?
  4. 4.What is implied in the expression, "Be of good cheer"? Note 1.
  5. 5.What did certain scribes say? Verse 3.
  6. 6.What question did Jesus ask about their thoughts? Verse 4. Note 2.
  7. 7.What twofold question did He then ask? Verse 5.
  8. 8.What did He then say to the palsied man? Verse 6, last part.
  9. 9.How did the man respond? Verse 7; Luke 5:25.
  10. 10.Of what ought this manifestation of power by Jesus convince the scribes? Matt. 9:6, first part. Note 3.
  11. 11.How did this miracle affect the multitudes? Verse 8.
  12. 12.Relate the calling of Matthew. Verse 9. Note 4.
  13. 13.Who sat at meat with Jesus in the house of Matthew? Verse 10.
  14. 14.What criticism did the Pharisees make to the disciples? Verse 11.
  15. 15.How did Jesus answer for His disciples? Verse 12.
  16. 16.What did He urge the Pharisees to do? Verse 13, first part.
  17. 17.In what words did Jesus define the purpose of His coming to earth? Verse 13, last part.
  18. 18.What question did the disciples of John the Baptist ask Jesus? Verse 14. Note 5.
  19. 19.In what figurative language did Jesus answer their question? Verse 15. Compare John the Baptist's own words in John 3:29.
  20. 20.By what parable did Jesus seek to win His hearers from the Jewish traditions to His teachings? Verse 16. Note 6.
  21. 21.Relate His second parable to the same end. Verse 17.

Notes

1. "Be of good cheer." These words imply that the palsied man was in distress of mind. The cause of this distress is seen in the words which follow : "Thy sins be forgiven thee." This man was under conviction of sin. While he wanted to be healed of palsy, he feared that his sins would bar him from such a blessing. The reason for bidding him be of good cheer was to assure him that his sins were forgiven. It was the natural step, too, toward healing him of physical disease brought on by sins. Sorrow for sin draws forgiveness and restoring power from Jesus.

2. Their charge of blasphemy was based on the truth that no one but God can forgive sins. Luke 5:21. The evil in this charge was that it virtually denied that Jesus was divine.

3. The Pharisees might as well have asked, Who can heal but God alone? They could actually see that the effects of sin in this man's body were removed instantly by the power of Jesus' word. Has not He who can remove the results of sin the power to forgive the sin itself? If He can forgive sin, is He not God? So were the Pharisees answered; and so ought they to have been convinced on their own ground, that no one but God can forgive sin.

4. This is the Matthew who was numbered among the twelve, and who became the author of the Gospel we are studying. Marvelous power in Jesus' bidding, "Follow Me"! The soil in Matthew's heart had been prepared by the previous teaching of Jesus, as was that of thousands who responded to the preaching of Pentecost. "In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thy hand."

5. The fact that John's disciples included the Pharisees with themselves in asking the question about fasting, indicates that the Pharisees had been seeking to create doubt and disaffection among John's disciples as well as those of Jesus. Thus do blind leaders, by secret and indirect means, seek to draw away disciples after themselves.

6. The traditions and ceremonies of the Jews were like an old garment, threadbare and unsightly, bringing neither comfort nor grace to the wearer. Jesus offered them a new robe of righteousness, like cloth of gold and fine twined linen. But before they could wear the new garment, they must put off the old entirely. The old could not be patched up with parts of the new. Jesus, the true antitypical Lamb, was about to be slain for the sins of the world. The typical sacrifices would no longer have virtue. We must receive Jesus as a whole sacrifice, making no reserve to consume upon our selfishness.
