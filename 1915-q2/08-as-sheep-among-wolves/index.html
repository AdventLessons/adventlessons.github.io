Lesson 8 · May 22, 1915

As Sheep among Wolves

Lesson Scripture: Matt. 10:16-33.

Questions

  1. 1.How did Jesus send His disciples forth? Matt. 10:16, first part.
  2. 2.What did He tell them to be? Verse 16, last part.
  3. 3.Of what were they to beware? Verse 17. For what reason? Note 1.
  4. 4.Why are workers brought before civil rulers? Verse 18. Note 2.
  5. 5.Under such circumstances, about what are we not to be anxious? Verse 19. Why? Note 3.
  6. 6.How does the Father help His children in the hour of trial? Verse 20.
  7. 7.How bitter will persecution for Jesus' sake become? Verse 21.
  8. 8.What must the Christian endure for the name of Christ? Verse 22, first part.
  9. 9.What comforting assurance is added? Verse 22, last part.
  10. 10.How is the worker to relate himself to his persecutors? Verse 23. Note 4.
  11. 11.How is the disciple related to his master? Verse 24.
  12. 12.What is it enough for the disciple to be? Verse 25. How is this illustrated?
  13. 13.Why is the gospel worker not to fear? Verse 26.
  14. 14.How is this reason explained? Verse 27. Note 5.
  15. 15.What kind of fear may the disciple cherish? Verse 28. Note 6.
  16. 16.How is our Father's care illustrated and impressed? Verses 29, 30.
  17. 17.Why are we therefore not to fear? Verse 31.
  18. 18.What is the basis of acceptance with the Father? Verse 32.
  19. 19.Whom can the Father not receive? Verse 33.

Notes

1. The mission of the worker for Christ is to save men. For this reason, he must go out among them, and mingle with them, searching out those whom he can lead to the Saviour. In this work, he will encounter those here called "wolves," ready to intimidate and devour and destroy. Of this class, says Jesus, "beware," that is, be wary, be watchful, be ready to meet them, not in the spirit of fight, as wolf meets wolf, but as explained in the following verses.

2. Jesus says that the faithful worker will be called before civil authorities "for My sake," or more exactly, "because of Me." The teaching and preaching of Jesus stirs up the wolfish nature in unrepentant or bigoted hearts. The purpose in allowing His servants to be persecuted is here made clear: "for a testimony to them and to the Gentiles." The gospel must reach men in authority, and often the only way to get their ear is through civil process. The worker, then, is to recognize such an occasion as his opportunity to bear witness for Jesus.

3. "Take no thought"—the same expression as in Matt. 6:25-31, rendered in the Revised Version, "Be not anxious." Its root idea is, to divide or distract the mind; in short: Do not worry. Be calm and self-possessed. Be not anxious over the how or what you shall speak. Depend upon the help of the Holy Spirit.

4. Fleeing, though apparently an act of cowardice, is often the better part of wisdom. There is such a thing as a masterly retreat in time of battle, to save the lives of men, and conserve fighting strength. In gospel work, the seeds of truth must be scattered. Persecution scattered the early Christians away from Jerusalem, throughout Judea and Samaria, and they "went everywhere preaching the Word." The worker is not to "tarry at all hazards," but to go where he can do the most good.

5. What the disciples had learned from Jesus in quiet retreats, in the still hours of the night, they were to teach openly and boldly, keeping back nothing; and this in the face of the hardships they should endure.

6. "I will not fear what man shall do unto me," but I may well fear the tempter and destroyer of souls, and find refuge in the tender care and protection of the Father.
