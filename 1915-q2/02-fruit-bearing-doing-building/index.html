Lesson 2 · April 10, 1915

Fruit Bearing; Doing; Building

Lesson Scripture: Matt. 7:15-29.

Questions

  1. 1.Of what are we to beware? Matt. 7:15. In what guise do false prophets come to us? What is their true nature?
  2. 2.How are we to know them? Verse 16, first part.
  3. 3.What illustration is given to enforce this principle? Verse 16.
  4. 4.What kind of fruit does a good tree bring forth? Verse 17. A corrupt tree?
  5. 5.What can a good tree not bring forth? Verse 18. A corrupt tree?
  6. 6.What is done to a tree that does not bring forth good fruit? Verse 19.
  7. 7.How, then, are we to know trees? Verse 20.—How may we discern the spirit of religious teachers? Note 1.
  8. 8.Who are not sure of entrance into the kingdom of heaven? Verse 21. Who are sure?
  9. 9.What pleas will many make in the day of the Lord? Verse 22.
  10. 10.How will the Lord respond to such a plea? Verse 23.
  11. 11.To what does Jesus liken one who hears and does His sayings? Verse 24.
  12. 12.What tests will such a house stand?—Verse 25. Why?
  13. 13.To what does Jesus liken one who hears His sayings, but does not do them? Verse 26.
  14. 14.What test will such a house not stand? Verse 27. What is said of its fall? Note 2.
  15. 15.How did the teachings of Jesus affect His hearers? Verse 28.
  16. 16.What reason is given for this effect? Verse 29.

Notes

1. With every wind of doctrine blowing, we are to know whether influences and teachings are drawing souls to the divine standard of the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus, and building up God's cause. See Isa. 8:20 for the Bible rule. And we must not omit applying to our own personal habits of life this principle of deciding the nature of a thing by its fruits. We shall get no good fruit out of wrong habits of talking, or thinking, or reading, etc. Let us bring every practice to the test: "Does it minister to my spiritual growth? and yield fruit to holiness of life and earnestness in God's service?"

2. This wonderful sermon on the mount, so called, is really many sermons condensed into one, so full of meaning is every passage. Jesus may have given it more fully than is recorded here; but as it has come down to us, it is a compendium of gospel principles to live by. There could hardly be a more fitting close, or climax, to this sermon than the parable of the two houses. The mere hearing of truth does not edify nor build strength into character. It is only when the truth takes hold upon the heart and works itself out in the deeds of life, that it brings salvation to the hearer.
