Lesson 10 · March 5, 1898

Opposition at Ephesus

This lesson PDF Whole quarter PDF · 13 lessonsShare on WhatsApp

(.)

  1. 1.How long did Paul labor at Ephesus?
  2. 2.Tell the striking results of his ministry there.
  3. 3.After these things, what did he purpose to do? Where did he intend to go before visiting Jerusalem? What place did he then propose to visit? .
  4. 4.Whom did he send on before he started? Where did he himself remain? .
  5. 5.What took place at that time? .
  6. 6.Who was the instigator of the trouble? What was the source of the silverworkers profit? .
  7. 7.Whom did this man assemble? How did he address them? .
  8. 8.What did he say as to the influence of Paul's preaching? .
  9. 9.What did he say was in danger? What as to the temple and worship of Diana? .
  10. 10.What effect had these words upon the craftsmen? How did they show their religious zeal? .
  11. 11.How extensively did the agitation spread? Whom did the people seize? and what did they do? .
  12. 12.What did Paul seek to do? .
  13. 13.What shows the esteem in which Paul was held by the better people? .
  14. 14.What was the condition of the assembled mob? .
  15. 15.Whom did the Jews put forward as their spokesman? .
  16. 16.How was he received? What sort of an uproar ensued? .
  17. 17.Who quieted the crowd? To what did he refer as an established fact? .
  18. 18.If, as they believed, the worship of Diana was from Heaven, what ought they to do? .
  19. 19.What testimony did he give concerning the peaceable character of Paul and his companions? .
  20. 20.What did he say was the proper means of settling any real grievance? .
  21. 21.In what danger did they stand? .
  22. 22.What did the town clerk then do? .

Additional Questions and Suggestions

  1. 1.The town clerk was undoubtedly a heathen; yet see how he had grasped the principle that truth can defend itself. Compare the words of Joash, the father of Gideon. .
  2. 2.Note that this great tumult, which but for the prudent management of the town clerk, might have resulted in a wholesale persecution and massacre of the Christians, was wholly a business matter. The silversmiths' devotion to the goddess Diana was wholly a question of profit: from the worship of Diana they got their living. Their opposition to Paul was solely on account of the fact that the spread of Christianity interfered with their business. Even so in the days of Trajan, the persecution that was raised against the Christians, concerning which Pliny wrote to the emperor, was due to the fact that those who sold cattle for the heathen sacrifices, found their business diminishing.
