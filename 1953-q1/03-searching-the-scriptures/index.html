Lesson 3 · January 17, 1953

Searching the Scriptures

PDFShare

Memory verse

Study Help: "The Bible, the Spirit of Prophecy, and the Church," chapter 3.

Daily Study Assignment

  1. 1.General survey of the lesson.
  2. 2.Questions 1-3; memorize 2 Tim. 2:15.
  3. 3.Question 4; read study help assignment.
  4. 4.Questions 5-7.
  5. 5.Questions 8, 9.
  6. 6.Questions 10-14.
  7. 7.Review the entire lesson.

Lesson Outline

I. The Authority of the Bible.

  1. 1.Jesus believed it all. Luke 24:25.
  2. 2.It was His unerring guide. Matt. 22:29.
  3. 3.He believed the Scriptures could not be broken. John 10:35.
  4. 4.They were the Alpha and Omega, as He also was. Rev. 21:6; Testimonies, vol. 8, p. 299.

II. The Blessings of the Bible.

  1. 1.It cleanses and sanctifies. John 15:3; 17:17.
  2. 2.It satisfies and edifies. Ps. 119:10; Acts 20:32.
  3. 3.It comforts and strengthens. Ps. 119:28, 52.

III. The Study of the Bible.

  1. 1.We must be ready to hear God's words. Rev. 1:3.
  2. 2.We should meditate prayerfully, daily, diligently. 1 Tim. 4:15; Ps. 119:18; Education, page 189.
  3. 3.We must follow the Holy Spirit's guidance. 1 Cor. 2:10, 11.

The Lesson

The Unerring Guide

  1. 1.What evidence do we have that Jesus accepted the Scriptures as an unerring guide? Luke 24:25, 27, 44.

Notes

"Jesus so reverenced the Scriptures for the sole reason that they are of God in the highest sense in which those words have ever been understood. The Old Testament was to Jesus God's Book—the Father's expressed mind and will."-John Urquhart, The Inspiration and Accuracy of the Holy Scriptures, pages 80, 81.

  1. 2.What further considerations lead us to conclude that the Holy Writings are unerring in their counsel? John 20:31.

Notes

"The Bible presents a perfect standard of character; it is an infallible guide under all circumstances, even to the end of the journey of life. Take it as the man of your counsel, the rule of your daily life."-Testimonies, vol. 5, p. 264.

The Alpha and Omega of Knowledge

  1. 3.What term, applied to Christ, the Eternal Word, may also properly be applied to His written word? Rev. 21:6.

Notes

"We have a Bible full of the most precious truth. It contains the Alpha and Omega of knowledge."-Testimonies, vol. 8, p. 299.

The Rule of Faith and Doctrine

  1. 4.By what should doctrines and faiths be tested? Isa. 8:20; John 5:39; 2 Tim. 3:15.

Notes

"The Bible, and the Bible alone, is our rule of faith."-Testimonies on Sabbath-School Work, page 32.

Blessings From the Study of the Word

  1. 5.What are some of the characteristics of the word of God? Ans. The word of God is:Pure. Prov. 30:5.Holy. Rom. 1:2.Sure. 2 Peter 1:19.Faithful. Titus 1:9.Reliable. 1 Kings 8:56.Living. Heb. 4:12.
  2. 6.What can be accomplished in the lives of God's children as a result of the study of the Scriptures? Ans. They can be:Cleansed. John 15:3.Sanctified. John 17:17.Satisfied. Ps. 119:103.Edified. Acts 20:32.Comforted. Ps. 119:52.Strengthened. Ps. 119:28.
  3. 7.When we permit our lives to be guided by God's word, how shall we regard it? Ans. We shall:Trust it. Ps. 119:42.Meditate on it. Ps. 119:15.Love it. Ps. 119:140.Preach it. Acts 8:4.

Duty to Study the Bible

  1. 8.What earnest counsel is given concerning the study of the word of God? 2 Tim. 2:15.
  2. 9.What examples are given in the Bible of those who diligently studied the Scriptures? 1 Peter 1:10; Acts 17:11.

Notes

The original words rendered "search" in these texts mean to search into, to examine fully, to explore.

"The minds of the Bereans were not narrowed by prejudice. . . . Daily they searched the Inspired Records; and as they compared scripture with scripture, heavenly angels were beside them, enlightening their minds and impressing their hearts."-The Acts of the Apostles, page 231.

How to Study

  1. 10.What counsel does the Lord give us as to how to study His Sacred Word? Isa. 28:10.

Notes

"In daily study the verse-by-verse method is often most helpful. Let the student take one verse, and concentrate the mind on ascertaining the thought that God has put into that verse for him, and then dwell upon the thought until it becomes his own. One passage thus studied until its significance is clear, is of more value than the perusal of many chapters with no definite purpose in view, and no positive instruction gained."—Education, page 189.

  1. 11.What assurance of divine help do we have as we approach the word of God? Eph. 1:17, 18.
  2. 12.Through whom does the Lord direct us in our study of His word? 1 Cor. 2:10, 11, 14; John 14:26.
  3. 13.What may we sometimes encounter in our meditation upon the divine word? 2 Peter 3:15, 16.

Notes

"The word of God, like the character of its divine Author, presents mysteries that can never be fully comprehended by finite beings. The entrance of sin into the world, the incarnation of Christ, regeneration, the resurrection, and many other subjects presented in the Bible, are mysteries too deep for the human mind to explain, or even fully to comprehend. But we have no reason to doubt God's word because we cannot understand the mysteries of His providence."—Steps to Christ, page 111.

  1. 14.What prayers of David indicate the desires of a sincere student of the word of life? Ps. 119:18; 143:10; 25:5.

Illustration

A great philosopher and poet had given a discourse on the internal evidences of the Bible. He closed with a statement of his own experience with the Bible. "Other books I find at my will," he said, then added impressively, "but the Bible finds me!"

Do you study the Bible? Has it found you?

For Further Study

  1. 1.Searching the Bible makes us fall in love with Jesus, the Man of Calvary. See Life Sketches, page 293.
  2. 2.Utilizing the spare moments of a busy life in reading the word is profitable. Counsels to Parents, Teachers, and Students, page 463.
  3. 3.The Bible alone is our rule of faith and doctrine. Testimonies on Sabbath-School Work, page 32; Gospel Workers, page 249.
  4. 4.Some productive methods of Bible study: a. Biographical or character study. Hebrews 11; Testimonies, vol. 4, pp. 9, 15. b. This method gives us wide views as we read large sections at one sitting. Various inexpensive outline book studies are on the market. The Outlined Bible, by G. Campbell Morgan, is good, but larger. c. Chapter-and-verse study. The Desire of Ages, page 390; Education, page 189. d. Doctrinal study. To take a doctrine like faith, righteousness, grace, the Sabbath, and follow it through the Bible is an exhaustless source of profit. e. Topical study. As distinct from doctrines, we might take themes like patience, alertness, activity, the purposes of God, etc. f. Historical and chronological study. Such study, by a good student especially, may help him profit from events and dates. The sojourn of Israel in Egypt, the Exodus wanderings, the captivities, the apostasies and reforms in Israel, the 2300 days, are but a few illustrations.
