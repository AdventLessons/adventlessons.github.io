Lesson 7 · February 17, 1934

The Holy Spirit and the New Birth

PDFShare

Daily Lesson Study: Place a check mark in the proper space.

Offering Record: Fill in each space with the weekly record.

Memory Verse: "Being born again, not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, by the word of God, which liveth and abideth forever." 1 Peter 1:23.

Introduction

"The Christian's life is not a modification or improvement of the old, but a transformation of nature. There is a death to self and sin, and a new life altogether. This change can be brought about only by the effectual working of the Holy Spirit."—"The Desire of Ages," p. 172.

The Lesson

  1. 1.What solemn statement did the Saviour make to Nicodemus? John 3:3.

Notes

"It is impossible for finite minds to comprehend the work of redemption. Its mystery exceeds human knowledge; yet he who passes from death to life realizes that it is a divine reality. The beginning of redemption we may know here through a personal experience. Its results reach through the eternal ages."—"The Desire of Ages," p. 173.

  1. 2.How much could Nicodemus, whose heart was unrenewed, understand concerning this spiritual experience? With what words did Jesus seek to make His meaning clear? Verses 4-6.
  2. 3.How only can spiritual truth be discerned? 1 Cor. 2:14.

Notes

"It is by faith that the soul beholds the glory of Jesus. This glory is hidden, until, through the Holy Spirit, faith is kindled in the soul."—Id., p. 392.

"Not by the decisions of courts or councils or legislative assemblies, not by the patronage of worldly great men, is the kingdom of Christ established, but by the implanting of Christ's nature in humanity through the work of the Holy Spirit."—Id., p. 509.

  1. 4.What conflict is going on in the human heart? Rom. 7:23, 24.

Notes

"To man the first intimation of redemption was communicated in the sentence pronounced upon Satan in the garden. . . . This sentence, uttered in the hearing of our first parents, was to them a promise. While it foretold war between man and Satan, it declared that the power of the great adversary would finally be broken. . . . When Satan heard that enmity should exist between himself and the woman, and between his seed and her Seed, he knew that his work of depraving human nature would be interrupted; that by some means man would be enabled to resist his power."—"Patriarchs and Prophets," pp. 65, 66.

  1. 5.What decides the conflict and gives victory to the struggling human soul? Rom. 8:1, 2.
  2. 6.To what is the new birth likened? John 3:8.

Notes

"Like the wind, which is invisible, yet the effects of which are plainly seen and felt, is the Spirit of God in its work upon the human heart. That regenerating power, which no human eye can see, begets a new life in the soul; it creates a new being in the image of God. While the work of the Spirit is silent and imperceptible, its effects are manifest."—"Steps to Christ," p. 61.

  1. 7.How are the natural and the spiritual birth defined by the Saviour? Verse 6.
  2. 8.What is accomplished by the new birth? Gal. 6:15.
  3. 9.What agency does the Holy Spirit use in bringing about the new birth? 1 Peter 1:23.
  4. 10.What must always precede the new birth? Gal. 2:19, 20.
  5. 11.By whom is the new creation? Eph. 2:10.

Notes

He who created the world and man in the beginning, is the One who creates men and women anew, and who will one day create new heavens and a new earth.

  1. 12.How is it shown that the One who created the worlds is the One who purges our sins, thus making us new creatures in Christ Jesus? Heb. 1:1-3.
  2. 13.What part did the Holy Spirit act in the work of creation, in the beginning? Gen. 1:2, last part.

Notes

In the work of the new creation, the Holy Spirit moves upon the sinful human heart, bringing conviction, which is the first step in the work of conversion.

  1. 14.What then is accomplished through the new birth? Eph. 2:4-6.

Notes

"By an agency as unseen as the wind, Christ is constantly working upon the heart. Little by little, perhaps unconsciously to the receiver, impressions are made that tend to draw the soul to Christ. These may be received through meditating upon Him, through reading the Scriptures, or through hearing the word from the living preacher. Suddenly, as the Spirit comes with more direct appeal, the soul gladly surrenders itself to Jesus."—"The Desire of Ages," p. 172.
