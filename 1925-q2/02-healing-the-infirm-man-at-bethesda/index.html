Lesson 2 · April 11, 1925

Healing the Infirm Man at Bethesda

PDFShare

Lesson Scripture: John 5:1-24.

Memory verse

Lesson Help: "The Desire of Ages," pp. 201-213.

Place: The pool of Bethesda in Jerusalem.

Persons: Jesus; the sick lying at the pool of Bethesda; the infirm man; the Jews.

Setting of the Lesson

"Jesus was again at Jerusalem. Walking alone, in apparent meditation and prayer, He came to the pool. He saw the wretched sufferers watching for that which they supposed to be their only chance of cure. He longed to exercise His healing power, and make every sufferer whole. But it was the Sabbath day. Multitudes were going to the temple for worship, and He knew that such an act of healing would so excite the prejudice of the Jews as to cut short His work. But the Saviour saw one case of supreme wretchedness. It was that of a man who had been a helpless cripple for thirty-eight years."—"The Desire of Ages," p. 201.

Suggestions for Daily Family Study

  1. 1.Read the "Setting of the Lesson." Read a description of the pool of Bethesda. (See "Sabbath School Worker" or Bible dictionary.) Read John 5:1-16.
  2. 2.Read eight paragraphs of the primary lesson story in "Our Little Friend." Let the story of the infirm man be told, by each member of the family contributing a part. Note the omitted details in the story-telling. Study Notes 1-4. Drill on the memory text.
  3. 3.Read selected portions of the Lesson Help, pp. 201-204. Discuss topics in the junior lesson in "The Youth's Instructor" under the heading "Can You Tell?" Drill on the memory text.
  4. 4.Read John 5:17-24. Ask questions on that portion of the lesson, from senior, youth's, junior, or primary lesson.
  5. 5.Study Questions 12-16. Read selected portions of the Lesson Help, pp. 204-213. Drill on the memory text.
  6. 6.Review the lesson. Use questions from the senior or the youth's lesson, and study notes in the "Instructor." Review the teaching of this lesson concerning the forgiveness of sin. Refer again to Note 2. Review the memory text.
  7. 7.Review the lesson. Drill on the more difficult portions. Let each member of the family relate parts of the story, emphasizing important, practical truths. Repeat the memory text.

Questions

  1. 1.What occasion brought Jesus to Jerusalem? John 5:1.
  2. 2.What noted pool was in Jerusalem? Describe it briefly. Who at times gathered at this pool? Verses 2, 3.
  3. 3.What was the common belief concerning the troubling of the water at certain times? Verse 4. Note 1.
  4. 4.What case of infirmity of long standing did Jesus find there? Verse 5.
  5. 5.As Jesus looked upon this man, what question did He ask? What reply did the man give? Verses 6, 7.
  6. 6.Upon hearing his reply, what did Jesus do? With what result? Verses 8, 9. Note 2.
  7. 7.How did the Jews refer to their narrow restrictions concerning Sabbath observance? Verse 10. Note 3.
  8. 8.How did the man reply? What further questions did the Jews ask him? Verses 11, 12.
  9. 9.Why could not the healed man directly answer the questions asked by the Jews? Verse 13.
  10. 10.How was the identity of his benefactor revealed to him? What did he then tell the Jews? Verses 14, 15.
  11. 11.What effect did his testimony have on the Jews? Verse 16. Note 4.
  12. 12.With what statement did Jesus justify His act of healing on the Sabbath? Verse 17.
  13. 13.How did this statement affect the Jews? Why did it have this effect? Verse 18.
  14. 14.What relationship did Jesus say exists between Himself and the Father? Verses 19, 20.
  15. 15.What had the Father done that the Son might be honored? Verses 21-23.
  16. 16.What is said of those who hear Christ's words, and believe on the Father who sent Him? Verse 24.

Notes

1. "At certain seasons the waters of this pool were agitated, and it was commonly believed that this was the result of supernatural power, and that whoever first after the troubling of the pool stepped into the waters, would be healed of whatever disease he had. Hundreds of sufferers visited the place; but so great was the crowd when the water was troubled that they rushed forward, trampling underfoot men, women, and children, weaker than themselves. Many could not get near the pool. Many who succeeded in reaching it, died upon its brink."—"The Desire of Ages," p. 201.

2. "From the simple Bible account of how Jesus healed the sick, we may learn something about how to believe in Him for the forgiveness of sins. Let us turn to the story of the paralytic at Bethesda. The poor sufferer was helpless; he had not used his limbs for thirty-eight years. Yet Jesus bade him, 'Rise, take up thy bed, and walk.' The sick man might have said, 'Lord, if Thou wilt make me whole, I will obey Thy word.' But no, he believed Christ's word, believed that he was made whole, and he made the effort at once; he willed to walk, and he did walk. He acted on the word of Christ, and God gave the power. He was made whole.

"In like manner you are a sinner. You can not atone for your past sins, you can not change your heart, and make yourself holy. But God promises to do all this for you through Christ. You believe that promise. You confess your sins, and give yourself to God. You will to serve Him. Just as surely as you do this, God will fulfill His word to you. If you believe the promise,—believe that you are forgiven and cleansed,—God supplies the fact; you are made whole, just as Christ gave the paralytic power to walk when the man believed that he was healed. It is so if you believe it."—"Steps to Christ," chapter "Faith and Acceptance."

3. "Jesus had purposely chosen the Sabbath day upon which to perform the miracle at the pool. . . . A wise purpose underlay every act of Christ's life on earth; everything He did was important in itself and its teaching. . . . The Sabbath, instead of being the blessing it was designed to be, had become a curse through the added requirements of the Jews. Jesus wished to rid it of these incumbrances and leave it standing upon its own holy dignity. Therefore He chose the Sabbath for this special work."—"Spirit of Prophecy," vol. 2, pp. 161, 162.

4. Christ was persecuted by the Jews, who would have put Him to death at once if they could have had their way. The contest always has been, and still is, between God's way and man's way. Now a controversy about the Sabbath question has been entered upon, which will end only in the effort to put to death those who will not yield to the demand that they keep the Sabbath according to man's command (Rev. 13:15); but God will work deliverance for His people. Read the book of Esther upon this subject, especially chapters 3, 8, and 9.
