Lesson 5 · November 4, 1893

Abiding in Christ.—1 John 2:26-29; 3:1-3

This lesson PDF Whole quarter PDF · 13 lessonsShare on WhatsApp
  1. 1.Concerning whom has this instruction been given?
  2. 2.What abides in the believers?
  3. 3.Of what have they no need?
  4. 4.What teaches them?
  5. 5.What will they do as a consequence of this teaching?
  6. 6.To what are the "little children" exhorted?
  7. 7.What will be the result of thus abiding in him?
  8. 8.What is the character of Christ?
  9. 9.What is true of everyone who does righteousness?
  10. 10.To what is especial attention now directed?
  11. 11.How is this love manifested?
  12. 12.What follows from this relationship?
  13. 13.When is this relationship entered upon?
  14. 14.What doth not yet appear?
  15. 15.What change will take place "when he shall appear"?
  16. 16.What effect does this hope have upon its possessor?
  17. 17.What is the true standard of purity of character?

Notes

1. .—Paul warned the church of future trouble (), and this apostasy began in his day (). Jude writes very strongly concerning these deceivers (, 10-13), who abounded before John's death (). The Holy Spirit, which was promised by Christ (), and had been poured upon them (), would teach them the truth (), and would protect them from deceivers (). Jesus says, "Abide in me." These words convey the idea of rest, stability, confidence. See the lesson in . Such a life brings conformity to the divine character () and confidence at his coming (). "God is righteous. . . . And if so, then the source of righteousness. When, therefore, a man doeth righteousness, we know . . . that the source of his righteousness is God: that in consequence he has acquired by new birth from God that righteousness which he had not by nature. We argue from his doing righteousness to his having been begotten of God. . . . The whole mass of Socinian and Pelagian commentators have reversed the members of the argument, and made it conclude that doing righteousness is the condition, on our part, of becoming a child of God. Roman Catholic expositors . . . go equally wrong, in understanding hath been begotten, not as the statement of a past abiding fact, but as the ground of a confidence as to the future."—Alford, Lee & Shepard, edition 1883.

2. Verses 1-3.—"It would require the language of the Infinite to express the love that has made it possible for us to be called the sons of God." Faith makes us children of God (), and this is a present experience. The Holy Spirit belongs to the sons of God (), who are also heirs () of the inheritance. In fact, "all things" belong to them (). The world knew not Christ () nor the father (), and for this reason () will persecute true Christians (). This present life is the time for the perfect character. "If you give yourself to him and accept him as your Saviour, then, sinful as your life may have been, for his sake you are accounted righteous. Christ's character stands in the place of your character, and you are accepted before God just as if you had not sinned." The character is fixed at the coming of Christ (), who "shall fashion anew the body of our humiliation, that it may be conformed to the body of his glory" (, R. V.), and thus shall we "appear with him in glory" (). So shall we be satisfied. . Holiness is required of those who shall see God (), and this privilege is promised to the pure in heart (); but "who can say, I have made my heart clean, I am pure from my sin?" . It is by faith () which works () in obedience () that we are purified. "He who hath this hope in him learns from the Scriptures that he must be a worker together with God."
