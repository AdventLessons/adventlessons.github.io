Lesson 2 · April 14, 1900

Meeting with the Disciples

PDFShare
Side lights "Desire of Ages," chapter 83.

Jerusalem, A. D. 31.

(Matt. 28:9, 10; Mark 16:12; Luke 24:13-35.)

  1. 1.After Jesus had ascended to the Father and returned, to whom did He show Himself? Matt. 28:9.
  2. 2.What message did He deliver? Verse 10.
  3. 3.On what occasion did Christ appear to two of His disciples later in the day? Mark 16:12.
  4. 4.Where were these two journeying? Luke 24:13.
  5. 5.What was the subject of their conversation? Verses 14, 21.
  6. 6.While thus conversing, who joined them on the way? Verse 15.
  7. 7.How did He introduce Himself? Verse 17. See note 1.
  8. 8.Relate the conversation that followed. Verses 18, 19. Side texts: Deut. 18:18, 19; Isa. 9:6.
  9. 9.What did the disciples say concerning recent events? Verses 20, 21. Side texts: Isa. 50:6; 53:6-12.
  10. 10.What reports had been brought to them? Verses 22-24. Side texts: Luke 24:1-10.
  11. 11.What response did Jesus make to this recital? Verse 25. See note 2.
  12. 12.What question did He ask them? Verse 26.
  13. 13.How did He expound the Scriptures? In whom did He show that they all centered? Verse 27. Side texts: Acts 8:27-35.
  14. 14.As they drew near to Emmaus, what did Jesus do? Verse 28.
  15. 15.Why did He stop with them? Verse 29. See note 3. Side texts: Matt. 15:21-28.
  16. 16.Under what circumstances did He make Himself known to them? Verses 30, 31.
  17. 17.After the Saviour left them, what did they say one to another? Verse 32.
  18. 18.Where did they then go? Verse 33.
  19. 19.With what exclamation did the disciples greet them? Verse 34. Side texts: 1 Cor. 15:5; John 1:42.
  20. 20.What did the two report to the disciples? Verse 35.

Reading

"Desire of Ages," chapter 83.

  1. It seems from the conversation that these disciples were anxiously desirous to learn, if possible, what these events all meant to their hopes of their future. Jesus did then as He does now for every anxious inquirer,—draw near to give them that for which their soul longed,—a knowledge of His plan of salvation.
  2. The English word "fools" as represented to have been used by the Saviour on this occasion was not one expressing lack of sense, but rather a gentle reproof for their forgetfulness of Scripture declarations that they had been taught to cherish, which, if remembered, would have saved them from their perplexity. The sense of the text would have been as well preserved had it been translated, "O thoughtless man." The Revised Version reads, "O foolish men."
  3. They "constrained" Him, that is, pressed Him beyond measure. They had not been satisfied with His brief words so cheering, but urged His presence. This desire for His presence caused Him to tarry with them, and gave Him opportunity to make Himself known to them. Their experience in this line may be ours, and will be when we become as importunate as they.
