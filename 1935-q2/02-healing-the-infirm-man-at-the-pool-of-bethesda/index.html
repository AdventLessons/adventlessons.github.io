Lesson 2 · April 13, 1935

Healing the Infirm Man at the Pool of Bethesda

PDFShare

Daily Lesson Study: Place a check mark in the proper space.

Lesson Scripture: John 5:1-24.

Memory verse

Study Help: "The Desire of Ages," pages 201-213.

Place: The pool of Bethesda in Jerusalem; the temple.

Persons: Jesus; the sick lying by the pool of Bethesda; the infirm man; the Jews.

The Lesson

The Infirm Man

  1. 1.What occasion caused Jesus to go again to Jerusalem? John 5:1.
  2. 2.What noted pool was in that city? Who were gathered in the porches? For what were they waiting? Verses 2, 3.
  3. 3.What did these people believe concerning the water in the pool? Verse 4.

Notes

"At certain seasons the waters of this pool were agitated, and it was commonly believed that this was the result of supernatural power, and that whoever first after the troubling of the pool stepped into the waters, would be healed of whatever disease he had."—"The Desire of Ages," p. 201.

  1. 4.What man among the throng attracted the attention of Jesus? What question did Jesus ask him? Verses 5, 6.
  2. 5.How did the infirm man describe his experiences? Verse 7.
  3. 6.What did Jesus say in reply? What response did the man make? On what day of the week did this occur? Verses 8, 9.

Notes

"Jesus does not ask this sufferer to exercise faith in Him. He simply says, 'Rise, take up thy bed, and walk.' But the man's faith takes hold upon that word. Every nerve and muscle thrills with new life, and healthful action comes to his crippled limbs. Without question he sets his will to obey the command of Christ, and all his muscles respond to his will. Springing to his feet, he finds himself an active man. Jesus had given him no assurance of divine help. The man might have stopped to doubt, and lost his one chance of healing. But he believed Christ's word, and in acting upon it he received strength."—"The Desire of Ages," pp. 202, 203.

The bed was only a blanket, or mat, which could easily be rolled up and carried.

  1. 7.What criticism did the Jews at once make? Verse 10.

Notes

"Jesus had purposely chosen the Sabbath day upon which to perform the miracle at the pool. . . . A wise purpose underlay every act of Christ's life on earth; everything He did was important in itself and its teaching. . . . The Sabbath, instead of being the blessing it was designed to be, had become a curse through the added requirements of the Jews. Jesus wished to rid it of these incumbrances and leave it standing upon its own holy dignity. Therefore He chose the Sabbath for this special work."—"The Spirit of Prophecy," vol. 2, pp. 161, 162.

  1. 8.What further conversation did the Jews have with the man whom Jesus healed? Why could the man not point out the Healer? Verses 11-13.
  2. 9.Where did Jesus and the man He had healed soon meet? What counsel did Jesus give him? Verse 14.

Notes

"In the temple Jesus met the man who had been healed. He had come to bring a sin offering and also a thank offering for the great mercy he had received. Finding him among the worshipers, Jesus made Himself known."—"The Desire of Ages," p. 204.

The Father and the Son

  1. 10.How did the man unintentionally work against Jesus? What use did the Jews make of the information given them? Verses 15, 16.
  2. 11.What words of Jesus incensed the Jews still more? Verses 17, 18.
  3. 12.In what words did Jesus reveal His dependence upon the Father? Verse 19.
  4. 13.What does the Father show to the Son? To whom may the Son give life? Verses 20, 21.
  5. 14.What has the Father committed to the Son? To whom should all men show honor? Whose adoration will the Father not receive? Verses 22, 23.
  6. 15.What is said of those who hear the words of Jesus, and believe on the Father who sent Him? Verse 24.

Notes

"'He that heareth My word, and believeth on Him that sent Me, hath everlasting life.' In every command and in every promise of the word of God is the power, the very life of God, by which the command may be fulfilled and the promise realized. He who by faith receives the word is receiving the very life and character of God."—"Christ's Object Lessons," p. 38.

Think on These Things

  • Is my condition similar in any way to that of the infirm man?

Notes

  • The inference given that this man's condition was the result of sin.
  • Obedience dependent upon the exercise of the will.
  • The meaning of, "Wilt thou be made whole?"
