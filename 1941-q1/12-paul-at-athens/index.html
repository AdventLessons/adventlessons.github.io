Lesson 12 · March 22, 1941

Paul at Athens

PDFShare

Scripture: Acts 17:16-34.

Memory verse

Study Help: "The Acts of the Apostles," pages 233-242.

Places: City of Athens—the market, and Areopagus on Mars' Hill.

Persons: Paul and the Jews, devout persons; people in the market place, Greek philosophers; Dionysius, the Areopagite, and a woman named Damaris.

The Lesson

Picture: An Athenian altar gave Paul an opening for the truth. This altar gives us our opportunity

Setting of the Lesson

Athens, the capital of Attica, Greece, and the great metropolis of literature and art for the world. It was named from Athena, identified with the Roman goddess Minerva, and was founded about 1556 B. C. Succeeding generations and all civilized nations have acknowledged the intellectual supremacy of Athens.

Paul had been driven from Berea by the Jews who opposed his work at Thessalonica. He was conducted by friends to the renowned city of Athens. It is said that in Athens were more than three thousand public statues, the majority of which were in honor of heathen gods. In one street there stood before every house a square pillar upon the top of which was a statue of the god Hermes, regarded as the herald or messenger of the gods. Every gateway and porch carried its protecting god. A Roman poet ironically said, "In Athens it is easier to find gods than men."

A City of Many Gods

  1. 1.While Paul waited in the city of Athens for Silas and Timothy, what stirred his feelings? Acts 17:16.

Notes

"In this great city, where God was not worshiped, Paul was oppressed by a feeling of solitude; and he longed for the sympathy and aid of his fellow laborers. So far as human friendship was concerned, he felt himself to be utterly alone. In his epistle to the Thessalonians, he expresses his feelings in the words, 'Left at Athens alone.' Obstacles that were apparently insurmountable presented themselves before him, making it seem almost hopeless for him to attempt to reach the hearts of the people."—The Acts of the Apostles, page 234.

  1. 2.How did Paul improve the opportunity this situation afforded him? Verse 17.

Notes

Paul began to preach to the Jews in the synagogue, and talked with any who were willing to listen in the crowded market place. Around the market were grouped the public buildings, shops, and bazaars. "Within the square, beat the heart of the city."

  1. 3.Whom did he encounter as he preached "Jesus, and the resurrection"? What did some lightly ask? What did others say? Verse 18.

Notes

Paul met the Greek philosophers as he began work in Athens. The Epicureans were disciples of Epicurus, a famous teacher. They were virtually atheists.

The Stoics were a sect of heathen philosophers, believing that the Deity was not the Creator of the universe, but its reason and soul, pervading and organizing all matter.

Paul Led to the Areopagus

  1. 4.To what place did these philosophers take Paul? What did they say to him? Verses 19, 20.

Notes

On the summit of the rugged, stony hill of Aeropagus, was a temple of Mars, the god of war, and the hilltop was the meeting place of the Athenian Council, which investigated all new teaching.

"After discussing with them for a time, they requested him [Paul] to leave the curious and thronging crowds of the market, which was not a proper arena for discussion; and for the convenience of both parties, and in order to give him a fair hearing, they led him up the steps of the Areopagus, Mars' Hill, to the platform on its summit, where were assembled 'the noblest blood of Athens, the first politicians, the first orators, the first philosophers; a court the most august, not only of Athens, but of Greece, and indeed of the whole world.' It was at this bar that Socrates was arraigned and condemned on the ground of innovating upon the state religion. Here Paul was allowed to speak 'from the tribune of a regular court, where both speaker and audience would be free from disturbance.'"—Peloubet's Select Notes.

  1. 5.What characteristic of the people of Athens is noted? Verse 21.
  2. 6.How did Paul begin his sermon on Mars' Hill? Verse 22.

Notes

This text is sometimes translated to read, "Bear witness to your carefulness in religion." The thought seems to be that their devotions were bestowed upon too many objects.

  1. 7.What strange inscription had he noticed? What use did he make of it? Verse 23.

Notes

The people sacrificed to gods to propitiate them, and keep them from sending trouble upon the people. They built altars to all the gods known among them, and then fearing that some other might punish them for neglect, they erected still another altar to any god of whom they might not have heard.

The Unknown God

  1. 8.Who is the God they did not know? Of what is He the Lord? Where does He not dwell? Verse 24.

Notes

All through the Bible the true God is distinguished as the Creator of the heavens and the earth. See Jer. 10:10-12; 32:17; 51:15; Ps. 96:5; Acts 4:24; 14:15.

  1. 9.Why does the true God need nothing we can give Him? Acts 17:25.
  2. 10.How did Paul make known the great truth that all men are brothers? What did he say had been before determined? Verse 26.
  3. 11.What should this lead all to do? What is said of the Father's nearness to us? Verse 27.

Notes

"The Greek word translated 'feel after' denotes the action of the blind person groping after what he desires to find."—Philip Schaff.

  1. 12.How dependent are all men upon God? Whom did Paul tactfully quote as authority for his statement? Verse 28.
  2. 13.Why can no idol be a god? Verse 29.

Notes

Children usually take the form and features of their parents. So the apostle argued that if we are the children of God, it is plainly to be seen that He is not made of wood, gold, silver, or marble, as are images formed by men's hands.

A New Day and Opportunity Opened for Athens

  1. 14.What did Paul say of that time of ignorance? But what are all now commanded to do? Verse 30.

Notes

God winks at ignorance in the sense that He overlooks it, unless it is willful ignorance. He is so loving and merciful that He bears with the sins of men while He tries to send them the light which would turn them away from their sins.

  1. 15.What is one reason why all should repent? What assurance is given to all? Verse 31.
  2. 16.As soon as the resurrection was mentioned, what did some do? What did others say? Verse 32.

Notes

It is quite probable that the apostle's speech was stopped before it was completed. So long as he confined himself to philosophy, even though it were new, they would listen, but the doctrine of the resurrection was too strange, too personal, too condemning.

  1. 17.What was the result of the Mars' Hill sermon where Paul matched argument with argument? Verses 33, 34.

Notes

Paul was working against a philosophical background in the minds of these Athenian Stoics and Epicureans, and in consequence found, as would a preacher of Christianity in our day, the philosophic mind closed to simple truth and faith. Under the circumstances the results of his efforts are truly remarkable.

Think on These Things

  • The value of the wisdom of this world compared with a knowledge of God.

Notes

  • The most comforting thought in the lesson—the Father is "not far from every one of us."
