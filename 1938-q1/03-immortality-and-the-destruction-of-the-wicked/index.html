Lesson 3 · January 15, 1938

Immortality, and the Destruction of the Wicked

PDFShare

Daily Lesson Study: Place a check mark in the proper space.

Memory verse

Lesson Helps: "Here and Hereafter," pp. 263-275, 286, 287; "Our Day in the Light of Prophecy," pp. 287-293.

The Lesson

For Life or Death

  1. 1.For what purpose did God send Jesus into the world? Matt. 1:21; 9:12, 13.
  2. 2.How many have sinned? Rom. 3:23.
  3. 3.What came upon men as a result of sin? Gen. 2:17; Rom. 5:12.
  4. 4.In what state are all sinners? Eph. 2:1; Col. 2:13.
  5. 5.What transformation takes place in the one who is converted and accepts Christ? John 5:24; 1 John 3:14.
  6. 6.In what condition do those remain who do not believe on Christ? 1 John 5:12.
  7. 7.In whom is the life of the Christian? 1 John 5:11; Col. 3:3, 4.
  8. 8.When will the Christian actually receive immortality? 1 Cor. 15:51-54.

Notes

Faith in Christ translates the believer out of the kingdom of darkness (Col. 1:13), out of the land of the valley of the shadow of death into the kingdom of His dear Son, out of death unto life; but man does not actually receive immortality until the resurrection.

The Death Penalty—Its Effectiveness

  1. 9.How is the final death penalty executed upon the wicked? Rev. 21:8; 20:13-15.
  2. 10.With what kind of fire is the eternal death executed? Matt. 25:41; 3:12.
  3. 11.What will this fire do to the wicked? Mal. 4:1-3; Ps. 37:20.

Notes

"With unquenchable fire." Unquenchable fire is fire that cannot be put out till it consumes utterly that which it is burning. In many texts of Scripture the entire destruction of sin and sinners is illustrated by the withered branches to be burned (John 15:6), by bad fish cast away to corruption (Matt. 13:47, 48), by the destruction of Sodom by fire (Luke 17:29; 2 Peter 2:5, 6). In the reference in Matthew 3:12 the unquenchable fire is to burn up the chaff, to completely wipe it out. So it will be with the wicked when the Lord purges the earth and cleanses it from sin. Malachi speaks of the wicked reduced to ashes, the revelator says they shall be devoured, while the psalmist speaks of them as consumed away into smoke. Each of these Bible writers gives one impression to the mind—that of the complete destruction or annihilation of the wicked.

  1. 12.In contrast to the reward of the righteous, what do the wicked receive? Matt. 25:46.

Notes

"Everlasting punishment" may, and in this case does, mean a punishment that is complete, and will never have to be repeated. Sin and sinners will be wiped out, destroyed to arise no more. Hence punishment will never again be necessary.

  1. 13.What is the end of the wicked? 2 Thess. 1:9; Rev. 21:8.
  2. 14.How complete will be their destruction? Ps. 37:10; Mal. 4:1; Nahum 1:9.
  3. 15.In what manner will Satan be destroyed? Ezek. 28:18.

Notes

"Said the angel, 'Satan is the root, his children are the branches. They are now consumed root and branch. They have died an everlasting death. They are never to have a resurrection, and God will have a clean universe.' "—"Early Writings," p. 295.

  1. 16.In view of the human tendency to turn to worldliness and evil, what counsel does the prophet Hosea give? Hosea 12:6.
  2. 17.What will be the result of such repentance and continued loyalty? Rom. 2:7.

Lesson Outline

I. For Life or Death

  1. 1.God sent Christ into the world to save men. Matt. 1:21.
  2. 2.All have sinned—death is the penalty. Rom. 5:12.
  3. 3.The man converted by the gospel is transformed. John 5:24; 1 John 3:14.
  4. 4.Eternal life, immortality, comes to us only in Christ. 1 John 5:11; 1 Cor. 15:51-54.

II. The Death Penalty—Its Effectiveness

  1. 1.Death's final penalty executed. Rev. 21:8; 20:13-15.
  2. 2.Death and destruction of evildoers will be complete and final. Mal. 4:1-3; Ps. 37:20; Matt. 25:46.
  3. 3.Satan will be included in the final destruction of evil. Ezek. 28:18.
  4. 4.God desires man's return to Him and his continual loyalty. Hosea 12:6; Rom. 2:7.
