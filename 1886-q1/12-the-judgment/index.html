Lesson 11 · March 20, 1886

The Judgment

PDFShare
  1. 1.How were the sins of the people figuratively borne into the earthly sanctuary?
  2. 2.How are sins borne into the heavenly sanctuary?—Christ takes upon himself the sins of penitent believers.
  3. 3.In what other way do they find a lodgment there? Jer. 2:22; Isa. 65:6, 7.
  4. 4.For what purpose are they thus recorded? Rev. 20:12; Dan. 7:9, 10.
  5. 5.Can these records be blotted out before they have served their purpose in the Judgment?
  6. 6.Can the sanctuary be cleansed without blotting out sin records contained therein?
  7. 7.What does this prove?—That the Judgment and the cleansing of the sanctuary go on together.
  8. 8.How was the atonement made in the earthly sanctuary?—By presenting the blood of the sin-offering.
  9. 9.How will it be made in the heavenly?—Christ will present his own blood.
  10. 10.How were they removed from the earthly sanctuary?—They were put upon the head of the scape-goat.
  11. 11.How will they be removed from the heavenly?—They will be blotted from the books, and laid upon Satan, the antitypical scape-goat. (See note.)
  12. 12.Whom will Christ confess and accept in the Judgment? Matt. 10:32.
  13. 13.Whom will he deny? Verse 33.
  14. 14.Whose names will be retained in the book of life? Rev. 3:5.
  15. 15.Whose names will be blotted out? Ex. 32:33.
  16. 16.What blotting out will be done for those whose names are retained in the book of life? Acts 3:19.
  17. 17.Will these sins ever be brought up again? Jer. 31:34, last part; Micah 7:19, last clause.
  18. 18.What may this part of the Judgment be called?—The Investigative Judgment.
  19. 19.What view of this judgment was given to Daniel? Dan. 7:9, 10.
  20. 20.What scene was next opened before the astonished prophet? Verses 13, 14.
  21. 21.What was there given to Christ?
  22. 22.To what may this scene be compared?—To the coronation of an earthly monarch.
  23. 23.What now remains for Christ to do?—To take possession of his kingdom.
  24. 24.Read the description of this in Rev. 19:11-21.
  25. 25.How is the same scene described in 1 Thess. 4:16, 17?
  26. 26.What resurrection is here described?—The first resurrection. See Rev. 20:5.
  27. 27.Who are to have part in this first resurrection?—They which shall be accounted worthy. Luke 20:35, 36.
  28. 28.Where have they been accounted worthy?—In the Investigative Judgment.
  29. 29.What name has been given to Christ's work of destroying the wicked at his second coming?—The Executive Judgment. Jude 14, 15.

Notes

Scapegoat.—The Hebrew word for "scape-goat," as given in the margin of Lev. 16:8, is Azazel. On this verse, Jenks, in his Comprehensive Commentary, remarks: "'Scape-goat.' See different opinions in Bochart. Spencer, after the oldest opinion of the Hebrews and Christians, thinks Azazel is the name of the devil; and so Rosenmüller, whom see. The Syriac has Azzail, the angel (strong one) who revolted. These authorities unmistakably point out Satan. Thus we have the definition of the Scripture term for scape-goat, in two ancient languages, with the oldest opinion of both Hebrews and Christians, in favor of the view that the scape-goat is a type of Satan."

Charles Beecher, in "Redeemer and Redeemed," pp. 67, 68, says: "What goes to confirm this is that the most ancient paraphrases and translations treat Azazel as a proper name. The Chaldee paraphrase and the targums of Onkelos and Jonathan would certainly have translated it if it was not a proper name, but they do not. The Septuagint or oldest Greek version, renders it by ἀποπομπαῖος (apopompaios), a word applied by the Greeks to a malign deity sometimes appeased by sacrifices."—The Sanctuary and its Cleansing, pp. 310, 311.
