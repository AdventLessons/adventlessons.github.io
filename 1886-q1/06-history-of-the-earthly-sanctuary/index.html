Lesson 5 · February 6, 1886

History of the Earthly Sanctuary

PDFShare

[Note to the Student.—Do not consider the lesson learned until you can give at least the substance of every text, with the correct reference for each. The references in black letters indicate those texts that should be committed to memory. A little diligent application each day will enable you to learn them, although this need not be considered a test of scholarship.]

  1. 1.How many years before Christ, did Israel leave Egypt?—1491. (See Ex. 12, margin.)
  2. 2.How long was it from the exodus to the setting up of the tabernacle by Moses? Ex. 40:17.
  3. 3.Where was it first located? Num. 9:1, 5.
  4. 4.How long did it remain in this place? Num. 10:11, 12.
  5. 5.In what place was it first pitched after entering the land of Canaan? Josh. 4:19.
  6. 6.Where was it next located? Josh. 18:1.
  7. 7.What finally took the place of the sanctuary that Moses built? 1 Kings 6:1, 2.
  8. 8.Where was this temple erected? 2 Chron. 3:1; 1 Chron. 21:14-18.
  9. 9.What remarkable event occurred on this spot nine hundred years before? Gen. 22:2.
  10. 10.When did this building cease to be their central place of worship? 2 Chron. 36:6, 7, 16-19.
  11. 11.How long was this captivity to continue? 2 Chron. 36:21.
  12. 12.After the close of this captivity, what provision was made for another temple? Ezra 1:1, 5.
  13. 13.When was the work completed? Ezra 6:15. (Margin, B. C. 515.)
  14. 14.What next occurred in the history of the sanctuary? (See note.)
  15. 15.What mournful words did our Saviour utter with reference to this building? Matt. 23:38.
  16. 16.What miraculous event occurred indicating that the sacredness of the sanctuary was forever gone? Matt. 27:51.
  17. 17.When and by whom was the sanctuary destroyed?—By the Roman army under Titus, A. D. 70.
  18. 18.How many years had now passed since Moses set up the sanctuary in the wilderness of Sinai?

Notes

About 500 years after the completion of the temple by Ezra, the edifice sadly needed repairing. Herod the Great, desirous of ingratiating himself with the Jews, decided to rebuild it. The old one was pulled down to its foundation, and the building of the new begun B. C. 19. It was completed in A. D. 27.

It was to this building that the Jews referred when they said to the Saviour at his first Passover, in the spring of A. D. 28, "Forty and six years was this temple in building," John 2:20. According to Haggai's prophecy, the desire of all nations had now come to this temple.
