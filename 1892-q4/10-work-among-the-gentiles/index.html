Lesson 10 · December 3, 1892

Work Among the Gentiles.—Acts 14:8-22

This lesson PDF Whole quarter PDF · 13 lessonsShare on WhatsApp

(Memory Verses, 8-10.)

Golden Text.—"In His name shall the Gentiles trust." .

  1. 1.Trace the route of the missionary journey of Paul and Barnabas from Antioch in Syria to Antioch in Pisidia.
  2. 2.What did they do in Antioch?
  3. 3.Where did they go next?
  4. 4.When driven from there, where did they go?
  5. 5.What afflicted man was in the congregation at Lystra? .
  6. 6.What did Paul perceive in this man? .
  7. 7.What followed? .
  8. 8.What did this cause the people to think? .
  9. 9.What did they proceed to do? .
  10. 10.When the apostles learned what the people were about to do, what did they do and say? .
  11. 11.What evidence of himself has God always given to man, as a witness against their idolatry? .
  12. 12.What may be learned of God from his works? .
  13. 13.Where is the foolishness of worshiping idols most strikingly illustrated? .
  14. 14.As we meditate upon God's wonderful works, of what else are we reminded? .
  15. 15.What effect did the apostles' words have on the excited crowd at Lystra? .
  16. 16.What was more effective in causing a revulsion of feeling, and what was the result? .
  17. 17.What wonderful miracle followed? .
  18. 18.When the apostles returned to these cities where they had been persecuted, what did they do? .
  19. 19.What are the only references that Paul ever made to this fierce persecution? ; .

Notes

It was not because of fear that the apostles fled from one city to another when they were persecuted, as is shown by the fact that they returned over the same road. After Paul was stoned, he went back into the city, and when they had finished their missionary tour, they went back over every city that they had visited. They fled because they were driven away, and could not at that time do anything more in those places. The slight reference, also, that is made to the stoning of Paul, contains a wonderful lesson, more expressive than anything else could be. What a rebuke for those of us who are inclined to make a great ado over even a little slight or reproach that comes to us in the line of our work. It can be explained only by remembering that Paul had in him the mind of Christ (see ; ; ; ), and esteemed all these things as light afflictions ().
