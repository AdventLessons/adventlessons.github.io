Lesson 5 · January 29, 1916

Hypocrisy and Its Remedy

Lesson Scripture: Matt. 23:13-26.

Questions

  1. 1.How did Jesus address the scribes and the Pharisees directly? Matt. 23:13, first part.
  2. 2.What did He charge them with doing? What did they not do? Verse 13, last part.
  3. 3.How did He address them again? Verse 14, first part.
  4. 4.What reasons did He give for calling them hypocrites? What is the consequence of such a course? Verse 14, last part.
  5. 5.How did Jesus denounce them a third time? Verse 15, first part. Note 1.
  6. 6.What was the basis for the utterance of this third woe? Verse 15, last part. Note 2.
  7. 7.What did Jesus next call the scribes and the Pharisees? Verse 16, first part.
  8. 8.How did they show their blindness? Verse 16, last part.
  9. 9.By what question did Jesus make their blindness appear foolish? Verse 17.
  10. 10.What other example of their blindness did Jesus give? Verse 18. Note 3.
  11. 11.By what question did He expose their foolishness in this case? Verse 19.
  12. 12.What conclusion did He draw about the altar? Verse 20.
  13. 13.What is His conclusion about the temple? Verse 21.
  14. 14.How did He apply the same principle to heaven? Verse 22.
  15. 15.What ground did Jesus give for repeating the woe again? Verse 23, first part. Note 4.
  16. 16.What was the right course to follow? Verse 23, last part.
  17. 17.In what striking language did Jesus characterize such a course? Verse 24. Note 5.
  18. 18.What is the basis for another repetition of the woe? Verse 25.
  19. 19.How can so blind a course be corrected? Verse 26.

Notes

1. Repetition is one of the strongest ways of emphasizing. It is a way that is much used in the Scriptures. It is an idiom in the Hebrew of the Old Testament; as, "Blessing I will bless thee." It is employed in the Greek of the New Testament; as, "Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, rejoice;" "Babylon is fallen, is fallen;" "Woe, woe, woe to the inhabiters of the earth." In the twenty-third chapter of Matthew, the woe pronounced upon the scribes and the Pharisees is repeated seven times in the same words, and uttered once with a little variation, making eight times in all. We must conclude that the sins enumerated under these woes are particularly offensive. They should be carefully studied, lest the principle of some of them be found in our own lives.

2. Jesus does not condemn the zeal of the scribes and the Pharisees in making proselytes, that is, worshipers, or converts. Paul was a Pharisee of the most zealous type. When converted, his zeal never flagged, carrying him to all parts of the known world, through much peril and suffering, to win souls to Christ. Jesus Himself traveled to and fro from Bethany to Sidon, and from beyond Jordan to the coast, seeking and saving the lost. It was the hypocritical example and influence of the Pharisees over converts that Jesus condemned.

3. The avarice of the Pharisees is made plain in this twofold denunciation. The gold of the temple and the gift on the altar had commercial value. Taking advantage of religious scruples for gain is one of the most wicked methods of the hypocrite. Jesus encountered it on the occasion when He drove the money changers and the stock dealers from the temple.

4. Jesus does not here denounce scrupulous painstaking to tithe the smallest income, as is shown by the next expression. We cannot be too particular to render to God every whit that belongs to Him as the special portion of our income that He has asked. Our failure is, rather, to grow careless in this matter. But along with this, Jesus would have us heedful of things of greater moment as affecting the building of character — the exercising of the Christlike qualities of impartial judgment, of plenteous mercy, and of faith that works by love.

5. The Revised Version states it thus: "Ye blind guides, that strain out the gnat, and swallow the camel!"
