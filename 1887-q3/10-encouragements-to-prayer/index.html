Lesson 2 · September 3, 1887

Encouragements to Prayer

PDFShare
  1. 1.How does God encourage Christians to pray?—By promises and by examples in which prayers have been answered.
  2. 2.What precious promise is made in Matt. 7:7, 8?
  3. 3.In what other place is the same promise recorded? Luke 11:9.
  4. 4.Mention some conditions of this promise. Matt. 21:22; Mark 11:24; 1 John 3:22.
  5. 5.What promise does the Saviour make to his disciples? John 14:13, 14.
  6. 6.What does David say concerning his own experience? Ps. 3:4.
  7. 7.How does he express his confidence in the promises of God? Ps. 6:9.
  8. 8.What strong assurance does he manifest? Ps. 66:19.
  9. 9.What does the Saviour say in addressing his Father? John 11:42.
  10. 10.How may it be shown that David was heard in personal matters as well as in the affairs of state? Ps. 34:4; 40:1-3.
  11. 11.How do you show that God will hear the prayers of those in the humblest walks of life, as well as the prayers of kings and princes? Ps. 34:6.
  12. 12.What other encouragement is given to the humble and the oppressed? Ps. 10:17.
  13. 13.What promise is made to the destitute? Ps. 102:17.
  14. 14.What strong assurance is given to those who are in trouble? Ps. 50:15.
  15. 15.What encouragement is given to those who lack wisdom? James 1:5.
  16. 16.To what extent may this promise be fulfilled? Jer. 33:3.
  17. 17.To whom is salvation promised? Rom. 10:13.
  18. 18.What must be implied in a prayer that secures salvation?—Such depth of sincerity as would lead to repentance, faith, and obedience.
  19. 19.What promise is made to those who unite in social prayer? Matt. 18:19.
  20. 20.What must be observed in order to secure this promise? 1 John 5:14, 15.
  21. 21.When a people shall be generous to the poor, kind to the afflicted, and merciful to the oppressed, what assurance have they from God?—"Then shalt thou call, and the Lord shall answer; thou shalt cry, and he shall say, Here I am." Isa. 58:9.
  22. 22.What will be said to those who shall be refined as silver is refined, and tried as gold is tried? Zech. 13:9.
  23. 23.Unto whom must all flesh come? Ps. 65:2.
  24. 24.How will they be received? Lam. 3:25.
  25. 25.What does the prophet say to those who have repented of their sins, and turned to the Lord?—"He will be very gracious unto thee at the voice of thy cry; when he shall hear it, he shall answer thee." Isa. 30:19.
  26. 26.Give some examples in which prayers have been answered.

Notes

Our Father Invites Us to Come.—Why should people be so shy of God? He is doing everything to woo and win them, and to secure their confidence. So much has he done, that he asks (and I cannot answer) what he could have done more. He waits on his throne of grace to be gracious to them, but they come not near to him. He even calls to them to come to him, using, too, the language of most affectionate address: "Son, my son;" but they respond not, "Abba, Father." It is strange they should treat this Father so. They treat no other father so. What child does not, in the morning, salute his father? And what father does not expect the salutation of each child, as he comes into his presence? Oh, yes, we love our father who is on earth; and we remember with gratitude the favors he does us. And does the Father of our spirits, the Giver of every good gift, deserve no daily notice from us, no affectionate salutation, no grateful recognition of indebtedness to him? I am certain he expects it, for he says, "A son honoreth his father; if then I be a father, where is mine honor?" He claims to be a father; and O how well he has established that claim! Truly he is a father, and "like as a father pitieth his children, so the Lord pitieth" his. And to the compassion of the father, he adds the tender care and untiring mindfulness of the mother. "Can a woman," he asks, "forget her sucking child?" She may, he says, but he will not forget his people. How strange it is that men will not go to the closet to meet and to pray to such a Father.—Nevins.
