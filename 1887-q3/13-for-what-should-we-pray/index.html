Lesson 5 · September 24, 1887

For What Should We Pray?

PDFShare
  1. 1.For what does David pray in Psalms 25:11?
  2. 2.What is implied by the last words of this verse?—That David had a deep sense of his own sinfulness.
  3. 3.In what words does Job pray for the convicting power of the Spirit of God? Job 13:23.
  4. 4.As David saw himself a sinner, and entangled in the net of Satan, to whom did he look for deliverance? Ps. 25:15.
  5. 5.How does he describe his feelings? Verse 16.
  6. 6.Was he so cast down by outward troubles, or was it troubles of the heart? Verse 17.
  7. 7.How did he expect to be delivered from this state of misery? Verse 18.
  8. 8.How far back do David's convictions take him? Verse 7.
  9. 9.Of what depths of sin did he confess himself guilty? Ps. 51:14.
  10. 10.In what words did he plead for the mercy of God? Verse 1.
  11. 11.Unto what state did he hope to be brought? Verse 8.
  12. 12.How did he hope to be brought into this state of rejoicing? Verse 9.
  13. 13.How are the longings of the Christian heart set forth in Ps. 85:7?
  14. 14.In whose name did David pray for salvation? Ps. 54:1.
  15. 15.In whose name do we now plead for the same? John 14:6; Acts 4:10, 12; 1 Tim. 2:5.
  16. 16.What is the sentiment of every penitent heart? Ps. 51:2.
  17. 17.If the petitioner would receive an answer to such prayer, how should he be prepared for making his petition? Verse 3.
  18. 18.What state of purity should we pray for? V. 7.
  19. 19.How did David pray for the regenerating power of God to be exercised upon him? Verse 10.
  20. 20.When God has forgiven our sins, what should be our prayer? Ps. 25:5.
  21. 21.With what determination should such a prayer be made? Ps. 86:11.
  22. 22.With what view should we pray for an understanding of God's word? Ps. 119:33, 34.

  24. 24.What did David pray God to teach him by his good spirit? Ps. 143:10.
  25. 25.How far did he expect to be led by that Spirit?—Into the land of uprightness. Last part of same verse.
  26. 26.What assurance have we that God will give true wisdom to those who ask for it in sincerity? Jas. 1:5.

Notes

Few exercises in connection with Sabbath-school work are more profitable than taking a bird's-eye view of the ground over which we have passed. To this end we will briefly notice the lessons we have already had on the subject of prayer.

Lesson 1.—Prayer is prompted by a sense of our need and a consequent yearning after God. When these yearning desires are expressed in words, they constitute oral prayer; when unexpressed, mental prayer. They are often too deep to be uttered. Rom. 8:26. David at times tries to find expression for such yearnings. Ps. 42:1; 63:1. Job also manifests strong desires to draw near to God that he might present his case, and learn the Divine will concerning him.

God, through his Spirit, has given many sweet invitations to prayer. Hosea 14:2; Phil. 4:6; 1 Tim. 2:8; Isa. 55:6; 1 Chron. 16:8. He also warns us against neglect of prayer (Prov. 1:28), and exhorts to faithfulness and constancy in the exercise. 1 Chron. 16:11; Col. 4:2; 1 Thess. 5:17; Luke 18:1-5.

Our Saviour recommends prayer as a safe-guard against temptation (Matt. 26:41; Luke 22:46), and urges it especially upon those who are to live in the last generation, and see heaven and earth pass away. Luke 21:36; Mark 13:33. Such are to be sober and watch unto prayer; for the wrath of God will be poured out upon the kingdoms that do not call upon the name of the Lord. 1 Pet. 4:7; Ps. 79:6.

Lesson 2.—Encouragements to prayer are numerous both in promises and in prayers that have been answered. Matt. 7:7, 8; Luke 11:9; John 14:13, 14. These promises, however, are based upon the conditions of faith and obedience. Matt. 21:22; Mark 11:24; 1 Jno. 3:22. In the book of Psalms we have many strong assurances that God will both hear and answer prayer (Ps. 3:4; 6:9; 66:19; 34:4; 40:1-3); and the Saviour says: "I knew that thou hearest me always." John 11:42. But God is as ready to hear the prayers of the oppressed, the poor, the destitute, and those that are in trouble, as he is to hear the prayers of kings and princes. Ps. 34:6; 10:17; 102:17; 50:15. Those that call upon the Lord for wisdom have the promise that they shall receive liberally, and shall be shown great and mighty things. Jas. 1:5; Jer. 33:3.

All who would obtain salvation must call upon the name of the Lord (Rom. 10:13); and those who join in social prayer, have the promise that whatever they shall unite in asking, shall be given them if they ask according to his will. Matt. 18:19; 1 John 5:14, 15. The Lord is especially ready to hear the prayer of those who are generous to the poor, kind to the afflicted, and merciful to the oppressed (Isa. 58:9), and allows a near kinship to those who shall be refined as silver is refined, and tried as gold is tried. Zech. 13:9.

Indeed, the Lord is a prayer-hearing God, and unto him must all flesh come. Ps. 65:2. He is "good to them that wait for him, and to the soul that seeketh him." Lam. 3:25. To such it may be said, "He will be very gracious unto thee at the voice of thy cry; when he shall hear it, he shall answer thee." Isa. 30:19.

Lesson 3.—To the righteous the Lord has promised that his eyes shall be upon them, and that his ears shall be open unto their cry. But the wicked shall cry in vain, unless they humble themselves and forsake their sins. John 9:31; Prov. 15:29; 1 Pet. 3:12.

God will not regard the prayer of vain persons, of the hypocrite, nor of those who cherish iniquity (Job 35:13; 27:8, 9; Ps. 66:18); and of those who turn away from his law he has said that even their prayer shall be abomination. The consequences of refusing to heed God's law are set forth in Zech. 7:11-14.

The Lord will not hear the prayers of those who are guilty of bloodshed, or of those who cherish hatred or iniquity in the heart; for the way of the wicked is an abomination unto the Lord. Isa. 1:15; 1 John 3:15; Isa. 59:1, 2; Prov. 15:9. But to the sinner God has given gracious counsel and a precious promise in Isa. 1:16-19. Further assurance is given in 2 Chron. 7:14; etc.

Lesson 4.—The duty of confessing is set forth both by precept and by example. Num. 5:6, 7; Dan. 9:3-21; Prov. 28:13. Our prayers should be addressed to God the Father and in the name of Jesus Christ our Mediator. Ps. 5:1, 2; Phil. 4:6; John 14:6; 16:23. But in order to make prayer effectual, we must not only confess our sins, and present our requests in the name of our Redeemer, but we must exercise faith, and practice obedience. Heb. 11:6; Mark 11:24; John 15:7. We must also come in a humble, forgiving spirit. 2 Chron. 7:14; Luke 18:9-14; 22:42; Mark 11:25, 26.

But in order to prevail in prayer, we must seek God with the whole heart. Deut. 4:29; Jer. 29:13; Ps. 119:10, 145. We must also be patient and persevering in prayer. Ps. 40:1; Luke 18:1-7; Gen. 32:9-12, 24-26; Dan. 10:2, 4, 5, 12. Having sought to fulfill all these conditions of acceptable prayer, we should "come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need;" for "we have not an High Priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities, but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin." Heb. 4:16, 15.

Lesson 5.—The sinner should pray for thorough conviction, that he may understand his sins and know his true condition before God. Even those who have been long in the way need to pray for a deeper work of this kind. Job 13:23; Ps. 25:11. When David found himself in deep affliction on account of his sins, he turned to God and sought relief by asking forgiveness. Ps. 25:15-18. David seems to have been very thorough; for his convictions take him back even to the sins of his youth, and he confesses himself guilty of murder. Ps. 25:7; 51:14. He pleads very humbly for the mercy of God; and, sinful as he is, believes that God will hear his prayer, since it comes from a broken and a contrite heart. Ps. 51:1, 17.

He confidently hoped to be brought into a state of rejoicing through the blotting out of all his iniquities. Verses 8, 9. He recognized that the power of God was necessary to give him a clean heart and a right spirit. Verse 10. Like David, the prayer of every true Christian should be, "Wash me thoroughly from mine iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin;" and like him, we should be ready to acknowledge our transgressions. Ps. 51:2, 3. Through the blood of Christ we may become whiter than snow. Verse 7. Having obtained forgiveness, we should pray to be led into all the truth of God. Ps. 25:5. And this prayer should be made with a full determination to walk in the truth. Ps. 86:11; 119:33, 34. Even the inspired psalmist felt the need of God's help in understanding his word. Verses 73, 125. He prayed for power to do the will of God, to be quickened by the Holy Spirit, and led by it clear through to the "land of uprightness." Ps. 143:10, 11.

We are to pray, then, for conviction, forgiveness, purity of heart, a knowledge of the truth, an understanding of God's word, the power to do his will, and for final salvation through Christ; and we are to come confessing our sins, possessed of a humble, forgiving spirit, yet in the full assurance of faith, and with a strong determination to do all that God requires of us.
