Lesson 12 · March 20, 1920

Doing All Things Through Christ

PDFShare

Lesson Scripture: Phil. 4:10-23

Daily Study Outline

  1. 1.Read the lesson scripture.
  2. 2.Christian contentment, questions 1-3.
  3. 3.Doing through Christ, questions 4-6.
  4. 4.Giving and receiving, questions 7-9.
  5. 5.Christian greetings, questions 10-13.
  6. 6.Paul's work in Rome, questions 14-16.
  7. 7.Review the lesson.

Questions

  1. 1.How did the Philippians bring Paul such joy in the Lord? Phil. 4:10. Note 1.
  2. 2.What great lesson in respect to want had Paul learned? Verse 11. Note 2.
  3. 3.Mention the contrasting conditions under which Paul had learned the secret of contentment. Verse 12.
  4. 4.What general assertion does he make? Verse 13. (Memorize this verse.)
  5. 5.How had the Philippians done well? Verse 14.
  6. 6.What made their ministry of special value? Verses 15, 16.
  7. 7.What was Paul's motive in accepting their gifts? Verse 17.
  8. 8.With what appreciation did Paul receive the things sent him by Epaphroditus? Verse 18.
  9. 9.In return, how did he assure the Philippians their need would be supplied? Verse 19, first part. (Memorize this verse.)
  10. 10.What would be the measure of their supply? Verse 19, last part.
  11. 11.What did he ascribe to God? Verse 20.
  12. 12.What greetings did Paul send the Philippians? Verse 21.
  13. 13.Who especially sent their salutations? Verse 22. Note 3.
  14. 14.Through Christ, what had Paul been able to do for the gospel in Rome? Phil. 1:14. Note 4.
  15. 15.Where did the converts in Cæsar's household take up their work for Christ? Note 5.
  16. 16.With what prayer does Paul close his letter to the Philippians? Phil. 4:23.

Notes

1. This is not the kind of "care" warned against in verse 6. That was overanxiety, worry. This is thoughtfulness, interest in Paul's welfare.

2. This verse should not be interpreted too broadly. Paul is talking here chiefly of his temporal needs. Closely rendered, the verse reads, "I have learned, under whatever circumstances I am, to be satisfied with what I have." In matters of external circumstance and bodily need, Paul had learned the lesson of indifference; but in matters of spiritual and mental attainment, he was never satisfied, but always reaching out for more.

3. "Nowhere could there exist an atmosphere more uncongenial to Christianity than in the Roman court. Nero seemed to have obliterated from his soul the last traces of the divine, and even of the human, and to bear the impress of Satan. His attendants and courtiers were in general of the same character as himself—fierce, debased, and corrupt. To all appearance it would be impossible for Christianity to gain a foothold in the court and palace of Nero.

"Yet in this case, as in so many others, was proved the truth of Paul's assertion that the weapons of his warfare were 'mighty through God to the pulling down of strongholds.' Even in Nero's household, trophies of the cross were won. From the vile attendants of a viler king were gained converts who became sons of God. These were not Christians secretly, but openly. They were not ashamed of their faith."—"Acts of the Apostles," pages 462, 463.

4. "Paul's patience and cheerfulness during his long and unjust imprisonment, his courage and faith, were a continual sermon. His spirit, so unlike the spirit of the world, bore witness that a power higher than that of earth was abiding with him. And by his example, Christians were impelled to greater energy as advocates of the cause from the public labors of which Paul had been withdrawn. In these ways were the apostle's bonds influential, so that when his power and usefulness seemed cut off, and to all appearance he could do the least, then it was that he gathered sheaves for Christ in fields from which he seemed wholly excluded.

"Before the close of that two years' imprisonment, Paul was able to say, 'My bonds in Christ are manifest in all the palace, and in all other places;' and among those who sent greetings to the Philippians he mentions chiefly them 'that are of Cæsar's household.' "—Id., page 464.

5. "Not only were converts won to the truth in Caesar's household, but after their conversion they remained in that household. They did not feel at liberty to abandon their post of duty because their surroundings were no longer congenial. The truth had found them there, and there they remained, by their changed life and character testifying to the transforming power of the new faith."—Id., page 466.
