Lesson 13 · November 19, 1887

Posture in Prayer

PDFShare
  1. 1.What position did Solomon take when he prayed before all the people at the dedication of the temple? 1 Kings 8:22; 2 Chron. 6:12, 13.
  2. 2.What must we conclude from these passages?—That he either stood first and kneeled afterward, or that he stood upon his knees on the scaffold.
  3. 3.How does our Saviour recognize the standing position as acceptable in prayer? Mark 11:25.
  4. 4.How did Abraham's servant pray? Gen. 24:52.
  5. 5.What example did Moses set? Ex. 34:8.
  6. 6.What exhortation did David make? Ps. 95:6.
  7. 7.What seems to be a reasonable conclusion from all these passages?—That the proper position depends upon the occasion and the attendant circumstances.

What Should Accompany Prayer

  1. 8.Where may we find a prayer that consists mainly of praise to God? 1 Chron. 29:10-19.
  2. 9.What prophet made a prayer that has the same peculiarity? Jer. 32:16-22.
  3. 10.What is said of him that offereth praise? Ps. 50:23.
  4. 11.What psalms consist almost wholly of praise? Ps. 103; 104; 106; 111; 112; 113; 117; 138; 145-150.
  5. 12.What would be an appropriate prayer for any Christian? Ps. 51:15.
  6. 13.What should be our constant desire? Ps. 71:8.
  7. 14.What would be the result if all the people would praise the Lord? Ps. 67:5, 6.
  8. 15.What is it a good thing to do? Repeat Ps. 92:1, 2.
  9. 16.What command is given in 1 Chron. 16:8?
  10. 17.Why should we sing unto the Lord? Isa. 12:5.
  11. 18.What exhortation does the Psalmist give? Ps. 95:2.
  12. 19.What is one appropriate way of giving thanks? Ps. 147:7.
  13. 20.What is the express will of God with reference to thanksgiving? 1 Thess. 5:18.
  14. 21.When should thanks be given, and for what? Eph. 5:20.

Supplementary Questions

  1. 1.Tell briefly how David cried to God for deliverance from trouble; and what he asked for. Ps. 143:6-11.
  2. 2.Relate some of the experiences of Moses when he was in great trouble. Ex. 17:4-6.
  3. 3.What was David's experience in affliction? Ps. 118:5; 18:6.
  4. 4.What words of comfort are spoken to the afflicted and the poor? Ps. 22:24; 40:17.
  5. 5.Give a brief account of some of the experiences of the children of Israel. Ps. 107:11-14.
  6. 6.From whose experience may we learn that it is better to trust in God than to oppose our enemies in our own strength? Ps. 143:9; 59:1-4, 9.
  7. 7.What wonderful example has God given of his power to protect his people? Isa. 37:1-36.

Compendium

In the account of Solomon's prayer at the dedication of the temple, he is spoken of both as standing and kneeling. He might have stood first, and kneeled afterwards, or an upright position on his knees might have been called standing, in distinction from kneeling and bowing the face to the earth.

Our Saviour recognizes the standing position as acceptable, in the words, "And when ye stand praying, forgive, if ye have ought against any." Abraham's servant "worshiped the Lord, bowing himself to the earth." Moses "bowed his head to the earth and worshiped;" and David says, "Let us worship and bow down; let us kneel before the Lord our Maker." From all these passages we conclude that any of the positions therein described may be acceptable.

Some of the recorded prayers of holy men consist almost entirely of praise to God, and the Lord himself says, "Whoso offereth praise, glorifieth me." Many of the psalms are wholly, or almost wholly, made up of praise. In view of these examples, a Christian might well say, "O Lord, open thou my lips, and my mouth shall show forth thy praise;" "Let my mouth be filled with thy praise and with thy honor all the day." If all the people would praise the Lord, we have the promise that the earth would yield her increase, and that God would bless us. David says, "It is a good thing to give thanks unto the Lord, to sing praises unto thy name, O Most High; to show forth thy lovingkindness in the morning, and thy faithfulness every night." The prophet Isaiah says, "Sing unto the Lord; for he hath done excellent things;" and the psalmist exhorts: "Let us come before his presence with thanksgiving, and make a joyful noise unto him with psalms;" "sing unto the Lord with thanksgiving, sing praise upon the harp unto our God." The will of God is thus expressed by the apostle Paul: "In everything give thanks, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you;" "Giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ."

Condensed Analysis

  1. 1.Examples of different positions in prayer.
  2. 2.Examples of psalms and other prayers that consist mainly of praise to God.
  3. 3.Exhortations to praise, both in prayer and song.
  4. 4.The giving of thanks enjoined in connection with prayer.
