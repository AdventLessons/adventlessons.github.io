Lesson 7 · August 15, 1942

Known by Their Fruits

PDFShare

Lesson Scripture: James 3:10-18.

Memory Verse: "The wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be entreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy." James 3:17.

Study Help: "Steps to Christ," pages 110-118.

The Lesson

The Fruitage Tells

  1. 1.After speaking of an unruly tongue, what common inconsistency in life does James mention? What comment is made? James 3:10.

Notes

"These things ought not so to be." The Twentieth Century New Testament translates this, "It is not right that this should be so." It is out of place, contrary to Christian doctrine and conduct.

  1. 2.How is the incongruity of such a life illustrated? James 3:11.

Notes

In no instance does a fountain send forth sweet water and bitter. If two streams come into contact, they unite and the bitter contaminates the sweet, but from the same source both sweet and bitter cannot come. No such inconsistency is possible in nature, nor should it be in human experience, especially among Christians.

  1. 3.How is this evil further illustrated? Verse 12.

Notes

Three symbols are given to show the inconsistency of living such a contradictory life: (a) a fig tree; (b) a vine; (c) a fountain. A fig tree cannot produce olive berries; a vine cannot bear figs; a fountain cannot produce fresh water and salt. In the operations of nature these things are impossible. All these illustrations are used to show the utter impossibility of depravity and virtue dwelling in the same heart, or of the love of God dwelling in the heart when hatred and malice are cherished.

  1. 4.By what illustration did Jesus set forth this same truth? Matt. 7:15-20.
  2. 5.What does Solomon say concerning the power of the tongue? Prov. 18:21.
  3. 6.What do our words reveal? Luke 6:45; Matt. 12:33-35.

Results of Worldly Wisdom

  1. 7.By what are true wisdom and understanding revealed? James 3:13.

Notes

Proud, haughty, disdainful, unholy persons may pass for great scholars, and have the reputation of being very learned, but such do not have true wisdom. This wisdom comes from above and those who possess it reveal in their humble, godly lives the character of their Teacher.

  1. 8.Through whom was the true light made known to the world? John 1:4; 9:5.

Notes

That "the life" is "the light" is true of Christ's followers also. "Ye are the light of the world." We are living epistles "known and read of all men." Our words will have but little weight unless they are emphasized by a consistent Christian life.

  1. 9.If strife is in our hearts, what are we urged not to do? James 3:14.

Notes

"If ye be under the influence of an unkind, fierce, and contemptuous spirit, even while attempting or pretending to defend true religion, do not boast. . . . Ye have no religion, and no true wisdom; and to profess either is to lie against the truth."—Dr. Adam Clarke.

  1. 10.From whence is such wisdom? Verse 15.
  2. 11.What fruitage follows earthly wisdom? Verse 16.
  3. 12.What led the people and the chief priests to deliver the Saviour to Pilate? Mark 15:10.
  4. 13.What comparison does Solomon draw between a sound heart and one filled with envy? Prov. 14:30.

The Wisdom From Above

  1. 14.How is the wisdom from heaven described? James 3:17.

Notes

"You need not go to the ends of the earth for wisdom, for God is near. . . . He can sharpen the intellect. He can give tact and skill. Put your talents into the work, ask God for wisdom, and it will be given you."—Christ's Object Lessons, page 146.

  1. 15.How is the fruit of righteousness sown? By whom? Verse 18.

Notes

"Sown in peace." Fields are not sown with grain in the tumult of a mob, or in a battle. Nothing is more peaceful than a farmer going forth with measured tread to scatter seed over his field.

"The whole effect, therefore, of religion is to produce peace. It is all peace—peace in its origin and in its results; in the heart of the individual, and in society; on earth, and in heaven."—Barnes.
