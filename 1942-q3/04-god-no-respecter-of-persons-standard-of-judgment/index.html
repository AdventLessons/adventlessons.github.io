Lesson 4 · July 25, 1942

God No Respecter of Persons; Standard of Judgment

PDFShare

Lesson Scripture: James 2:1-13.

Memory verse

Study Help: "Patriarchs and Prophets," pages 207-210.

The Lesson

Natural Trends to Be Avoided

  1. 1.With what exhortation is this chapter introduced? James 2:1.

Notes

The Revised Version, margin, reads, "My brethren, do ye, in accepting persons, hold the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory?" None who profess the pure and undefiled religion of our Lord should countenance any distinction, or manifest any partiality, among members of the church, because of difference in wealth or social standing. We are all one in Christ Jesus.

  1. 2.What description is given of the rich man and the poor man? Verse 2.
  2. 3.How do men ordinarily relate themselves to these two classes? Verse 3.
  3. 4.What does such partiality reveal? Verse 4.

Notes

"Judges with evil thoughts" (Revised Version), "that is, judges who reason wickedly; who, in effect, say in your hearts, we will espouse the cause of the rich, because they can befriend us; we will neglect that of the poor, because they cannot help us, nor have they power to hurt us."—Dr. Adam Clarke.

  1. 5.How did Jesus teach and exemplify in His life the truth regarding God's impartiality to men? Matt. 5:45; 11:19.

Notes

"No distinction on account of nationality, race, or caste, is recognized by God. He is the Maker of all mankind. All men are of one family by creation; and all are one through redemption. Christ came to demolish every wall of partition, to throw open every compartment of the temple, that every soul may have free access to God. . . . In Christ there is neither Jew nor Greek, bond nor free. All are brought nigh by His precious blood."—Christ's Object Lessons, page 386.

God No Respecter of Persons

  1. 6.What lesson was taught the apostle Peter concerning the equality of men in God's sight? Acts 10:34, 35.
  2. 7.What should be our attitude toward all men? To what does respect of persons lead? Prov. 24:23; 28:21.

Notes

God is not a respecter of persons. God values those traits of character which indicate a love for that which is good and loving and true. "Unsanctified partiality for special ones who may please the fancy, to the neglect of others who are conscientious and God-fearing, and in His sight of more value, is offensive to God. That which God values, we should value. The ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, He regards of higher value than external beauty, outward adornment, riches, or worldly honor."—Testimonies, vol. 3, p. 24.

  1. 8.To whom does God promise His kingdom? James 2:5; Matt. 5:3, 5, 10.

Notes

These promises indicate that character alone makes a man worthy of a place in God's kingdom. Neither wealth, nor position, nor power makes it possible for one to enter that holy land. "Not many wise men after the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called: but God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty." Therefore, "he that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord." 1 Cor. 1:26, 27, 31.

  1. 9.What charge is made against those who respect persons? What is the customary attitude of the rich? James 2:6, 7.

Fulfilling the Royal Law

  1. 10.How is the royal law fulfilled? Verse 8. (Compare with Matt. 22:35-40.)

Notes

We are here reminded of that supreme law, before which all stand equal. There will be no rich or poor, no high or low. Right and wrong, guilt and innocence alone, will be considered. It is the "royal law" because it is the law of the great King, before which all must bow. By it all differences must finally be settled.

  1. 11.What do those who have respect of persons do? Of what does their attitude convict them? James 2:9.
  2. 12.Into conflict with what great body of guiding principles does the breaking of one command of God's law bring the transgressor? Verse 10.

Notes

The moral law "is that revelation of the divine will which relates to the duties men owe both to their Maker and to their fellow men, or neighbors, as explained in Luke 10:33-37. This law is spiritual and perfect, extending to all the inward creations and outward actions of men, and can never be changed or annulled."—Binney's Theological Compend, page 153.

One step in disobedience may lead to complete rebellion on the part of the transgressor, unless in repentance he turns back to God for pardon and cleansing. Man's only safety is in yielding himself fully to follow the divine pattern, and in daily becoming one with God in every life purpose.

  1. 13.How is this illustrated? Verse 11.
  2. 14.What are we exhorted to do in our relationship to God's law? Verse 12.
  3. 15.What may the ones who show no mercy expect in God's hour of judgment? Verse 13.
