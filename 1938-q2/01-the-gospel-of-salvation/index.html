Lesson 1 · April 2, 1938

The Gospel of Salvation

PDFShare

Daily Lesson Study: Place a check mark in the proper space.

Memory verse

Study Help: "Patriarchs and Prophets," pp. 63-70.

The Lesson

Introduction

The word "gospel" means "good news." "Glad tidings, especially the good news concerning Christ, the kingdom of God, and salvation."—Webster. Christ's command, "Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature," is given to every believer in the gospel. The gospel is as necessary to a man in darkest heathenism as it is to the one living by us. "If every church member were a living missionary, the gospel would speedily be proclaimed in all countries, to all peoples, nations, and tongues."—"Testimonies," vol. 9, p. 32.

Eternal Life the Fruit of Perfect Obedience

  1. 1.What was man's condition when created? Gen. 1:26, first part.

Notes

"Man was to bear God's image, both in outward resemblance and in character. Christ alone is 'the express image' of the Father; but man was formed in the likeness of God. His nature was in harmony with the will of God. His mind was capable of comprehending divine things. His affections were pure; his appetites and passions were under the control of reason. He was holy and happy in bearing the image of God, and in perfect obedience to His will."—"Patriarchs and Prophets," p. 45.

  1. 2.How only could man remain in this perfect condition? Ans. By obeying his Creator's commands. Gen. 2:16, 17.

Notes

"God placed man under law, as an indispensable condition of his very existence. He was a subject of the divine government, and there can be no government without law. God might have created man without the power to transgress His law; He might have withheld the hand of Adam from touching the forbidden fruit; but in that case man would have been, not a free moral agent, but a mere automaton. Without freedom of choice, his obedience would not have been voluntary, but forced. There could have been no development of character. Such a course would have been contrary to God's plan in dealing with the inhabitants of other worlds. It would have been unworthy of man as an intelligent being, and would have sustained Satan's charge of God's arbitrary rule."—"Patriarchs and Prophets," p. 49.

  1. 3.What is sin? 1 John 5:17; 3:4.
  2. 4.How many have sinned? Rom. 3:23; 5:12.
  3. 5.What is the wages of sin? Rom. 6:23, first part.

The Plan of Redemption

  1. 6.What provision has God made to save man from the consequences of sin? John 3:16; Rom. 8:38, 39.

Notes

"The broken law of God demanded the life of the sinner. In all the universe there was but one who could, in behalf of man, satisfy its claims. Since the divine law is as sacred as God Himself, only one equal with God could make atonement for its transgression. None but Christ could redeem fallen man from the curse of the law, and bring him again into harmony with heaven."—"Patriarchs and Prophets," p. 63.

  1. 7.To how many was the message of peace to go which was proclaimed at our Saviour's advent? Luke 2:10-14.
  2. 8.How was Christ empowered for His work? Acts 10:38.
  3. 9.In what words did Jesus describe His work? What else was He to do besides preaching? Luke 4:18, 19.

Notes

"From Him flowed a stream of healing power, and in body and mind and soul men were made whole. . . . Where He had passed, the objects of His compassion were rejoicing in health, and making trial of their new-found powers."—"Ministry of Healing," pp. 17-19.

In Christ Our Character Is Perfected

  1. 10.What is God's standard of character for man? Matt. 5:48.

Notes

"Christ reaches us where we are. He took our nature and overcame, that we through taking His nature might overcome. Made 'in the likeness of sinful flesh,' He lived a sinless life. Now by His divinity He lays hold upon the throne of heaven, while by His humanity He reaches us. He bids us by faith in Him attain to the glory of the character of God. Therefore are we to be perfect, even as our 'Father which is in heaven is perfect' "—"The Desire of Ages," p. 312.

  1. 11.Through whom is this perfection to be obtained? 1 Cor. 1:30; Col. 2:10.
  2. 12.What part has faith in the work of the gospel? Rom. 1:17.
  3. 13.Whence comes faith? Rom. 12:3; Eph. 2:8.

Notes

"Through faith in Christ, every deficiency of character may be supplied, every defilement cleansed, every fault corrected, every excellence developed."—"Education," p. 257.

Citizenship in God's Kingdom

  1. 14.How may men become citizens of the kingdom of God? John 1:12; 2 Cor. 5:19.
  2. 15.What did Christ leave with His followers as one of the fundamental principles of His kingdom? John 14:27.
  3. 16.As Christ was leaving His disciples to return to His Father, what command did He give them? Mark 16:15.

Lesson Outline

I. Eternal Life the Fruit of Perfect Obedience

  1. 1.Man was created to have dominion: (1) over himself; (2) over all the earth. Gen. 1:26.
  2. 2.To eat of the tree of life perfect obedience is required. Gen. 2:16, 17.
  3. 3.Transgression of God's commands is sin. 1 John 5:17; 3:4.
  4. 4.Because of Adam's transgression all are born in sin. Rom. 3:23; 5:12.
  5. 5.Therefore all are under sentence of death. Rom. 6:23.

II. The Plan of Redemption

  1. 1.God sacrificed His Son to save sinners. John 3:16; Rom. 8:38, 39.
  2. 2.Every troubled heart may have the joy and peace of heaven. Luke 2:10-14.
  3. 3.God was in Christ in His ministry of salvation. Acts 10:38.
  4. 4.Christ brings healing power to body and soul. Luke 4:18, 19.

III. In Christ Our Character Is Perfected

  1. 1.Perfection of character is God's standard. Matt. 5:48.
  2. 2.The power of the gospel regenerates the heart. 1 Cor. 1:30; Col. 2:10.
  3. 3.Faith is the hand that lays hold of God's promises. Rom. 1:17.
  4. 4.Saving faith is the gift of God. Rom. 12:3; Eph. 2:8.

IV. Citizenship in God's Kingdom

  1. 1.Sinners become children of God by repenting and believing. John 1:12; 2 Cor. 5:19.
  2. 2.Troubled hearts have peace in a rebellious world. John 14:27.
  3. 3.Believers in Christ are to witness for Him. Mark 16:15.
