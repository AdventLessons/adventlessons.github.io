Paul at Thessalonica and Berea

Lesson Scripture: Acts 17:1-15.

Memory verse

The Narrative

  1. 1.What seaport of Macedonia was next visited by Paul and those laboring with him? Acts 17:1. Note 1.
  2. 2.What custom did Paul follow here? Out of what did he reason? Verse 2. Note 2.
  3. 3.What great truth did he allege from the Scriptures? Verse 3. Note 3.
  4. 4.What was the result of his preaching the Word? Verse 4.
  5. 5.How did the success of the apostles affect the unbelieving Jews? What did they do? Verse 5.
  6. 6.With what did the mob charge the apostles? Verses 6-8.
  7. 7.What disposition was finally made of them? Verse 9.
  8. 8.To what place did the brethren send Paul and Silas? On arriving at Berea, what did they do? Verse 10.
  9. 9.What was the attitude of the Bereans toward the Word which was spoken to them? Verse 11. Note 4.
  10. 10.How did the enemy seek to hinder the work at Berea? Verse 13.
  11. 11.What steps did the brethren take to protect Paul? Who remained behind to instruct further the believers? Verse 14.
  12. 12.To what city was Paul brought? What message did he send to Silas and Timothy? Verse 15.

Lessons for Us

  1. 1.Power attends the word of God when spoken in faith. 1 Thess. 1:5.
  2. 2.Much trial and affliction are sometimes experienced in establishing the work of the gospel. 1 Thess. 1:6; 2:2.
  3. 3.Those who are Christians should give the light to others. 1 Thess. 1:7, 8. Note 5.
  4. 4.Those who preach the gospel may sometimes be looked upon as traitors. This was involved in the charge that the apostles claimed another king than Cæsar. Acts 17:7.
  5. 5.It is sometimes wise when persecution is manifested in one place to go quietly to another. Verse 10.
  6. 6.The Word is the standard by which to test what is being taught. Verses 11, 12.

Notes

1. "Thessalonica.— A city of Macedonia, now called Salonica."—"Twentieth Century Dictionary."

Notice the words, "they had passed." (Compare Acts 17:1 and 16:10-13, 16, 17.) It is evident from this that Luke is not with them and writes as a historian and not as an eyewitness. This perhaps explains why the narrative of events at Thessalonica is quite general, while that of Philippi is more in detail. Luke does not appear to be with Paul again until the time of his second visit to Macedonia on his third missionary tour. See Acts 20:4-6. Timotheus however seems to have rejoined Paul and Silas either at Thessalonica or Berea.

2. While it might be inferred from Paul's statement that he preached "three Sabbath days," that his stay was limited to this time, many Bible writers think that he remained a longer time.

3. Paul, as a great gospel preacher, did not dwell upon the social and ethical questions of his time, but proclaimed with power the vital truth of Christianity from the Scriptures. He preached in verity the word of God.

4. "The length of St. Paul's stay in the city is quite uncertain. From the fact that the Bereans were occupied 'daily' in searching the Scriptures for arguments to establish or confute the apostle's doctrine, we conclude that he remained there several days at least. From his own assertion in his first letter to the Thessalonians, that, at the time when he had been recently taken away from them, he was very anxious, and used every effort to revisit them, we cannot doubt that he lingered as long as possible in the neighborhood of Thessalonica."—"Life and Epistles of the Apostle Paul," Conybeare and Howson, page 294.

5. Thessalonica was a seaport of great commercial importance at that time. As news from there would be carried throughout a wide area, it is quite probable that the disorderly demonstrations, as well as the miraculous demonstration in the deliverance of Paul and Silas, were very widely known.
