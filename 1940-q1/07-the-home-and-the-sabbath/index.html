Lesson 7 · February 17, 1940

The Home and the Sabbath

PDFShare

This diagram for daily lesson study records only. Place a check mark in proper space.

Memory verse

Study Helps: "Patriarchs and Prophets," pp. 47, 48, 296; "Education," pp. 250-252.

Seed Thought: "The Sabbath should be made so interesting to our families that its weekly return will be hailed with joy. In no better way can parents exalt and honor the Sabbath than by devising means to impart proper instruction to their families, and interesting them in spiritual things, giving them correct views of the character of God, and what He requires of us in order to perfect Christian characters and attain to eternal life. Parents, make the Sabbath a delight, that your children may look forward to it, and have a welcome in their hearts for it."—"Testimonies," vol. 2, p. 585.

The Lesson

The Sabbath of the Commandment

  1. 1.Repeat the Sabbath commandment. Ex. 20:8-11.
  2. 2.How are we to keep the Sabbath day? What is the force of the word "remember"? Ex. 20:8.

Notes

We are to remember the Sabbath to keep it holy. Throughout the entire week we are so to plan our secular affairs that, when the Sabbath comes to us, we will be ready to receive it and not desecrate its sanctity because of our forgetfulness. In this sense the fourth commandment, like the other nine, covers all the transactions of life during the entire seven days of the week.

  1. 3.When does the Sabbath begin and end? Lev. 23:32, last clause; Mark 1:32.

Notes

There is no more fitting way to begin the Sabbath than, as its sacred hours draw near, to assemble the family, and, after singing, and reading God's blessed word, engage in a season of prayer, invoking pardon for sin, and asking for a Sabbath blessing. Then as its closing moments draw nigh, offer a prayer of thanksgiving, and ask for strength for the coming week.

Preparation for the Sabbath

  1. 4.What is the day before the Sabbath called? Ex. 16:5; Mark 15:42.

Notes

"Friday is the day of preparation. Time can then be devoted to making the necessary preparation for the Sabbath, and to thinking and conversing about it. Nothing which will in the sight of Heaven be regarded as a violation of the holy Sabbath should be left unsaid or undone, to be said or done upon the Sabbath."—"Testimonies," vol. 2, pp. 702, 703.

  1. 5.What preparation should be made for the Sabbath? Ex. 16:4, 5, 23.

Proper Sabbath Observance

  1. 6.What miracle was wrought to teach proper reverence for the Sabbath? Ex. 16:19, 20, 24-29.
  2. 7.How was the Sabbath observed by the New Testament believers? Luke 23:55, 56.

Lawful to Do Good

  1. 8.What example of Christ's indicates the true spirit of Sabbath observance? Matt. 12:10-13.
  2. 9.What else is included in proper Sabbath observance besides refraining from work? Isa. 58:13, 14.

Notes

"God requires not only that we refrain from physical labor upon the Sabbath, but that the mind be disciplined to dwell upon sacred themes. The fourth commandment is virtually transgressed by conversing upon worldly things, or by engaging in light and trifling conversation. Talking upon anything or everything which may come into the mind, is speaking our own words."—"Testimonies," vol. 2, p. 703.

  1. 10.What is the character of God's law? Rom. 7:12, 14.
  2. 11.What condition of heart is essential for properly keeping the Sabbath? Ezek. 36:26, 27.

Blessing to the Obedient

  1. 12.What blessings are promised to the obedient? Ps. 19:7-11.
  2. 13.What further significance is attached to the Sabbath day? Ezek. 20:12.

Notes

" 'If thou turn away thy foot from the Sabbath, from doing thy pleasure on My holy day, and call the Sabbath a delight, the holy of the Lord, honorable; . . . then shalt thou delight thyself in the Lord.' To all who receive the Sabbath as a sign of Christ's creative and redeeming power, it will be a delight. Seeing Christ in it, they delight themselves in Him. The Sabbath points them to the works of creation as an evidence of His mighty power in redemption. While it calls to mind the lost peace of Eden, it tells of peace restored through the Saviour. And every object in nature repeats His invitation, 'Come unto Me, all ye that labor and are heavy-laden, and I will give you rest.' "—"The Desire of Ages," pp. 288, 289.
