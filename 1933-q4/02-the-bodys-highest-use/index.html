Lesson 2 · October 14, 1933

The Body's Highest Use

PDFShare

Daily Lesson Study: Place a check mark in the proper space.

Offering Record: Fill in each space with the weekly record.

Memory verse

Key Thought: "The very flesh in which the soul tabernacles, and through which it works, is the Lord's."—"Testimonies to Ministers," p. 456.

The Lesson

  1. 1.What is the measure of love which we should give to God? Luke 10:27.

Notes

"To love Him, the infinite, the omniscient One, with the whole strength, and mind, and heart, means the highest development of every power. It means that in the whole being—the body, the mind, as well as the soul—the image of God is to be restored."—"Education," p. 16.

  1. 2.Where is the efficient and victorious power of God wrought? Phil. 2:13; Heb. 13:20, 21.

Notes

It is within the human being, through man's faculties, functions, and powers, that God, by Christ and the Holy Spirit, is making Himself known.

  1. 3.How literally is the indwelling of Christ identified with one's Christian living? Gal. 2:20.

Notes

The three words "in the flesh" are often omitted when this verse is quoted, yet these very words emphasize how literally Christ is to possess and use the human being in the manifestation of the divine life.

  1. 4.How is the thought again expressed that it is the body that is the place of Christ's indwelling? 2 Cor. 4:10, 11.

Notes

"Christ is to live in His human agents, and work through their faculties, and act through their capabilities."—"Thoughts From the Mount of Blessing," p. 139.

There is no other life through which Christ could now manifest Himself in us than this one life which we have; and there is no other way in which He could live His life through us except in this physical being of ours.

  1. 5.How much of the being should sanctification include? 1 Thess. 5:23.

Notes

"Sanctification is not merely a theory, an emotion, or a form of words, but a living, active principle, entering into the everyday life. It requires that our habits of eating, drinking, and dressing, be such as to secure the preservation of physical, mental, and moral health, that we may present to the Lord our bodies—not an offering corrupted by wrong habits, but—'a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God.'"—"Counsels on Health," p. 67.

"If we would be sanctified in soul, body, and spirit, we must live in conformity to the divine law. The heart cannot preserve consecration to God while the appetites and passions are indulged at the expense of health and life."—Id., p. 69.

  1. 6.How completely should we surrender our bodies to God? Rom. 12:1.

Notes

"God calls for a living sacrifice, not a dead or dying one. When we realize the requirements of God, we shall see that He requires us to be temperate in all things. The end of our creation is to glorify God in our bodies and spirits which are His. How can we do this when we indulge the appetite to the injury of the physical and moral powers? God requires that we present our bodies a living sacrifice. Then the duty is enjoined on us to preserve that body in the very best condition of health, that we may comply with His requirements."—"Testimonies," vol. 2, p. 65.

"The body is the only medium through which the mind and the soul are developed for the upbuilding of character."—"The Ministry of Healing," p. 130.

  1. 7.How literal is God's possession of the body to be? 2 Cor. 6:16.
  2. 8.In view of God's dwelling in us, what should we do? What is the relationship between cleanliness and character building? 2 Cor. 7:1.

Notes

In this verse and in 1 Corinthians 6:20 the body and spirit are jointly concerned in our religious experience. What affects one affects the other. In other words, the spiritual life does not exist independent or separate from the physical.

"Let none who profess godliness regard with indifference the health of the body, and flatter themselves that intemperance is no sin, and will not affect their spirituality. A close sympathy exists between the physical and the moral nature. The standard of virtue is elevated or degraded by the physical habits. Excessive eating of the best of food will produce a morbid condition of the moral feelings. And if the food is not the most healthful, the effects will be still more injurious."—"Counsels on Health," p. 67.

  1. 9.What conflict exists between the flesh and the spirit? Gal. 5:17.
  2. 10.With what sins is drunkenness classed? Verses 19-21.
  3. 11.What is said concerning temperance? Verses 22, 23.
  4. 12.What does the Christian do to all works of the flesh? Verse 24.
  5. 13.What warning does the apostle Peter give? 1 Peter 2:11.

Notes

"Many regard this warning as applicable only to the licentious; but it has a broader meaning. It guards against every injurious gratification of appetite or passion. It is a most forcible warning against the use of such stimulants and narcotics as tea, coffee, tobacco, alcohol, and morphine. These indulgences may well be classed among the lusts that exert a pernicious influence upon moral character. The earlier these hurtful habits are formed, the more firmly will they hold their victims in slavery to lust, and the more certainly will they lower the standard of spirituality."—Id., pp. 67, 68.

Thoughts for Meditation

  1. 1.How fully do I acknowledge God's ownership of all my being?
  2. 2.Is it not better for Him to have His own way with me than for me to have my own way?
