Lesson 8 · August 23, 1930

The Return from Exile

PDFShare

Memory Verse: Jer. 29:14.

Lesson Help: "Prophets and Kings," pp. 551-581, 607-617.

Introduction

Only a comparatively small number of those who had been carried captive into Babylon were prepared to accept the call to return to Judea. Those who did return were men and women who had kept in touch with the divine will and purpose, and were prepared to meet the opposition and endure the hardships incident to the restoration of the Holy City, and the replanting of the wasted fields and vineyards of their homeland. The struggles incident to the rebuilding of the temple were but a prelude to more intense conflict in the rebuilding of the city walls, and the work of restoration covered many years of conflict, with discouragement and apathy on the part of the returned exiles themselves, and opposition, misrepresentation, and treachery from their neighbors. But eventually under Ezra and Nehemiah the work was completed and the land restored.

The Lesson

  1. 1.What prophecy was fulfilled when Israel and Judah were taken into captivity? Deut. 28:47-49, 64-68.

Notes

In Deuteronomy 28:47-68 God reveals the overpowering curse that would come to Israel as the result of disobedience. He pictures the attacks of the nations, the siege, the captivity, and the scattering of the majority of the tribes among earth's peoples.

  1. 2.How long was Judah to remain in captivity? Jer. 29:10.
  2. 3.Who is mentioned by name in Isaiah's prophecy as one who should help Israel out of captivity? Isa. 44:28; 45:1-3.
  3. 4.At the end of the period, whom did God stir up to carry out His purpose for His children? What was this king instructed of God to do? 2 Chron. 36:22, 23; Ezra 1:1, 2.

Notes

"Just at the time God had said He would cause His temple at Jerusalem to be rebuilt, He moved upon Cyrus as His agent to discern the prophecies concerning himself, with which Daniel was so familiar, and to grant the Jewish people their liberty. . . . His [Cyrus'] heart was profoundly moved, and he determined to fulfill his divinely appointed mission. He would let the Judean captives go free; he would help them restore the temple of Jehovah."—"Prophets and Kings," pp. 557, 558.

  1. 5.What were the men of Judah called upon to do? What instruction was given to others regarding giving assistance? Ezra 1:3, 4.

Notes

"Tidings of this decree reached the farthermost provinces of the king's realm, and everywhere among the children of the dispersion there was great rejoicing. Many, like Daniel, had been studying the prophecies, and had been seeking God for His promised intervention in behalf of Zion. And now their prayers were being answered."—Id., pp. 558, 559.

  1. 6.Who were the first to respond? What was given them? What articles did Cyrus add from the royal custody? Verses 5-11.
  2. 7.What was the number of the whole congregation that returned from captivity with the first company? Ezra 2:64-67.
  3. 8.What was one of the first objectives to which they gave time and means? Verses 68-70.

Notes

"As speedily as possible, an altar was erected on the site of the ancient altar in the temple court. . . 1. The setting up of the altar of daily burnt offerings greatly cheered the faithful remnant. Heartily they entered into the preparations necessary for the rebuilding of the temple, gathering courage as these preparations advanced from month to month. . . . With joyous anticipation they looked forward to the time when, with temple rebuilt, they might behold the shining forth of His glory from within."—Id., pp. 560, 563.

  1. 9.What celebration was held when the foundations of the temple were laid? Ezra 3:10-13.
  2. 10.Who offered to help Judah in rebuilding the temple? What reply did they receive? Ezra 4:1-3.
  3. 11.What plotting continuously went forward against Judah? Verses 4-6.
  4. 12.What complaint did their enemies make to Artaxerxes the king? What occurred as a result of these conspiracies? Verses 11-24.

Notes

"For over a year the temple was neglected, and well-nigh forsaken. The people dwelt in their homes, and strove to attain temporal prosperity; but their situation was deplorable. Work as they might, they did not prosper. The very elements of nature seemed to conspire against them. Because they had let the temple lie waste, the Lord sent upon their substance a wasting drought. God had bestowed upon them the fruits of field and garden, the corn and the wine and the oil, as a token of His favor; but because they had used these bountiful gifts so selfishly, the blessings were removed."—Id., p. 573.

  1. 13.When Zerubbabel and Joshua began to go forward with the temple building, who came and demanded their authority for the work? Ezra 5:1-5.
  2. 14.In reporting the building work to Darius, king of Persia, what statement was made? What request was also made? Verses 6-17.
  3. 15.When search for the decree of Cyrus was made, what was found? Ezra 6:1-5.
  4. 16.What command was given to Tatnai, governor beyond the river? What was he ordered to do for the Jews at Jerusalem? Verses 6-12.
  5. 17.How many decrees for the restoration of Jerusalem and the temple were made? By whom were they given? Verses 13-15.
  6. 18.When did Ezra go up to Jerusalem? Who went with him? When did he leave Babylon? In what month did he reach Jerusalem? Ezra 7:1-9.
  7. 19.What commission did he carry from King Artaxerxes? Verses 11-13, 21-26.

Notes

The decree of Artaxerxes permitted all Jews in his realm who were so minded to go to Jerusalem. It allowed taking with them silver and gold, the freewill offerings of the people. It also permitted their taking vessels for the service of the temple. Artaxerxes allotted a hundred talents of silver and a hundred measures each of wheat, wine, and oil, and salt unlimited, from the royal treasury, and gave permission for setting up magistrates and judges, with power of life and death.

  1. 20.How does Ezra express his gratitude to God for these favors? Ezra 7:27, 28.

Meditation Topics

  1. 1.God never punishes without holding before the offender hope of forgiveness and restoration if he repents and turns to Him.
  2. 2.As God remembered His promise and brought His people from captivity to the Promised Land, so He will, according to His promise, bring His flock into the heavenly Canaan.
  3. 3.No outside force or power can retard the work of God so long as He works for His people.
