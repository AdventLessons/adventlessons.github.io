Lesson 10 · September 7, 1929

Obedience Better Than Sacrifice

PDFShare

Lesson Scripture: Gen. 4:1-15.

Memory verse

Seed Thought: "True reverence is shown by obedience. God has commanded nothing that is unessential, and there is no other way of manifesting reverence so pleasing to Him as obedience to that which He has spoken."—"Education," p. 244.

The Lesson

  1. 1.Name the first brothers. What occupation did each follow? Gen. 4:1, 2.
  2. 2.How did they differ in character? 1 John 3:12.

Notes

"Abel had a spirit of loyalty to God; he saw justice and mercy in the Creator's dealings with the fallen race, and gratefully accepted the hope of redemption. But Cain cherished feelings of rebellion, and murmured against God because of the curse pronounced upon the earth and upon the human race for Adam's sin. He permitted his mind to run in the same channel that led to Satan's fall,—indulging the desire for self-exaltation, and questioning the divine justice and authority."—"Patriarchs and Prophets," p. 71.

  1. 3.From the record, what undesirable traits of character can you discern in Cain? Gen. 4:5, 7-9.
  2. 4.What did these brothers understand? Heb. 9:22, last part.

Notes

"These brothers were tested, as Adam had been tested before them, to prove whether they would believe and obey the word of God. They were acquainted with the provision made for the salvation of man, and understood the system of offerings which God had ordained. They knew that in these offerings they were to express faith in the Saviour whom the offerings typified. . . . Without the shedding of blood, there could be no remission of sin; and they were to show their faith in the blood of Christ as the promised atonement, by offering the firstlings of the flock in sacrifice."—Ibid.

  1. 5.When Cain and Abel came to make an offering to God, what did each bring? What did each offering signify concerning the one who offered it? Gen. 4:3, 4.

Notes

"Cain came before God with murmuring and infidelity in his heart in regard to the promised sacrifice and the necessity of the sacrificial offerings. His gift expressed no penitence for sin. . . . Abel grasped the great principles of redemption. He saw himself a sinner, and he saw sin, and its penalty death, standing between his soul and communion with God. He brought the slain victim, the sacrificed life, thus acknowledging the claims of the law that had been transgressed. Through the shed blood he looked to the future sacrifice, Christ dying on the cross of Calvary; and trusting in the atonement that was there to be made, he had the witness that he was righteous, and his offering accepted."—Id., p. 72.

  1. 6.How did the Lord distinguish between the offerings of Cain and Abel? Verses 4, 5.
  2. 7.What does the Lord account of greater worth than any offering? 1 Sam. 15:22.

Notes

The Lord never changes. He is "the same yesterday, and to-day, and forever." He said to Israel by His prophet, "I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings." Hosea 6:6. In our worship, are we not in danger of substituting material gifts and ceremonies for those traits of character,—obedience, mercy, and love,—that alone find acceptance with the Lord? Our knowledge of God increases as we study the word, His handiwork in the natural world, and His dealings with His people in all ages, and this knowledge results in deepening our love for Him and for His children. Abiding love for God brings willing obedience.

  1. 8.What spirit did Cain manifest when it was observed that his offering was not accepted? Gen. 4:5.

Notes

We are told of the spirit of love Abel manifested toward his brother: "Abel pleaded with his brother to approach God in the divinely prescribed way; but his entreaties only made Cain the more determined to follow his own will. As the eldest, he felt above being admonished by his brother, and despised his counsel."—Id., pp. 71, 72.

  1. 9.When Cain through his disturbed feelings stood on dangerous ground, how did the Lord manifest His love toward him? Verses 6, 7.

Notes

"Through an angel messenger the divine warning was conveyed: 'If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? And if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door.' The choice lay with Cain himself. . . . Should he persist in unbelief and transgression, he would have no ground for complaint because he was rejected by the Lord."—Id., p. 74.

  1. 10.Through continuing to cherish hatred and jealousy, what crime did Cain finally commit? Verse 8.

Notes

In the case of Cain and his parents, we can see the far-reaching results of a wrong choice. Is it not a certainty that our choices go far beyond ourselves?

  1. 11.How can Cain's feeling of hatred toward his brother, who was in the right, be explained? John 3:20; Amos 5:10.

Notes

"The murder of Abel was the first example of the enmity that God had declared would exist between the serpent and the Seed of the woman,—between Satan and his subjects and Christ and His followers."—Id., p. 77.

  1. 12.How was Cain soon called to account for his crime? By what question did Cain's insolence reveal itself? Gen. 4:9.

Notes

Does not the Lord put the question, Where is thy brother? to each of us? Are we not to look about us "diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God"? Heb. 12:15. Are we not, like the apostle Paul, debtors to all men? Rom. 1:14. This question imposes upon us a duty not only to look after the spiritual welfare of our brother, but also to minister to his temporal needs.

  1. 13.In what striking words did the Lord refer to Cain's great crime? Verse 10.
  2. 14.What penalty was then pronounced upon the guilty one? Verses 11, 12.

Notes

"God had given Cain an opportunity to confess his sin. He had had time to reflect. He knew the enormity of the deed he had done, and of the falsehood he had uttered to conceal it; but he was rebellious still, and sentence was no longer deferred. The divine voice that had been heard in entreaty and admonition pronounced the terrible words."—Ibid.

  1. 15.What was Cain's response when he learned of the punishment he was to receive? Verses 13, 14.

Notes

Cain desired God's protection even though he was unwilling to give the Lord his heart's devotion.

  1. 16.How did the Lord still manifest a tender sympathy toward the erring one? Verse 15.

Notes

Even then mercy would have been extended to Cain had he repented. Forgiveness is ever ready for the repentant sinner, as the prophet Hosea's appeal to Israel testifies: "Come," he entreated, "and let us return unto the Lord: for He hath torn, and He will heal." Hosea 6:1.
